## 1. Executive Summary & Historical Research Dossier

### Archaeological & Anthropological Grounding (1177 BCE Late Bronze Age Collapse)
Around 1177 BCE, the entire civilized Mediterranean and Near Eastern world—including the Mycenaean kingdoms of Greece, the Hittite Empire of Anatolia, the New Kingdom of Egypt, the Mitanni, and the coastal trading city-states of the Levant (such as Ugarit)—suffered sudden, simultaneous, and catastrophic collapse. Within a single generation, magnificent stone palaces were burned, monumental architecture ceased, specialized bureaucracies dissolved, and even written script (such as Mycenaean Linear B) was completely forgotten.

The definitive archaeological consensus (championed by Eric Cline, Colin Renfrew, and Robert Drews) attributes this catastrophe not to a single mysterious invader ("The Sea Peoples"), but to a **"Perfect Storm" of Systemic Cascading Collapse**:
1. **Hyper-Specialized Interdependence**: Bronze required a precise 90/10 alloy of copper (readily available on Cyprus) and tin (an extraordinarily scarce metal sourced thousands of kilometers away across precarious mountain routes in Afghanistan or Cornwall). No single kingdom controlled both. Palatial economies were hyper-optimized for long-range elite exchange.
2. **Synchronous Multi-Vector Stresses**: Paleoclimatological data (pollen cores and speleothems) reveal a centennial mega-drought across the Aegean and Levant, triggering famine. Earthquakes shattered palace masonry along fault lines.
3. **The Domino Effect of Trade Severance**: When agricultural distress hit one peripheral supplier, tin routes dried up. Without tin, armories could not forge weapons or maintain chariots. Bureaucracies could not pay grain rations to palace guards. Stateless, displaced maritime raiders ("Sea Peoples") seized undefended coastal chokepoints, cutting the remaining links.
4. **De-urbanization**: Surviving populations abandoned burning stone palaces, fleeing into rugged highlands to form small, self-sufficient, illiterate agrarian villages.

## 2. Thematic Premise & Syllogism (writing-discovery)

* **The Stasis / The Lie:** "Our imperial treaties, centralized specialization, and interconnected maritime trade leagues make civilization completely immune to localized crop failure or isolated barbarian raids."
* **The Crucible / Conflict:** Distant tin shipments abruptly cease; unprecedented multi-year drought empties the palatial grain silos; and displaced, starving coastal refugees arrive not to trade, but to sack the undefended storehouses.
* **Dramatic Syllogism:**  
  $$\text{Hyper-Specialized Imperial Complacency} + \text{Multi-Node Resource Severance} = \text{Cascading Civilizational De-Urbanization}$$
* **The Truth / Synthesis:** Extreme efficiency creates extreme fragility. Resilience requires redundant local slack and autarky; an over-optimized global network shatters catastrophically when a single critical dependency fails.

### Truby 4-Corner Opposition Web
* **Corner 1 (The Establishment / Palatial Scribes & Central Treasury):** Clings fanatically to centralized bureaucratic accounting, requisitioning bronze pots and sacred temple statues to melt into spear tips while rigidly rationing moldy barley to rebellious guards.
* **Corner 2 (The Zealot Opponent / Littoral Sea-Raider Coalition):** Displaced former mariners whose own homelands burned; they believe the corrupt palatial empires deserve total annihilation and coordinate fleet landings to burn coastal ports.
* **Corner 3 (The Thematic Shadow / Autarkic Smuggler Syndicates):** Merchants hoarding scrap bronze, flint tools, and grain in mountain redoubts. They actively accelerate the palace's collapse by running speculative black markets, aiming to buy up bankrupt royal estates.
* **Corner 4 (The Tertiary Outcast / Highland Clay-Potters & Nomads):** Mountain herders who never relied on bronze tools, Linear B script, or palace grain. They watch the burning capitals with grim satisfaction, possessing the only survival skills suited for the coming Dark Age.

## 3. Injected Tabletop Mechanics (adventure_framework_srd.md)

### 3.1 4-Level Dynamic Dial: The Systemic Collapse Cascade
* **Level 1 (Tin Scarcity & Scribe Rations / Light):**  
  * *Condition:* Tin deliveries missed. Palatial armories substitute copper or re-forge scrap.
  * *Mechanics:* Cost of manufactured weapons, armor, and metal tools doubles. DC 10 Diplomacy/Insight checks to negotiate with anxious palatial quartermasters.
* **Level 2 (Interdependent Edge Severance / Moderate):**  
  * *Condition:* One primary trade edge on the graph goes dead. Garrison wages go unpaid; border outposts report starvation.
  * *Mechanics:* Adjacent nodes suffer a -2 penalty to economic morale. Unrest checks DC 13. Travel along coastal routes requires DC 13 Survival to evade hungry deserter patrols.
* **Level 3 (Palatial Requisition & Bread Riots / Severe):**  
  * *Condition:* High scribes dispatch royal chariots to violently confiscate peasant seed grain and sacred temple idols.
  * *Mechanics:* Civil unrest breaks out across the node. DC 16 Fortitude/Athletics checks to navigate barricaded streets without taking 2d6 bludgeoning damage. Influence links degrade into hazardous raider corridors.
* **Level 4 (Catastrophic De-Urbanization & Palatial Inferno / Lethal Climax):**  
  * *Condition:* Scribes flee, palace archives burn, and central order dissolves completely into stateless anarchy.
  * *Mechanics:* The node loses its Sovereign status immediately. Massive fire and mob violence inflict DC 20 Reflex checks (or 4d10 lethal fire/trampling damage) per turn. All downstream supply lines permanently collapse into standalone fallback mode.

### 3.2 Threat Clocks
* **Regional Supply Cascade [8 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks each time a connected node's trade or resource link is severed, or when a grain harvest fails.
  * *Consequence when full:* Triggers Dial Level 4 immediately; palace burns and network enters a regional Dark Age.
* **Sea-Raider Littoral Incursion [4 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks each turn the coastal trade route remains unpatrolled or harbor defense taxes go unpaid.
  * *Consequence when full:* Sea-raider fleet lands in force; sacks the harbor terminal and burns coastal granaries.

## 5. Scene Calculus & Encounter Sparks (writing-generation)

* **Encounter 1: The Melt-Down Requisition (Arrival Collision):**
  * *Collision ($><$):* Palatial bronze-bailiffs demand the party surrender their foreign bronze weapons and armor for "the Royal Emergency Furnace," while temple priests physically barricade the sacred bronze bells.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* The party enters expecting imperial hospitality but is instantly classified as hoarding contraband metal.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The bailiff speaks with imperious legal authority, but his hands tremble and his linen tunic is stained with soot from fleeing burning outlying farms.
* **Encounter 2: The Scribe's Clay Tablet Bonfire:**
  * *Dilemma:* A dying chief archivist begs the party to smuggle out the palace's tax and trade records before the mob burns the archives. The mob offers the party a chest of un-smelted copper ingots to help them break down the archive doors and burn the debt ledgers.
