## 1. Executive Summary & Historical Research Dossier

### Archaeological Grounding (The American Bottom Metropolis of Cahokia)
Located in the rich alluvial floodplain of the Mississippi River opposite modern St. Louis, Missouri, Cahokia was the undisputed apex metropolis of pre-Columbian North America north of Mexico. Flourishing between 1050 and 1250 CE, the urban complex covered more than 16 square kilometers and supported an estimated population of 20,000 to 40,000 citizens—surpassing contemporary London or Paris.

Key archaeological and environmental findings (synthesized by Timothy Pauketat, Thomas Emerson, and Samuel Munoz):
1. **Monks Mound & The Monumental Earthworks**: The city was centered upon **Monks Mound**, a colossal four-tiered earthen pyramid rising 30 meters (100 feet) into the sky, covering 14 acres (larger footprint than the Great Pyramid of Giza), and containing more than **22 million cubic feet (600,000 cubic meters)** of earth. Every basket of clay and silt was hand-carried on human backs using woven reed baskets. Atop Monks Mound sat a massive 5,000-square-foot theocratic temple where the "Great Sun" chief conducted solar rituals.
2. **The Theocratic Maize Machine & Woodhenge**: Cahokia’s explosive rise was propelled by high-yield flint maize monoculture, shell-tempered pottery technology, and astronomical solar alignment circles known as **Woodhenge**—massive timber circles of red cedar posts used to mark solstices, equinoxes, and planting calendars, cementing the priests’ claims of cosmic control over the seasons.
3. **Severe Hierarchy and Human Sacrifice (Mound 72)**: Excavations at Mound 72 revealed the brutal reality of Mississippian theocracy. The central "Beaded Burial" featured an elite male laid atop an effigy cape made of **20,000 marine shell beads** imported from the Gulf of Mexico. Surrounding him were mass sacrificial graves containing **more than 250 sacrificial victims**, including a pit of 53 strangled young women, four decapitated and handless young men, and 39 executed retainers—dramatizing the absolute life-and-death power of the ruling sun caste.
4. **Deforestation and the 20,000-Log Palisade**: To protect the sacred central plaza and elite mounds from rising regional unrest, Cahokia constructed a monumental **2-mile-long defensive palisade** equipped with bastions and shooting platforms. Archaeological trenching shows this wall was rebuilt at least three times, with each iteration consuming over **20,000 mature oak, hickory, and red cedar logs**. Clear-cutting the upland bluffs for palisade logs, houses, and firewood stripped the watershed of root cover, dramatically accelerating soil erosion and runoff into Cahokia Creek.
5. **The Catastrophic Mega-Flood Horizon (circa 1200 CE)**: High-resolution sediment cores from Horseshoe Lake (Samuel Munoz et al., *PNAS* 2015) identified an immense, catastrophic Mississippi River **mega-flood around 1200 CE**. Floodwaters surged over 10 meters above baseline, breaching natural levees and inundating the entire American Bottom under brown muddy torrents. The flood drowned thousands of acres of unharvested maize, silted canals, and destroyed residential neighborhoods. Because the Great Sun had claimed absolute cosmic power over water and sky, the mega-flood shattered the theocracy's spiritual legitimacy. Massive civil unrest erupted; the defensive palisade was burned in repeated attacks; and by 1350 CE, the great metropolis was completely abandoned to the wetlands.

## 2. Thematic Premise & Syllogism (writing-discovery)

* **The Stasis / The Lie:** "The Great Sun Chief commands the sky and the river; the Mississippi swells only to nourish our holy maize, and our towering earthen mounds and wooden bastions make Cahokia an eternal, untouchable holy city."
* **The Crucible / Conflict:** A historic 10-meter Mississippi mega-flood breaches the natural river levees, turning the American Bottom into an ocean of brown mud that drowns the harvest; as starvation grips the city, commoners burn the palisade bastions and demand the execution of the priests whose cosmic prayers failed.
* **Dramatic Syllogism:**  
  $$\text{Theocratic Hegemony on Vulnerable Floodplain} + \text{Alluvial Mega-Flood Ecological Shock} = \text{Palisade Conflagration \& Total Metropolis Desertion}$$
* **The Truth / Synthesis:** Rulers who base their legitimacy on controlling the gods of nature are doomed the moment nature turns violent. When the waters rise higher than the pyramid, the temple becomes the altar where the rulers are sacrificed.

### Truby 4-Corner Opposition Web
* **Corner 1 (The Establishment / The Great Sun Chief atop Monks Mound):** Claims direct descent from the solar deity; demands forced maize tribute and human retainer sacrifices even as muddy floodwaters lap the lower terrace of his pyramid.
* **Corner 2 (The Zealot Opponent / Palisade Mutineer War Chief):** Elite palisade guardian who turns against the theocracy; leads starving commoners with firebrands to breach the royal granaries and hunt down the Sun priests.
* **Corner 3 (The Thematic Shadow / Marine-Shell Bead Oligarchs):** Merchant-nobles who hoard surplus dried maize in water-sealed pottery jars, trading grain for shell beads and forcing starving families into perpetual debt-bondage.
* **Corner 4 (The Tertiary Outcast / Woodhenge Cedar-Astronomer Hermit):** Outcast calendar-keeper who read celestial planetary alignments and warned the council of the impending flood; now uses his cedar astronomical posts to build emergency evacuation rafts for flood refugees.

## 3. Injected Tabletop Mechanics (adventure_framework_srd.md)

### 3.1 4-Level Dynamic Dial: River Inundation & Theocratic Collapse
* **Level 1 (Creek Backwater Swell & Palisade Rot / Light):**  
  * *Condition:* Spring rains saturate the floodplain; Cahokia Creek runs swollen; oak palisade timbers rot at ground level.
  * *Mechanics:* Difficult footing in muddy plazas (DC 10 Acrobatics to sprint). Woodhenge posts slick with moss; DC 10 Nature or Survival to predict storm crests.
* **Level 2 (Levee Slumping & Silt-Drowned Maize Fields / Moderate):**  
  * *Condition:* Mississippi flood crest rises; natural river levees begin to slough; miles of low-lying maize crops drown in stagnant water; food rations cut.
  * *Mechanics:* Navigating flooded outskirts requires DC 13 Athletics or Vehicles (Water). Purchasing food costs triple; civil unrest checks at DC 13 across all residential districts.
* **Level 3 (Mega-Flood Breach & Palisade Bastion Inferno / Severe):**  
  * *Condition:* A 10-meter flood surge ruptures the riverbanks, drowning the lower city; rebel commoners set fire to the 2-mile oak palisade wall.
  * *Mechanics:* Swift, muddy flood torrents sweep through streets (DC 16 Fortitude or be swept away, taking 4d8 bludgeoning/drowning damage). Blazing timber palisade towers collapse (DC 16 Reflex save or take 3d10 fire/crushing damage).
* **Level 4 (Monks Mound Slump & Complete Urban Abandonment / Lethal Climax):**  
  * *Condition:* Saturated clay causes the southern terrace of Monks Mound to suffer a massive rotational slope failure; the Great Sun temple collapses; the city completely fractures into warring bands fleeing the drowned valley.
  * *Mechanics:* Absolute civil and environmental apocalypse. Surviving on the collapsing mound requires DC 20 Athletics or Survival checks to avoid being buried under 1,000 tons of wet clay. Total systemic dissolution: the city ceases to function, trade networks dissolve, and the node permanently converts into an uninhabited wetland ruin.

### 3.2 Threat Clocks
* **Mississippi Alluvial Mega-Flood Meter [6 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks each turn torrential rains continue or upstream snowmelt swells the river.
  * *Consequence when full:* Levees fail catastrophically; 10-meter flood surge inundates the American Bottom; triggers Dial Level 3.
* **Palisade Conflagration & Mutiny Clock [4 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks when sun priests demand human sacrifices or royal guards deny food to starving refugees.
  * *Consequence when full:* Palisade is set ablaze; rebels storm the summit of Monks Mound.

## 5. Scene Calculus & Tactical Tabletop Sparks (writing-generation)

* **Scene 1: The Plaza Conscription at Monks Mound (Arrival Collision):**
  * *Collision ($><$):* The party arrives at the Grand Plaza seeking provisions or transit, but royal solar priests with copper celts and feather standards seize the party's retainers, intending to march them up Monks Mound as "voluntary sacrifices to appease the rising waters," while guards point flint-tipped lances at the party’s chest.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* The party enters what appeared to be a civilized imperial capital, only to find themselves classified as sacrificial cattle for an increasingly desperate theocratic death cult.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The high priest proclaims with divine authority that the Sun welcomes the foreign guests into eternal glory, while behind his back, junior acolytes hurriedly pack royal shell-bead treasuries into canoes on the rising creek.
* **Scene 2: The Sinking Grain Cache at the Broken Levee (Resource Dilemma):**
  * *Collision ($><$):* A natural levee collapses, and a torrent of brown river water threatens to submerge a sunken storage pit containing 500 bushels of dry seed maize. An elite merchant offers the party 10,000 marine shell beads to help him haul his personal grain to high ground, while 200 starving local farmers struggle with reed baskets to save the communal seed.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* If the party aids the merchant, they acquire immense currency and political influence with the palace elite, but condemn hundreds of local families to starvation; if they aid the farmers, the furious merchant alerts the palisade archers that the party is inciting a peasant mutiny.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The merchant boasts that the beads are immortal wealth that buy the favor of the gods, while a farmer's child uses a worthless shell bead to scoop mud out of her father's drowning grain sack.
* **Scene 3: The Burning of the Eastern Palisade Bastion (Sacrifice / Severance Crisis):**
  * *Collision ($><$):* Rebel warriors have ignited the 20-foot-tall oak logs of the Eastern Palisade, trapping a detachment of elite archers and forty civilian women inside a burning wooden bastion, while floodwaters rise 6 feet deep around the base of the wall.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* The party must choose between scaling the blazing timber scaffolding to lower the trapped civilians using ropes into the swirling water, or fighting off the rebel skirmishers who are attempting to spear the survivors as they jump.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The rebel war-leader screams that the wall must burn because it was built from the bones and sweat of their children, refusing to believe that any innocent people remain inside the fortress of the Sun.
