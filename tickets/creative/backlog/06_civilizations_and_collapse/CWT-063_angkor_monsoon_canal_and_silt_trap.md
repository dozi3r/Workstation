## 1. Executive Summary & Historical Research Dossier

### Archaeological & Engineering Grounding (The Hydraulic City of Greater Angkor)
The Khmer Empire of Angkor (modern Cambodia, 9th–15th century CE) created the largest low-density urban settlement complex in the pre-industrial world, covering nearly 1,000 square kilometers. At the core of this imperial civilization was an unprecedented hydraulic apparatus designed to harness, store, and redistribute water across a vast, flat floodplain.

Key archaeological and environmental findings (synthesized by Roland Fletcher, Damian Evans, and Christophe Pottier via high-resolution airborne LiDAR and sediment cores):
1. **The Mega-Baray Engineering Infrastructure**: The Khmer constructed immense artificial reservoirs known as *Barays*. The West Baray, measuring 8 kilometers long by 2.1 kilometers wide, held over **53 million cubic meters** of water within elevated earthen dikes, while the East Baray and Indratataka held tens of millions more. These were fed by monumental diversion dams constructed across the Puok, Siem Reap, and Roluos rivers descending from the holy Kulen hills.
2. **The Triple-to-Quadruple Rice Harvest Machine**: This continuous gravity-fed network of feeder canals, distributing flumes, and compartmentalized silt-traps transformed the natural flood-pulse into a hyper-productive agrarian engine, producing three to four rice harvests annually. This unprecedented caloric surplus supported an urban population exceeding 750,000 people and financed the monumental sandstone temples of Angkor Wat and Angkor Thom.
3. **The Climate Whiplash & Geomorphic Incision Trap**: Dendroclimatological data (tree-ring chronologies from *Fokienia hodginsii* pines) reveal that in the mid-14th to early-15th centuries, the region suffered severe climatic instability: decades of debilitating mega-droughts followed immediately by unprecedented, violent mega-monsoons. Decades of low water had caused the canal grid to accumulate thick plugs of laterite clay silt, while deforestation on the Kulen hills stripped topsoil.
4. **Structural Failure of the Hydraulic Mega-Machine**: When the mega-monsoons struck, runoff carrying massive boulders, fallen timber, and thousands of tons of sediment ripped through the un-dredged channels. The immense hydraulic head smashed the heavy masonry spillways (such as the masonry bridge-dam at Spean Thma). The raging waters did not merely overflow; they violently **incised the riverbed**, gouging channels 5 to 8 meters deep into the soft alluvium. Because the riverbeds dropped far below the intake thresholds of the primary distribution canals, the canals could no longer receive water. The entire irrigation grid became physically non-functional. Angkor collapsed from infrastructural sclerosis, triggering royal abandonment in 1431 CE.

## 2. Thematic Premise & Syllogism (writing-discovery)

* **The Stasis / The Lie:** "The God-Kings command the celestial waters; our Barays mirror the cosmic oceans of Indra, and the geometric canal grid will nourish four harvests a year forever without failure."
* **The Crucible / Conflict:** After two punishing years of dry drought that leave the West Baray a cracked mud basin, a violent mega-monsoon deluges the deforested Kulen highlands, sending a roaring wall of red mud, floating trees, and laterite silt crashing into the silted feeder canals.
* **Dramatic Syllogism:**  
  $$\text{Rigid Hydraulic Hyper-Optimization} + \text{Climate Whiplash Mega-Monsoon} = \text{Catastrophic Bed-Incision \& Total Agricultural Grid Death}$$
* **The Truth / Synthesis:** An artificial infrastructure that is hyper-specialized for predictable environmental cycles becomes fatally brittle when the climate swings to extremes. When an engineering network grows too vast for its human maintenance capacity, its own scale accelerates its collapse.

### Truby 4-Corner Opposition Web
* **Corner 1 (The Establishment / Royal High Hydraulic Scribe):** Demands unswerving adherence to the sacred geometric grid of the God-King; deploys whip-wielding corvée overseers to force 20,000 starving peasants into waist-deep sludge with wooden baskets instead of releasing emergency overflow into royal lotus parks.
* **Corner 2 (The Zealot Opponent / Upstream Kulen Forest Clans):** Upland hill-dwellers who clear-cut the watershed forests for swidden agriculture; refusing to serve in lowland corvée gangs, they deliberately breach diversion dams that flood their highland valleys.
* **Corner 3 (The Thematic Shadow / Canal Grain-Corvée Syndicates):** Corrupt warehouse masters who withhold emergency rice reserves, using private water-gates to irrigate their lucrative betel nut plantations while deliberately starving out royal tenant farmers to acquire their ancestral paddies.
* **Corner 4 (The Tertiary Outcast / Tonlé Sap Floating-Village Fisherfolk):** Bamboo-raft and stilt-village foragers on the Great Lake who reject canal irrigation entirely, living in harmony with the natural lake pulse; they view the destruction of the dikes as a sacred re-wilding that feeds the giant catfish runs.

## 3. Injected Tabletop Mechanics (adventure_framework_srd.md)

### 3.1 4-Level Dynamic Dial: Hydraulic Grid Overload & Silt Incision
* **Level 1 (Laterite Silt Accumulation & Low Head / Light):**  
  * *Condition:* Canals run shallow; silt bars accumulate at junctions; transport skiffs scrape muddy bottoms.
  * *Mechanics:* Flat-bottom boat transit requires DC 10 Athletics/Vehicles checks to avoid grounding. Minor water rationing reduces crop hydration; field unrest checks at DC 10.
* **Level 2 (Monsoon Flash Flood & Sluice Vibration / Moderate):**  
  * *Condition:* Torrential rains saturate embankments; laterite slurry flows through secondary channels; sluice winches strain under hydraulic head.
  * *Mechanics:* Moving along muddy earthen dikes requires DC 13 Balance checks; falling off inflicts 2d6 bludgeoning damage into rushing waters. Operating heavy wooden sluice gates requires DC 13 Athletics or Engineering checks.
* **Level 3 (Sluice Shear & Riverbed Incision Breach / Severe):**  
  * *Condition:* Masonry spillways shatter under water hammer pressure; raging flood gouges the riverbed 5 meters deeper, stranding irrigation intakes high and dry; adjacent villages flood.
  * *Mechanics:* Swift water torrents sweep across the landscape (DC 16 Fortitude or be swept away, taking 4d8 bludgeoning water damage). Irrigation to surrounding paddies ceases immediately.
* **Level 4 (Catastrophic Baray Embankment Rupture / Lethal Climax):**  
  * *Condition:* The eastern earthen retaining wall of the West Baray ruptures; tens of millions of cubic meters of water roar through the urban center, washing away wooden houses, silting temples, and turning paddies into a stagnant malarial lake.
  * *Mechanics:* Catastrophic deluge. Characters caught in the flood path must pass DC 20 Athletics (Swimming) or Survival saves or suffer 8d10 lethal bludgeoning/drowning damage. The node permanently loses its agricultural capacity; population flees into the jungle.

### 3.2 Threat Clocks
* **Laterite Silt Choke Meter [6 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks each turn corvée dredging is interrupted, or when deforested upland runoff dumps silt into canals.
  * *Consequence when full:* Silt traps plug solid; canals overflow into living quarters.
* **Earthen Baray Hydrostatic Breach Clock [4 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks when mega-monsoon rainfall continues without emergency sluice pressure-release.
  * *Consequence when full:* Triggers Dial Level 4 catastrophic earthen dam collapse.

## 5. Scene Calculus & Encounter Sparks (writing-generation)

* **Encounter 1: The Sluice Winch Mutiny (Arrival Collision):**
  * *Collision ($><$):* The party arrives at the central canal hub needing boat transit or supplies, but the Royal Hydraulic Scribe commands his armored guards to chain the party’s pack animals to the massive emergency sluice-winch to hold back a bulging water surge; simultaneously, armed local farmers attack the guards with rice-hooks to smash the chains and flood the royal pleasure gardens rather than drown their own villages.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* The party enters seeking simple river transport but is immediately caught in a violent labor insurrection between imperial engineers and drowning farmers.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The scribe insists he is preserving the divine cosmic harmony of the God-King, but his manic shouting reveals he knows the masonry beneath his feet is already cracking.
* **Encounter 2: The Silt Plug in the Grand Feeder:**
  * *Dilemma:* A 200-ton logjam and laterite silt dam has plugged the primary intake beneath a sacred stone bridge. If the plug is blown with alchemical fire-pots, the sudden hydraulic surge will wipe out a downstream refugee encampment of 500 corvée families. If the plug is left alone, the river will permanently incise into a new bed, leaving the entire city’s rice terraces dry and condemning 50,000 citizens to starvation within weeks.
