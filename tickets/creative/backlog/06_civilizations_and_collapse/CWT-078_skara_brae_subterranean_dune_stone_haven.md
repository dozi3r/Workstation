## 1. Executive Summary & Historical Research Dossier

### Archaeological Grounding (Neolithic Orkney & The Subterranean Midden Bio-Haven)
Perched on the windswept shore of the Bay of Skaill on Mainland Orkney in northern Scotland, Skara Brae is Europe's most complete and perfectly preserved Neolithic village. Inhabited for over six centuries (circa 3180–2500 BCE) by the Grooved Ware cultural complex, this settlement represents an extraordinary triumph of thermal bioclimatic engineering and egalitarian communal survival in a treeless sub-polar environment.

Key archaeological realities established by modern excavations (V. Gordon Childe, David Clarke, and Colin Renfrew):
1. **The Midden-Insulated Subterranean Bio-Architecture**: Because the Orkney archipelago was devoid of tall timber trees due to relentless Atlantic gales and salt spray, the inhabitants adapted brilliantly:
   * Rather than building above ground where hurricane-force winds would shatter roofs, the eight stone dwellings were clustered together and sunk directly into massive artificial mounds of **midden** (centuries of decomposed organic waste, cattle dung, crushed limpet shells, ash, and peat compost).
   * This thick midden envelope provided extraordinary thermal insulation, protecting interior living chambers from freezing North Atlantic blizzards and stabilizing indoor temperatures around a central peat fire year-round.
2. **The Flagstone Furniture Phenomenon**: Builders quarried the local **Old Red Sandstone**, a geological formation that naturally cleaves into uniform, flat, paper-thin flagstone slabs. Without timber to carve carpentry, the Neolithic inhabitants crafted their entire domestic furniture out of stone:
   * **Stone Dressers**: Two-tiered monumental stone shelves facing the doorway of every house, displaying status objects, carved beads, and finely incised Grooved Ware ceramic bowls.
   * **Stone Box-Beds**: Large rectangular flagstone enclosures flanking the central hearth, packed with heather, ferns, and animal furs.
   * **Clay-Sealed Limpet Tanks**: Small square flagstone boxes set flush into the floor, caulked with watertight clay to keep live limpets and shellfish fresh in seawater for fishing bait and emergency food.
3. **The Interconnected Covered Crawlways**: The individual houses were connected to one another by low, narrow, stone-roofed alleys less than 4 feet (1.2 meters) in height. Each house doorway was protected by a thick stone slab door that could be locked from the inside by sliding a wooden or whalebone bar into carved stone sockets. This allowed the entire village to socialize, share hearth embers, and move between homes during months of winter storms without ever stepping out into the lethal arctic gales.
4. **Absolute Egalitarian Clan Structure**: Every house in Skara Brae shares the exact same floor plan, identical central square stone hearth, identical right-hand larger bed and left-hand smaller bed, and identical dresser placement. Crucially, the archaeological record contains **zero palaces, zero chief's mansions, zero evidence of slavery, and zero weapons of interpersonal warfare** (no arrowheads, daggers, or fractured skulls). The community operated as an autarkic, cooperative clan collective based on mutual aid and shared marine foraging.
5. **The Carved Stone Balls (*Petrospheres*)**: Among the most famous artifacts recovered are enigmatic, intricately carved stone balls. Sculpted from hard igneous stone into spherical forms with 4, 6, or more symmetrical knobs carved with concentric spirals, these objects demonstrate remarkable mathematical geometry and were likely used as sacred clan prestige tokens, weaving weights, or celestial ritual dice.
6. **The Great Sand Inundation (~2500 BCE)**: Around 2500 BCE, regional climate cooling brought a period of catastrophic North Atlantic super-storms. A massive gale breached the coastal dune barrier, blowing millions of tons of suffocating sand across the Bay of Skaill. The villagers evacuated, leaving behind bone beads, ivory pins, and filled grain jars. The drifting dunes buried the village completely, sealing it hermetically under sand until a massive storm stripped the dunes in 1850 CE, revealing the village exactly as it was left 5,000 years ago.

## 2. Thematic Premise & Syllogism (writing-discovery)

* **The Stasis / The Lie:** "Beneath our warm midden mounds and stone flagstone roofs, the Atlantic gales cannot touch us; our hearths will burn peat and our stone dressers will display our clan pots for eternity."
* **The Crucible / Conflict:** A historic multi-day arctic hurricane breaches the coastal dune barrier, blowing millions of tons of blinding sand through the low crawlways; sand pours down the smoke-holes, threatening to suffocate the entire clan alive in their stone beds.
* **Dramatic Syllogism:**  
  $$\text{Subterranean Clannish Insulation} + \text{Catastrophic Dune Migration Hurricane} = \text{Hermetic Entombment \& Rapid Time-Capsule Exodus}$$
* **The Truth / Synthesis:** True civilizational resilience lies in knowing when to surrender to the earth. The community survived not by fighting the hurricane, but by sealing their stone doors, walking away into the hills, and leaving their homes as an immortal stone time-capsule.

### Truby 4-Corner Opposition Web
* **Corner 1 (The Establishment / Hearth-Keeper Matriarch of House 1):** Sits by the central square stone hearth; insists that wedging the slate door-plugs shut will allow the clan to ride out the storm, forbidding anyone from venturing out into the blinding white spindrift.
* **Corner 2 (The Zealot Opponent / Coastal Seal-Hunter Youth):** Experienced gale-forager who points out that sand is already choking the central smoke-holes, threatening to turn the warm village into a suffocating stone tomb; demands an immediate forced march inland into the peat bogs.
* **Corner 3 (The Thematic Shadow / Midden Bone & Ivory Hoarder):** Reclusive craftsman living in House 7; hoards dried peat briquettes, whalebone pins, and carved stone petrospheres in hidden wall niches, refusing to share fuel unless desperate families surrender their heirloom ivory pendants.
* **Corner 4 (The Tertiary Outcast / Sand-Dune Turf Cutter):** Outcast hermit living in an exposed turf hut outside the village perimeter; understands the fluid dynamics of coastal sand dunes and guides panicked villagers through the blinding whiteout toward the high moors.

## 3. Injected Tabletop Mechanics (adventure_framework_srd.md)

### 3.1 4-Level Dynamic Dial: Sub-Polar Gale & Dune Inundation
* **Level 1 (Peat Smoke Haze & Sand-Drift Draft / Light):**  
  * *Condition:* Atlantic gales howl outside; fine white sand blows under stone slab thresholds; peat smoke fills the low ceilings.
  * *Mechanics:* Crawling through the 3.5-foot stone alleys requires DC 10 Acrobatics or Athletics. Smoke irritation imposes DC 10 Constitution saves to avoid minor coughing fits.
* **Level 2 (Crawlway Sand Choke & Gale Spindrift / Moderate):**  
  * *Condition:* Migrating dunes pile up against the outer midden walls; sand pours down central hearth smoke-holes; exterior alleys become clogged with drifting sand.
  * *Mechanics:* Clearing sand-blocked stone doors requires DC 13 Athletics or Mining. Venturing outside into the 60 mph coastal gale requires a DC 13 Constitution check or characters suffer 2d6 cold damage and movement is quartered.
* **Level 3 (Smoke-Hole Suffocation & Slab Door Rupture / Severe):**  
  * *Condition:* Hurricane winds tear driftwood roof frames; sand avalanches pour directly onto sleeping box-beds; central hearths are extinguished, leaving houses in freezing, choking darkness.
  * *Mechanics:* Severe suffocation hazard. Characters inside unventilated chambers must pass DC 16 Fortitude saves every 10 minutes or take 3d8 suffocation/cold damage. Extracting pinned victims from sand-filled box-beds requires DC 16 Athletics.
* **Level 4 (Catastrophic Dune Entombment / Lethal Climax):**  
  * *Condition:* The coastal dune barrier collapses entirely; a 20-foot wall of sand engulfs the village, sealing roofs and alleys under hundreds of tons of sand; the settlement is hermetically entombed.
  * *Mechanics:* Absolute environmental burial. Any character caught inside an unsealed chamber suffers DC 20 Athletics or Survival checks to claw their way to the surface or takes 8d10 lethal crushing/suffocation damage. The village is completely buried for five millennia; all survivors scatter into the high moors.

### 3.2 Threat Clocks
* **Coastal Sand Dune Inundation Meter [6 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks each turn hurricane winds continue blowing sand across the Bay of Skaill.
  * *Consequence when full:* Entire village covered by 20 feet of sand; triggers Dial Level 4.
* **Smoke-Hole Suffocation Clock [4 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks when sand blocks the central ceiling smoke flues while peat hearths burn.
  * *Consequence when full:* Carbon monoxide and sand fill the chambers; villagers pass out from asphyxiation.

## 5. Scene Calculus & Tactical Tabletop Sparks (writing-generation)

* **Scene 1: The Jammed Slate Door in the Covered Alley (Arrival Collision):**
  * *Collision ($><$):* Fleeing an incoming sub-polar blizzard, the party crawls on hands and knees through a 3.5-foot-tall stone alley, only to find the heavy flagstone door of House 1 jammed shut by wet sand; behind the party, an avalanche of shifting dune sand roars down the tunnel entrance, threatening to trap them in a 20-foot stone crawlway with rising sand.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* The party reaches supposed underground sanctuary, only to find themselves wedged in a pitch-black, suffocating stone tube with freezing sand filling the crawlway behind them.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* From the other side of the stone slab, an elderly voice screams that the door is locked to keep the gale out, refusing to unbar the latch even as the party pounds on the stone.
* **Scene 2: The Sinking Dresser of Carved Petrospheres (Resource Dilemma):**
  * *Collision ($><$):* In House 7, the sandy floor has given way, causing a magnificent tiered stone dresser laden with incised Grooved Ware pots and sacred carved stone petrospheres to tilt dangerously over an open midden sinkhole; a hoarder artisan attempts to carry his ivory needles to safety, while a trapped young girl cries out from beneath a collapsing stone bed-slab.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* If the party uses their strength to shore up the dresser, they preserve the most intricate mathematical and religious treasures of the Neolithic world, but allow the child to be crushed by the slipping bed-slab; if they rescue the child, the dresser plunges into the dark midden pit, smashing the pottery to powder.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The hoarder screams that human flesh is born and dies in a winter, but the carved stone balls carry the memory of the stars and cannot be replaced.
* **Scene 3: The Sand Avalanche Down the Smoke-Hole (Severance Crisis):**
  * *Collision ($><$):* As the hurricane reaches its climax, the whalebone rafters supporting the turf-and-sand roof of the communal gathering hall snap; a roaring torrent of white beach sand cascades down the central smoke-hole directly onto the burning peat hearth, creating an explosive cloud of scalding steam, choking ash, and suffocating sand.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* The party must choose between risking life and limb to climb up through the raging sand spout to wedge a massive slate slab over the roof opening, or smashing through the rear flagstone wall of the house to lead twenty blinded villagers into the sub-zero blizzard on the open cliffs.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The Hearth-Keeper Matriarch clings to the stone hearth with bleeding fingers, chanting ancient sea verses into the hot sand, refusing to leave the fire she has tended for forty years.
