## 1. Executive Summary & Historical Research Dossier

### Archaeological Grounding (The Rano Raraku Quarry & The Rodent Ecocide Hypothesis)
Rapa Nui (Easter Island), located over 3,500 kilometers off the coast of Chile, represents the ultimate ecological microcosm of isolated civilizational specialization, monument mania, and rapid ecological collapse.

Key archaeological, botanical, and zooarchaeological discoveries (synthesized by Terry Hunt and Carl Lipo):
1. **The Rano Raraku Volcanic Tuff Foundry**: Nearly all of the island’s **nearly 900 colossal *Moai* statues** were quarried from the consolidated yellow-brown volcanic ash (tuff) inside the crater and slopes of Rano Raraku. Using heavy, hand-held basalt chisels (*toki*), specialized clans carved monolithic figures averaging 4 meters tall and weighing 14 metric tons directly from the rock face, with the largest unfinished colossus (*Te Tokanga*) measuring 21.6 meters and weighing roughly 160 metric tons. Statues were lowered down the caldera slopes on ropes, erected in pits for final detailing, and transported across miles of prepared roads to coastal ceremonial platforms (*ahu*).
2. **The Transport Engine and Material Demands**: Whether moved via the "walking" hypothesis (rocking upright using tri-directional ropes, as demonstrated by Lipo & Hunt) or rolled on wooden sledges over palm trunks, the physical requirements were staggering. Hauling a single 15-ton statue required tens of thousands of feet of thick rope woven from tree bark/fibers, dozens of straight timber poles, and hundreds of calories of food per day to feed the pulling crews.
3. **The True Ecological Culprit: *Rattus exulans* Seed Predation**: For decades, popular history (e.g., Jared Diamond) promoted a simplistic moral fable of "human ecocide," claiming reckless islanders cut down every tree solely to move stone statues. Modern paleoethnobotany has overturned this narrative: the primary driver of catastrophic deforestation was the **Polynesian rat (*Rattus exulans*)**, introduced by early Polynesian voyagers. Lacking natural predators, the rat population exploded into the millions. Microscopic analysis of fossilized seeds from the extinct giant Easter Island palm (*Paschalococos disperta*) reveals that **over 99% of all preserved palm nuts exhibit gnaw marks from rat teeth**. The rats consumed virtually every seed before it could germinate, preventing forest regeneration. Human tree-felling for gardens, firewood, and hauling gear dealt the final blow to an already doomed ecosystem.
4. **The Loss of the Sea**: The extinction of the giant palm tree triggered an invisible, catastrophic domino effect: **without massive palm trunks, the islanders could no longer build large, ocean-going outrigger canoes**. Zooarchaeological analysis of early garbage middens shows that deep-sea porpoises and open-ocean tuna accounted for over 30% of the islanders' diet. Within a few generations, porpoise bones vanished from the archaeological record. The islanders were trapped on a deforested rock, forced to subsist on terrestrial sweet potatoes, chickens, and rats.
5. **The Lithic Mulching Response and the *Huri Moai* Fracture**: Faced with depleted, windswept soils, the islanders engineered an extraordinary agronomic survival technology: **lithic mulching**. They broke up millions of basalt stones and blanketed hundreds of acres of gardens with rock fragments to retain soil moisture, buffer diurnal temperature shifts, and leach vital minerals into the earth. Yet as food insecurity worsened, competitive status display turned violent: clans abandoned the quarry, armed themselves with flaked obsidian spearpoints (*mata'a*), toppled rival statues face-down on the earth (*Huri Moai*), and replaced ancestral ancestor worship with the militaristic *Tangata Manu* (Birdman) cult.

## 2. Thematic Premise & Syllogism (writing-discovery)

* **The Stasis / The Lie:** "Our ancestors walk through the stone faces of the Moai; as long as we chisel the volcanic stone larger and erect them upon the coastal platforms, the divine *mana* will protect our palm groves and feed our children forever."
* **The Crucible / Conflict:** The last grove of giant palms fails to produce a single sapling as millions of hungry rats devour every dropped nut; unable to build canoes to hunt the deep-sea porpoises, a rival clan topples their neighbor’s 30-ton Moai onto the rocks and marches on the volcanic crater armed with obsidian spearpoints.
* **Dramatic Syllogism:**  
  $$\text{Competitive Ancestral Monumentalism} + \text{Invasive Rodent Ecosystem Devastation} = \text{Total Deforestation \& Violent Statuary Iconoclasm}$$
* **The Truth / Synthesis:** Spiritual pride and monumental grandeur cannot substitute for ecological balance. When an isolated world exhausts its living timber and food web, the gods of stone are toppled by their own starving worshipers.

### Truby 4-Corner Opposition Web
* **Corner 1 (The Establishment / Master *Toki* Sculptor of Rano Raraku):** Commands hundreds of clan laborers in the quarry crater; insists that carving an even larger 20-meter colossus will appease the spirits and bring back the trees, requisitioning the island’s last palm-fiber ropes.
* **Corner 2 (The Zealot Opponent / Coastal *Mata'a* War Leader):** Outlaw clan chieftain who arms youths with flaked obsidian spearpoints; views the Moai as parasitic stones that starved the island, leading raiding parties to topple rival *ahu* statues and loot chicken coops.
* **Corner 3 (The Thematic Shadow / Lithic Mulch Basalt-Cartel):** Wealthy garden elders who monopolize volcanic rock piles and rat-snare traps, selling stone mulch and roasted rat meat at extortionate rates to starving families in exchange for ancestral heirloom jade and obsidian blades.
* **Corner 4 (The Tertiary Outcast / The Last Palm Seed Keeper):** An elderly botanist-hermit living in a subterranean lava tube who guards the last three living, caged Easter Island palm saplings, fighting off rats day and night and begging the factions to stop carving stone and plant trees.

## 3. Injected Tabletop Mechanics (adventure_framework_srd.md)

### 3.1 4-Level Dynamic Dial: Ecological Deforestation & Iconoclastic Warfare
* **Level 1 (Rat Gnawing & Cordage Fraying / Light):**  
  * *Condition:* Hundreds of small Polynesian rats swarm the grass and brush; hauling ropes fray from gnawing; palm timber runs scarce.
  * *Mechanics:* Camp supplies must be secured in stone bins (DC 10 Survival or lose 25% of edible rations each night). Hauling rope checks require DC 10 Crafting to spot rodent damage before snapping.
* **Level 2 (Moai Walking Instability & Rat Swarm Raids / Moderate):**  
  * *Condition:* Moving a 14-ton Moai along unpaved dirt roads; timber rollers crack; rat swarms boldly raid garden patches in broad daylight.
  * *Mechanics:* "Walking" a Moai requires coordinated DC 13 Athletics or Engineering checks; failure causes the statue to tip at a dangerous angle. Rat swarms create DC 13 distraction hazards; foraging for timber requires DC 13 Survival.
* **Level 3 (Quarry Rockslide & Ahu Platform Desecration / Severe):**  
  * *Condition:* Quarry retaining terraces give way in Rano Raraku; rival warbands armed with obsidian *mata'a* topple coastal statues, shattering them on stone pavements.
  * *Mechanics:* Climbing the unstable quarry scree requires DC 16 Athletics (failure triggers a 30-foot slide of volcanic tuff dealing 4d8 bludgeoning damage). Obsidian skirmishers attack from ambush (DC 16 Reflex save or take 3d8 piercing damage from bleeding obsidian wounds).
* **Level 4 (Complete Forest Extinction & Total Statue Carnage / Lethal Climax):**  
  * *Condition:* The last palm trees are gone; ocean canoe building is permanently extinct; all coastal Moai have been violently toppled; starving clans engage in desperate scorched-earth warfare.
  * *Mechanics:* Absolute insular ecological collapse. Characters cannot leave the island without external ships. Food supplies drop to zero; hunting for rats or edible roots requires DC 20 Survival checks. Collapsing megaliths and raging clan riots inflict 8d10 lethal bludgeoning/crushing damage to anyone caught near contested *ahu* platforms.

### 3.2 Threat Clocks
* **Rapa Nui Palm Seed Extinction Clock [6 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks each time rat populations swarm seed caches or the remaining palm trees are felled for hauling levers.
  * *Consequence when full:* Giant palm trees go completely extinct; canoe manufacture hits 0 permanently.
* **Huri Moai Statue-Toppling Meter [4 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks when a clan suffers food riots or an obsidian warband breaches an *ahu* perimeter.
  * *Consequence when full:* All statues across the island are toppled face-down; ancestral worship collapses into the Birdman cult.

## 5. Scene Calculus & Tactical Tabletop Sparks (writing-generation)

* **Scene 1: The Moai "Walking" Cable Snap (Arrival Collision):**
  * *Collision ($><$):* The party arrives at the base of Rano Raraku seeking shelter or volcanic stone, but is conscripted by the Master Sculptor’s guards to take up hauling cables on a 15-ton upright Moai; as the statue rocks forward on its rounded keel, rat-gnawed hemp cables snap, sending the colossus tilting directly toward a crowd of resting porters.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* The party enters expecting an awe-inspiring religious spectacle, but is instantly forced to make life-or-death physical checks to arrest the fall of a 30,000-pound stone god.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The quarry overseer chants holy praises to the ancestors while furiously kicking dead rats off the broken hauling ropes, knowing his own clan will execute him if the statue falls.
* **Scene 2: The Rat Swarm in the Seed Silo (Ecological Scarcity):**
  * *Collision ($><$):* The party and a local guide discover a hidden stone grain vault containing the last bushels of giant palm nuts and seed tubers, only to find the interior swarming with thousands of ravenous Polynesian rats chewing through wicker baskets.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* The party can use fire or alchemical means to exterminate the rats, but doing so risks igniting the dry wicker bins and incinerating the island’s final seed bank.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The local guide weeps in the dark, explaining that his ancestors brought the rats as a delicacy on their voyaging canoes, never dreaming the animals would eat their children’s future.
* **Scene 3: The Ahu Standoff at Sunset (Iconoclastic Severance Crisis):**
  * *Collision ($><$):* A warband of 50 obsidian-wielding *mata'a* raiders surrounds an ancestral coastal platform, preparing to use wooden levers to topple a 20-ton red-topknot Moai face-down into the surf, while the temple priests threaten to kill their own captive children as a final curse if the statue is touched.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* If the party defends the statue, they protect the priests and the sacred art of the island, but the starving raiders will starve and attack the party with obsidian daggers; if the party steps aside, the statue shatters on the stones, breaking the spiritual backbone of the island forever.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The war chief tells the party that the stone god has eaten all their fish and trees, shouting that a god who cannot catch a fish does not deserve to stand upon the shore.
