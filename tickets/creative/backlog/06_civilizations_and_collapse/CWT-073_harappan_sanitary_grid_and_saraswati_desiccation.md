## 1. Executive Summary & Historical Research Dossier

### Archaeological Grounding (The Indus Valley Civilization & The Drying of the Ghaggar-Hakra)
The Mature Harappan Civilization (circa 2600–1900 BCE), centered along the Indus and the now-desiccated Ghaggar-Hakra river system (identified in Vedic texts and paleohydrology as the Saraswati), was the most spatially extensive urban culture of the ancient world. Spanning over one million square kilometers across modern Pakistan and northwestern India (with anchor metropolises at Mohenjo-Daro, Harappa, Dholavira, Rakhigarhi, and Lothal), it established urban standards of civil planning, municipal hygiene, and metric precision unmatched until the modern era.

Key archaeological and paleoclimatological findings (synthesized by Jonathan Mark Kenoyer, Gregory Possehl, and Rita Wright):
1. **The World's First Municipal Sewerage System**: Unlike the chaotic organic street plans of Mesopotamia or Egypt, Harappan cities were laid out along strict cardinal grid systems. Every private home, constructed around an interior courtyard, featured a dedicated terracotta bathing platform and a private latrine. Wastewater emptied through tailored earthenware pipes pierced through exterior walls into covered brick channels running down street centers. These covered drains featured removable limestone access slabs for maintenance, settling sumps (*inspection traps*) to catch heavy solids, and arched overflow sluices discharging wastewater safely outside the defensive settlement perimeter.
2. **Standardized Metrology & The 1:2:4 Brick Ratio**: Harappan civilization demonstrated an unprecedented degree of standardization across thousands of kilometers. All fired bricks adhered rigorously to the **1:2:4 dimensional ratio** (thickness to width to length, typically 7 × 14 × 28 cm), enabling universal bond-laying without mortar trimming. Commerce was regulated by polished cubical chert weights following a combined binary (1, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64) and decimal system accurate to fractions of a gram, ensuring trust across maritime links extending to Dilmun (Bahrain) and Ur in Mesopotamia.
3. **The Great Bath and Bitumen Waterproofing**: At Mohenjo-Daro, the monumental public citadel was anchored by the **Great Bath** (12 × 7 meters, descending 2.4 meters deep via dual brick staircases). The pool was waterproofed using finely fitted baked bricks laid on edge, bonded with gypsum plaster, and sealed behind a **3-centimeter-thick continuous layer of natural bitumen (asphalt)** between inner and outer brick courses—humanity’s earliest monumental public hydraulic civil engineering installation, utilized for civic ritual purification.
4. **The Enigma of Anarchic Peace**: Strikingly, excavations across hundreds of Harappan sites have yielded no palaces, no royal tombs, no depictions of monarchical warfare, and no standing military barracks. The society operated under a collective municipal ethos governed by merchant guilds, sanitary magistrates, and civic consensus rather than divine despots.
5. **The Saraswati River Avulsion & Desiccation (~1900 BCE)**: Geological and satellite paleochannel mapping confirms that the Ghaggar-Hakra was once a mighty perennial river fed by glacial Himalayan runoff. Tectonic shifts and seismic uplifts in the Siwalik hills diverted its primary glacial feeders: the Sutlej River was captured westward into the Indus, while the Yamuna migrated eastward into the Ganges. Simultaneously, the 4.2 ka BP aridification event weakened the Indian Summer Monsoon. The Ghaggar-Hakra dried into an ephemeral, seasonal stream. Without perennial flushing, the intricate brick drains choked on un-drained human waste, well tables plummeted, epidemics broke out, and the planned cities were peacefully abandoned as populations migrated eastward to form decentralized farming hamlets along the Ganges.

## 2. Thematic Premise & Syllogism (writing-discovery)

* **The Stasis / The Lie:** "Our standardized brick ratios and covered drains bring absolute order and purity; no king rules us with an iron sword, and the sacred river will flush our sewers and fill our Great Bath for eternity."
* **The Crucible / Conflict:** The perennial river headwaters abruptly fail as tectonic shifts divert the glacial melt; the municipal drains choke on dried sewage, sparking typhoid epidemics, while guild merchants violently hoard the last private cylindrical brick wells in the upper citadel.
* **Dramatic Syllogism:**  
  $$\text{Obsessive Municipal Standardization} + \text{Tectonic Glacial River Avulsion} = \text{Systemic Sanitary Sclerosis \& Gradual De-Urbanization}$$
* **The Truth / Synthesis:** Perfect brick geometry and civic hygiene cannot manufacture water. When the river migrates, the most civilized and peaceful grid on earth becomes an uninhabitable brick kiln.

### Truby 4-Corner Opposition Web
* **Corner 1 (The Establishment / Chief Metrological Surveyor of the Brick Guild):** Enforces 1:2:4 brick ratios and drainage tax tokens with bureaucratic rigidity; insists the river's drying is merely a seasonal ebb and deploys municipal gangs to dig silt from bone-dry canals.
* **Corner 2 (The Zealot Opponent / Eastward Exodus Patriarch):** Elder of an agricultural clan demanding the immediate abandonment of the planned metropolis; packs bronze ploughshares and humped zebu cattle to march toward the monsoon-fed Ganges forests before waterborne plague kills the young.
* **Corner 3 (The Thematic Shadow / Deep-Well Water Oligarchs):** Wealthy lapis-lazuli and carnelian bead merchants who control the few 80-foot cylindrical brick wells in the upper citadel, demanding astronomical chert-weight payments in gold and silver for a single clay jar of potable water.
* **Corner 4 (The Tertiary Outcast / Dry Hakra Mud-Scavenger):** Outcast potter living in the cracked riverbed outside the walls; ignored by the civic council, he tracks subterranean water seepages through gravel strata and knows that the city's sewers have become ticking bio-hazard incubators.

## 3. Injected Tabletop Mechanics (adventure_framework_srd.md)

### 3.1 4-Level Dynamic Dial: River Desiccation & Sanitary Grid Failure
* **Level 1 (Drain Silt Accumulation & Low Well Head / Light):**  
  * *Condition:* River runs low; silt settles in street-level brick gutters; street cleaning crews inspect private latrine sumps.
  * *Mechanics:* Street transit smells foul. Navigating drainage grates requires DC 10 Athletics to avoid twisting an ankle on dislodged limestone slabs. Purchasing clean well-water requires DC 10 Bureaucracy/Persuasion.
* **Level 2 (Sewer Backflow & Metrological Currency Friction / Moderate):**  
  * *Condition:* Covered sewers choke on solid waste; stagnant wastewater pools in residential courtyards; merchants refuse payment unless weighed with official chert cubes.
  * *Mechanics:* Difficult terrain along sewer-backed streets. Drinking municipal water requires a DC 13 Constitution save against dysentery (disadvantage on physical checks for 24 hours). Conducting trade requires DC 13 Appraisal to avoid fraudulent weight penalties.
* **Level 3 (Typhoid Outbreak & Wellhead Blockade / Severe):**  
  * *Condition:* Stagnant sewage triggers epidemic typhoid; citadel guards barricade the Great Bath and deep wells with bronze pikes, driving away thirsty crowds.
  * *Mechanics:* Airborne and waterborne pestilence (DC 16 Fortitude save or take 3d8 poison damage and gain 1 level of Exhaustion). Clashing with desperate citizens at wellheads requires DC 16 checks to avoid riot stampedes.
* **Level 4 (Complete River Avulsion & Desiccated Brick Tomb / Lethal Climax):**  
  * *Condition:* The Ghaggar-Hakra riverbed completely dries into cracked clay dust; all wells run dry; municipal governance dissolves; citizens flee en masse, leaving the dead unburied in alleys.
  * *Mechanics:* Absolute environmental collapse. Characters caught in the city without sealed water stores suffer DC 20 Survival checks every 6 hours or take 8d10 lethal dehydration damage. The node loses its urban status and converts into an archaeological ghost settlement.

### 3.2 Threat Clocks
* **Saraswati Glacial Avulsion Countdown [6 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks each turn upstream tremors occur or satellite river channels divert water into the Yamuna basin.
  * *Consequence when full:* Perennial river flow drops to zero; triggers Dial Level 4 desiccation.
* **Municipal Sewer Sump Choke Meter [4 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks when municipal dredging crews are unpaid or latrine settling tanks go uncleaned.
  * *Consequence when full:* Sewage erupts into streets, unleashing city-wide waterborne plague.

## 5. Scene Calculus & Tactical Tabletop Sparks (writing-generation)

* **Scene 1: The Inspection Sump Dispute (Arrival Collision):**
  * *Collision ($><$):* The party enters Mohenjo-Daro through the First Gate, but is halted by Sanitary Inspectors demanding that the party’s pack animals be stripped and inspected for "foreign river lice and illegal mud-dung"; when an animal steps on a cracked limestone drain cover, sewage spews over the street, and inspectors demand an immediate fine of 16 chert weights of silver.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* The party expects to marvel at a pristine utopian civilization, but is immediately dragged into a petty bureaucratic sanitation shakedown while standing in toxic effluent.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The Chief Inspector boasts that Harappa has no filthy tyrants like Egypt, while his fingers nervously flick dried dysentery crusts from his white cotton tunic.
* **Scene 2: The Bitumen Siphon in the Great Bath (Resource Dilemma):**
  * *Collision ($><$):* In the drained Great Bath, desperate citizens have begun hacking away the 3-centimeter waterproofing bitumen layer with bronze axes to melt it down for water-jug sealant; the Bath Guardians order the party to defend the sacred pool with lethal force, while a weeping mother begs for a single chunk of tar to waterproof her sick infant’s cradle against sewage seepage.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* If the party defends the bath, they preserve the architectural masterpiece of the ancient world and gain merchant guild patronage, but watch innocent families succumb to toxic water; if they step aside, the historic bath is ruined forever.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The head guardian claims the bath’s purity maintains the cosmic monsoon, while he surreptitiously fills private copper flasks from the bath’s last draining sump.
* **Scene 3: The Broken 80-Foot Wellhead (Severance Crisis):**
  * *Collision ($><$):* At the citadel’s deepest cylindrical brick well, the hemp rope has snapped, dropping the only copper bucket 80 feet down into murky sludge. A mob of 200 thirsty citizens storms the platform, threatening to tear down the brick wall and throw the well oligarch down the shaft.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* The party must choose between rappelling down the precarious, crumbling brick cylinder to recover the bucket and clear the silt blockage, or fighting off the mob before they collapse the brick wellhead, which will bury the water table beneath 20 tons of rubble forever.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The well oligarch whispers to the party that there are only two feet of water left at the bottom, meaning whoever drinks today will find only mud tomorrow.
