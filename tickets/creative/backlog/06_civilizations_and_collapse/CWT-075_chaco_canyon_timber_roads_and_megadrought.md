## 1. Executive Summary & Historical Research Dossier

### Archaeological & Dendroclimatological Grounding (The Chaco Phenomenon & The 1130 CE Megadrought)
Flourishing between 850 and 1150 CE in the hyper-arid, treeless sandstone badlands of the San Juan Basin in northwestern New Mexico, the Chaco Phenomenon represents one of the most astonishing logistical and architectural feats in North American prehistory. Anchored by monumental Great Houses such as **Pueblo Bonito**, **Chetro Ketl**, and **Pueblo Alto**, Chaco Canyon served as the ceremonial, administrative, and astronomical heart of the Ancestral Puebloan world.

Key archaeological realities established by modern research (Julio Betancourt, Christopher Guiterman, and Stephen Lekson):
1. **The 200,000-Timber Logistics Miracle**: Chaco Canyon is a barren, windswept desert canyon that supported only scrub juniper and pinyon. Yet, to construct the multi-story masonry Great Houses (Pueblo Bonito rose 4 to 5 stories high, containing over 650 to 800 rooms and dozens of subterranean circular Great Kivas), Chacoans required massive roof beams. Dendrochronological and strontium isotope analysis confirmed that **more than 200,000 ponderosa pine, Douglas fir, and white fir timbers** were harvested from distant high-altitude mountain ranges: the Chuska Mountains and Zuni Mountains, situated **50 to 75 miles (80 to 120 km) away**. Every single multi-ton beam was felled with stone axes, stripped, seasoned, and **hand-carried on human shoulders** across scorched desert terrain without draft animals, wheels, or navigable waterways.
2. **The Engineered Chacoan Road System**: Chaco was connected to over 150 outlying Great Houses across a 40,000-square-mile territory by a network of engineered roads (such as the 35-mile-long Great North Road). These highways were cut **30 feet (9 meters) wide**, cleared down to bare sandstone bedrock, bordered by low earthen berms, and engineered with massive rock ramps, causeways, and rock-cut stairways carved directly into vertical canyon cliffs. Rather than following the path of least resistance, these roads drove obsessively straight across desert mesas, serving as sacred cosmic pathways for pilgrimage processions, ritual relay races, and massive timber-hauling brigades.
3. **Core-and-Veneer Ashlar Masonry**: Chacoan architecture achieved unmatched structural sophistication using "core-and-veneer" stonework: a rubble-and-clay mortar core faced on both sides with meticulously dressed, paper-thin tabular sandstone veneers forming decorative banded geometric patterns, bearing massive vertical loads without timber frames.
4. **Astronomical Precision & The Sun Dagger**: Atop Fajada Butte, the three massive sandstone slabs of the **Sun Dagger** channeled sunlight onto carved spiral petroglyphs with microscopic accuracy, marking summer/winter solstices, equinoxes, and tracking the 18.6-year lunar standstill cycle. Great House alignments mirrored these celestial coordinates.
5. **The 50-Year Megadrought Collapse (1130–1180 CE)**: Tree-ring reconstructions reveal that around 1130 CE, the American Southwest was struck by an unprecedented **50-year megadrought**. Flash-flood arroyo runoff ceased; the Chaco Wash incised into a deep, unreachable gully, destroying the floodwater irrigation grid; and outlying mountain clans, facing their own crop failures, refused to supply further timber or corn to the canyon priests. The theocratic pilgrimage system collapsed. Inhabitants ritually sealed their Great Kivas with masonry plugs, set fire to the wooden roofs, and systematically abandoned Chaco Canyon, migrating north to fortified cliff shelters (Mesa Verde) and south toward the Rio Grande.

## 2. Thematic Premise & Syllogism (writing-discovery)

* **The Stasis / The Lie:** "The Sun and Moon obey the stone walls of our Great Kivas; as long as our timber brigades carry 200,000 mountain pines across the burning sands, the Great House will draw down the rain and rule the four directions forever."
* **The Crucible / Conflict:** A 50-year megadrought turns the canyon wash to dust; mountain logging clans mutiny and refuse to carry more beams, blockading the Great North Road while starving canyon farmers demand the distribution of sacred seed corn stored in the sealed Great Kivas.
* **Dramatic Syllogism:**  
  $$\text{Monumental Theocratic Resource Siphon} + \text{Multi-Decadal Aridification Megadrought} = \text{Supply Line Severance \& Ritual Abandonment of the Great Houses}$$
* **The Truth / Synthesis:** Human labor cannot force fertility from an exhausted climate. When an ideological elite demands infinite sacrifices of timber and sweat from an arid landscape, the people will burn the temples and return to the rivers.

### Truby 4-Corner Opposition Web
* **Corner 1 (The Establishment / High Sun-Priest of Pueblo Bonito):** Sits in the fifth-story balcony of Pueblo Bonito; demands that mountain clans double their timber relays to roof a new astronomical tower to appease the Sun, threatening to curse any clan that stops hauling.
* **Corner 2 (The Zealot Opponent / Chuska Mountain Timber Warlord):** Leader of the mountain logging clans; arms his men with stone axes and flint daggers, cutting off the Great North Road and declaring that mountain wood will now be used only to build stockades for his own hungry people.
* **Corner 3 (The Thematic Shadow / Turquoise & Scarlet-Macaw Smuggler Factor):** Trader importing exotic Mesoamerican turquoise, copper bells, and live macaws; hoards dried corn in subterranean kiva niches, trading sacred feathers for the last potable water gourds of dying families.
* **Corner 4 (The Tertiary Outcast / Arroyo Sand-Farmer Hermit):** Outcast farmer living in a brush shelter at the mouth of the Chaco Wash; understands sand-dune subsurface moisture farming and warns that the priests' stone masonry has dammed the natural silt, choking the valley to death.

## 3. Injected Tabletop Mechanics (adventure_framework_srd.md)

### 3.1 4-Level Dynamic Dial: Desert Megadrought & Timber Siphon Strain
* **Level 1 (Sun-Baked Sand Drift & Log Hauling Strain / Light):**  
  * *Condition:* Scorching 100°F heat; drifting sands obscure the 30-foot road; timber brigades march under heavy loads.
  * *Mechanics:* Long-distance desert marching requires DC 10 Constitution saves each day or characters gain 1 level of Exhaustion. Navigating rock-cut cliff stairways requires DC 10 Athletics.
* **Level 2 (Arroyo Incision & Roadside Dehydration / Moderate):**  
  * *Condition:* The Chaco Wash cuts 15 feet deep into clay soil, drying surface canals; water springs dry to salty trickles; timber convoys suffer heat stroke.
  * *Mechanics:* Water consumption doubles. Foraging for water requires DC 13 Survival. Hauling heavy timbers along cliff ramps requires DC 13 Athletics or Engineering checks; failure causes beams to tumble, risking 2d8 bludgeoning damage.
* **Level 3 (Timber Brigade Mutiny & Cliff Stair Skirmish / Severe):**  
  * *Condition:* Chuska loggers rebel; timber relays halt; armed skirmishers ambush convoys on the Great North Road, dropping boulders from cliff tops.
  * *Mechanics:* High-ground rockfalls and arrow ambushes inflict DC 16 Reflex saves (takes 4d8 bludgeoning damage). Approaching Great Houses requires DC 16 Diplomacy or Intimidation to bypass paranoid priest guards.
* **Level 4 (50-Year Megadrought Conflagration & Ritual Sealing / Lethal Climax):**  
  * *Condition:* Flash-flood farming completely fails; the Chaco Wash is bone dry; priests set fire to the ponderosa pine roofs of Pueblo Bonito and seal kiva doorways with masonry plugs before fleeing into the desert.
  * *Mechanics:* Absolute regional desiccation. Characters caught in the canyon without vast water supplies suffer DC 20 Survival checks every 6 hours or take 8d10 lethal dehydration damage. Collapsing multi-story ashlar walls inflict 4d10 crushing damage across ruined plazas. The node permanently converts into a deserted ruin.

### 3.2 Threat Clocks
* **San Juan Basin Megadrought Horizon [6 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks each turn tree-ring drought continues without monsoon rains or arroyo beds deepen.
  * *Consequence when full:* Canyon water table hits zero; all Great Houses abandoned; triggers Dial Level 4.
* **Great North Road Timber Brigade Strike [4 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks when timber haulers die of heat stroke or mountain food rations are cut by canyon priests.
  * *Consequence when full:* Loggers revolt; Great North Road blockaded; construction halts permanently.

## 5. Scene Calculus & Tactical Tabletop Sparks (writing-generation)

* **Scene 1: The Dropped Pine on the Cliff Stair (Arrival Collision):**
  * *Collision ($><$):* The party climbs the 30-foot-wide rock-cut stairway ascending the canyon rim, but collides with a 40-man brigade struggling to haul a 2-ton, 30-foot ponderosa pine log; when a hauler collapses from heat exhaustion, the massive beam slips, hurtling down the narrow stone stairs directly toward the party and a pack train of water jars.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* The party approaches an imperial road expecting easy transit, but is forced into split-second life-or-death athletic checks on a sheer vertical cliff face.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The brigade taskmaster whips the fallen hauler while screaming that the Sun Dagger will blind anyone who delays the beam, but his own waterskin sloshes with empty air.
* **Scene 2: The Sun Dagger Solstice Standoff (Resource Dilemma):**
  * *Collision ($><$):* Atop Fajada Butte, the party seeks an audience with the astronomical calendar-keeper to consult an ancient star map, but discovers the priest has barricaded the cleft of the Sun Dagger, threatening to smash the delicate spiral petroglyphs with a sledgehammer unless the canyon council releases five sacks of seed corn to save his starving acolytes.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* If the party forces the barricade, they preserve the irreplaceable astronomical computer of the ancestral world, but drive the acolytes to starvation; if they negotiate grain release, the canyon council condemns the party as heretic conspirators.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The astronomer points to the dagger of light slowly slicing across the stone spiral, whispering that the heavens have not moved an inch, but the hearts of men have withered into stone.
* **Scene 3: The Burning of the Great Kiva Roof (Severance Crisis):**
  * *Collision ($><$):* In the central plaza of Pueblo Bonito, desperate priests have poured pinon pitch over the timber roof of the Great Kiva, preparing to incinerate the sacred sanctuary and seal the stone hatch with mortar to prevent "unclean refugees" from sheltering inside during a massive dust storm.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* The party must choose between fighting through the suicidal priest guards to rescue a cache of 1,000-year-old painted cylindrical pottery jars and turquoise mosaics from the burning kiva, or using their turn to escort forty choking refugee children out of the flaming plaza before the multi-story masonry wall topples.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The high priest screams into the roaring flames that the gods have abandoned Chaco, and that it is better for the cedar beams to burn in sacred fire than be chopped into firewood by common beggars.
