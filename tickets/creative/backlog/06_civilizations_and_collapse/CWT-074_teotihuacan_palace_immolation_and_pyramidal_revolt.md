## 1. Executive Summary & Historical Research Dossier

### Archaeological Grounding (The Basin of Mexico & The Internal Revolution of 550–650 CE)
Flourishing between 100 BCE and 650 CE in the highland Basin of Mexico, Teotihuacan ("The City of the Gods") was the largest pre-Columbian urban center in the Americas, boasting a peak population between 100,000 and 125,000 citizens. Unlike contemporary Maya city-states dominated by dynastic god-kings, Teotihuacan was characterized by a unique, highly egalitarian corporate urban model that suddenly collapsed not from external nomadic invasion, but from a coordinated, surgical populist revolution.

Key archaeological realities established by modern excavations (synthesized by Linda Manzanilla, René Millon, and George Cowgill):
1. **The Multi-Family Apartment Compound**: Over 80% of Teotihuacan’s massive population lived in spacious, single-story, lime-plastered **apartment compounds** (over 2,000 excavated across the city). Each walled compound (such as Tetitla, Zacuala, and Atetelco) housed 60 to 100 people across multiple family suites, featuring private open-air courtyards, sophisticated drainage pipes, and vibrant polychrome fresco murals depicting rain spirits, jaguars, and feathered serpents. This high standard of commoner housing was completely unparalleled in the ancient world.
2. **The Green Obsidian Monopoly**: Teotihuacan controlled the exclusive geological sources of **green Pachuca obsidian** (the "steel" of Mesoamerica). Through specialized industrial workshops, millions of standardized prismatic razor-blades, projectile points, and ceremonial eccentrics were manufactured, exported as far as Tikal and Kaminaljuyu to fund the city’s colossal public works.
3. **The Absence of Royal Glorification**: Strikingly, Teotihuacan left zero royal stelae, zero individualized portraits of kings, zero dynastic tombs, and no written propaganda glorifying autocratic rulers. Its monumental architecture—the colossal **Pyramid of the Sun** (65m tall, over 1 million m³ of fill) and the **Pyramid of the Moon** flanking the 2.5-kilometer **Avenue of the Dead**—employed the standardized *talud-tablero* architectural style (a sloping base apron topped by a framed vertical cantilever panel), symbolizing collective civic order and cosmic alignment.
4. **The Forensics of the Selective Burn Horizon (~550–650 CE)**: For decades, historians assumed Teotihuacan was sacked and burned by barbaric northern nomads. Exhaustive stratigraphic and radiocarbon micro-sampling proved a radically different reality: **the conflagration was strictly internal, highly selective, and intensely symbolic**.
   * Only the elite administrative palaces, temples, and aristocratic residences situated along the Avenue of the Dead (including the Ciudadela and the Temple of the Feathered Serpent) were systematically looted, burned, and dismantled.
   * Sculptures of gods and elite emblems were decapitated, smashed, and dragged into public plazas.
   * Crucially, the **surrounding commoner apartment compounds show zero evidence of burning or military sacking**. The people did not burn their own neighborhoods; they rose up and surgically incinerated the seats of centralized theocratic authority following prolonged droughts (the 536 CE volcanic dust veil event) and corrupt attempts by elite councils to concentrate wealth and establish hereditary monarchy.

## 2. Thematic Premise & Syllogism (writing-discovery)

* **The Stasis / The Lie:** "Our city has no king; the Avenue of the Dead balances all neighborhoods in holy equality, and the gods require only that we feed the pyramids with obsidian and maize."
* **The Crucible / Conflict:** During a multi-year drought, an aristocratic council consolidates inside the Ciudadela, hoarding grain reserves, demanding human retainers for private burials, and cutting the plaster budgets of commoner apartment compounds.
* **Dramatic Syllogism:**  
  $$\text{Creeping Aristocratic Centralization} + \text{Armed Populist Compound Solidarity} = \text{Selective Palatial Immolation \& De-Sacralization of the Pyramids}$$
* **The Truth / Synthesis:** An egalitarian metropolis will not tolerate the rebirth of despotism. When rulers attempt to elevate their palaces above the common plaza, the people will burn the palaces to ash while keeping their homes intact.

### Truby 4-Corner Opposition Web
* **Corner 1 (The Establishment / High Priest-Prefect of the Ciudadela):** Attempts to establish hereditary royal lineage; hoards green obsidian cores and grain inside palatial vaults, guarded by foreign Maya and Zapotec mercenaries with feathered atlatls.
* **Corner 2 (The Zealot Opponent / Apartment Compound Militia Captain):** Veteran warrior from the Tetitla residential compound; mobilizes neighborhood obsidian-knappers into street phalanges armed with prismatic blade-lances to burn the elite palaces along the Avenue of the Dead.
* **Corner 3 (The Thematic Shadow / Obsidian Smuggling Factor):** Commercial merchant who supplies weapons to both sides; aims to use the chaos of the burning palaces to breach and loot the sacred emerald and jade offerings sealed deep within the Pyramid of the Sun.
* **Corner 4 (The Tertiary Outcast / Highland Lime-Kiln Burner):** Exhausted artisan living in the deforested hills surrounding the valley; blinded by decades of burning limestone into white plaster for the city, he warns that burning the palaces will release choking lime clouds that will poison the valley’s water cisterns.

## 3. Injected Tabletop Mechanics (adventure_framework_srd.md)

### 3.1 4-Level Dynamic Dial: The Populist Conflagration
* **Level 1 (Avenue Curfew & Obsidian Blade Rationing / Light):**  
  * *Condition:* Palace guards patrol the Avenue of the Dead; green obsidian sales restricted; murmuring in apartment courtyards.
  * *Mechanics:* Street transit delayed by checkpoint interrogations (DC 10 Deception or Bureaucracy to pass with weapons). Shaving raw obsidian cores requires DC 10 Crafting to avoid 1d4 bleeding damage.
* **Level 2 (Plaza Barricades & Smashed Frescoes / Moderate):**  
  * *Condition:* Street barricades erected between apartment districts; commoners refuse palace corvée; frescoes defaced with charcoal slogans.
  * *Mechanics:* Difficult terrain along compound alleys. DC 13 Athletics or Acrobatics to climb street barricades; riotous crowds impose DC 13 Will saves against crowd panic.
* **Level 3 (Surgical Palace Immolation & Talud-Tablero Collapse / Severe):**  
  * *Condition:* Thousands of compound citizens storm the Avenue of the Dead with pitch torches; elite palaces ablaze; timber ceiling beams snap, dropping heavy stone facades.
  * *Mechanics:* Blazing urban combat. Navigating burning palace corridors requires DC 16 Reflex saves to avoid collapsing talud-tablero stone lintels (takes 4d8 fire and bludgeoning damage). Palace guards unleash deadly atlatl dart volleys (DC 16 Cover/Dodge).
* **Level 4 (The Great Pyramidal Inferno & De-Sacralization / Lethal Climax):**  
  * *Condition:* The Ciudadela burns to the ground; the Feathered Serpent temple sculptures are smashed and dragged into the streets; centralized authority permanently dissolves; thick black smoke blankets the valley.
  * *Mechanics:* Absolute political collapse. The node loses its imperial status immediately. Characters inside burning administrative sectors must pass DC 20 Fortitude/Athletics saves or suffer 8d10 lethal fire/smoke damage. The central state is permanently destroyed; neighborhoods self-govern as autonomous clan communes.

### 3.2 Threat Clocks
* **Palace Immolation Ignition Clock [4 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks when palace guards execute protestors or commoner grain rations are halved.
  * *Consequence when full:* Fires ignited along the Avenue of the Dead; triggers Dial Level 3.
* **Avenue of the Dead Barricade Breach [6 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks each turn the populist compound militia repels mercenary sorties.
  * *Consequence when full:* Ciudadela gates battered down; elite rulers captured and tried.

## 5. Scene Calculus & Tactical Tabletop Sparks (writing-generation)

* **Scene 1: The Obsidian Razor Shakedown (Arrival Collision):**
  * *Collision ($><$):* The party enters the Sun Plaza attempting to purchase supplies or meet an official, but is cornered by Ciudadela palace mercenaries armed with obsidian atlatls who demand the party surrender all high-grade green obsidian weapons under emergency state decree; simultaneously, a mob of compound artisans armed with knapping hammers surrounds the guards, demanding the party give them the weapons to fight the palace.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* The party expects to navigate a wealthy commercial trade empire, but is caught in a violent jurisdictional crossfire between state confiscators and armed revolutionary artisans.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The palace captain demands obedience in the name of the divine cosmic serpent, but his sandals are caked in ash from a riot he barely escaped an hour ago.
* **Scene 2: The Firebrand in the Mural Courtyard (Resource Dilemma):**
  * *Collision ($><$):* In a burning aristocratic palace, rebels have cornered a group of teenage scribes and are preparing to throw pitch onto the walls to incinerate a library of priceless painted bark-paper tax and trade genealogies, which the rebels view as instruments of their enslavement.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* The party has only seconds to intervene: saving the historical scrolls will require fighting their way through the passionate populist rebels (alienating the entire commoner compound), while standing aside allows the recorded history of Central America’s greatest city to vanish into ash forever.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The rebel leader shouts that no child should grow up reading the debt-titles that enslaved their parents, holding the burning brand directly over the sacred pictograms.
* **Scene 3: The Toppling of the Feathered Serpent (Severance Crisis):**
  * *Collision ($><$):* At the summit of the Quetzalcoatl Pyramid, populist insurgents have hooked heavy maguey-fiber hauling ropes around a 4-ton basalt serpent head, rocking it to topple down the steep pyramid stairs into the burning courtyard below where trapped palace nobles are barricaded.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* The party must choose between cutting the hauling ropes to prevent the slaughter of the trapped nobles (risking being thrown down the 65-meter pyramid face by the crowd), or using the falling monolith to smash open the palace gates and loot the sacred subterranean chambers of green obsidian beneath the foundation.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* As the 4-ton stone head groans on its mortar, the air vibrates with rhythmic chanting from 10,000 commoners in the plaza below, marking the exact moment a dynasty dies.
