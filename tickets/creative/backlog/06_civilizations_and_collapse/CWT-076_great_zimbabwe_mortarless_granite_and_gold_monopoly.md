## 1. Executive Summary & Historical Research Dossier

### Archaeological Grounding (The Kingdom of Zimbabwe & The Indian Ocean Trade Network)
Flourishing between 1100 and 1450 CE on the southeastern highveld plateau of modern Zimbabwe, Great Zimbabwe was the capital of a flourishing medieval African empire established by the ancestors of the Shona people. Covering nearly 1,800 acres and housing an urban population exceeding 18,000 citizens, this stone metropolis served as the commercial nexus linking the interior goldfields of Southern Africa to the maritime trade networks of the Indian Ocean.

Key archaeological realities established by modern excavations (David Beach, Peter Garlake, and Innocent Pikirayi):
1. **The Pinnacle of Mortarless Drystone Architecture**: Great Zimbabwe’s monumental walls were constructed entirely **without mortar or cement**. Masons exploited the natural geological weathering of the surrounding granite batholiths, where thermal expansion and contraction caused granite to split naturally in uniform, flat sheets (*exfoliation granite*). Masons fractured these sheets with fire and water into regular, brick-like rectangular ashlar blocks.
   * **Battered Slopes & Rounded Corners**: To ensure structural integrity without mortar, walls were engineered with an inward slope (*batter*) of roughly 5 to 10 degrees, allowing gravity to lock the stones together. Straight right angles were strictly avoided; all walls curve in graceful, serpent-like arcs to eliminate shear stress points.
   * **Decorative Friezes**: Upper courses featured intricate decorative masonry friezes, most notably double-chevron and herringbone patterns symbolizing the sacred python, fertility, and royal lightning.
2. **The Great Enclosure and the Solid Conical Tower**: The largest pre-colonial architectural monument in sub-Saharan Africa, the **Great Enclosure**, features an outer perimeter wall stretching over 250 meters in circumference, rising up to 11 meters (36 feet) high and 6 meters thick, incorporating roughly **one million dressed granite blocks**. Inside the narrow stone corridors stands the enigmatic **Conical Tower**—a solid, 9-meter-tall cylinder of precision-fitted granite with no interior rooms, functioning as a colossal symbolic grain silo representing the royal monopoly on surplus food, wealth, and spiritual fertility.
3. **The Indian Ocean Commercial Monopoly**: Great Zimbabwe grew wealthy by controlling the chokepoint between the interior gold-panning streams of the Zimbabwean craton and the Swahili port cities of the Indian Ocean coast (particularly Sofala and Kilwa Kisiwani). Archaeological strata have yielded thousands of Persian glazed tiles, Syrian glass vessels, Arabian coins, and **exquisite Chinese Song and Ming dynasty celadon and blue-and-white porcelain**, alongside millions of colorful glass trade beads imported from India that circulated as regional currency.
4. **The Sacred Soapstone Birds**: Found in the hilltop sanctum (*the Hill Complex*), eight meticulously carved **soapstone birds** perched atop meter-high stone monoliths represented royal ancestors and the bateleur eagle (*chapungu*). In Shona cosmology, these eagles acted as divine messengers between the paramount king and *Mwari*, the supreme creator god.
5. **The Ecological Threshold & Peaceful Relocation (~1450 CE)**: Great Zimbabwe was never conquered, besieged, or sacked. Its abandonment was the result of exceeding its immediate environmental carrying capacity:
   * **Pastoral Over-Saturation**: Wealth was stored in vast herds of long-horned cattle. Over several centuries, thousands of royal cattle stripped the surrounding plateau grasses, causing severe soil erosion and cattle epidemics.
   * **Deforestation**: Sustaining 18,000 people and fueling continuous granite-splitting fires completely denuded the savannah woodlands of firewood for miles in every direction.
   * **The Trade Route Shift**: Simultaneously, alluvial gold reefs in the immediate south dried up, and maritime trade shifted north toward the Zambezi River. The royal court and population peacefully migrated north to establish the Kingdom of Mutapa and south to Torwa, leaving the monumental granite walls standing pristine in the empty savannah.

## 2. Thematic Premise & Syllogism (writing-discovery)

* **The Stasis / The Lie:** "Our granite walls curve without mortar like the coils of the sacred python; our gold buys the porcelain of the Emperor of China, and our cattle herds will graze these green hills forever."
* **The Crucible / Conflict:** Overgrazing turns the highveld pastures into dusty scrub while local gold panning yields dwindle; foreign Swahili trade dhows shift their caravans north to the Zambezi, leaving Great Zimbabwe’s kings stranded behind massive walls without cattle or trade goods.
* **Dramatic Syllogism:**  
  $$\text{Pastoral Ecological Overgrazing} + \text{Caravan Trade Route Relocation} = \text{Peaceful Evacuation \& Deserted Monumental Granite Reliquary}$$
* **The Truth / Synthesis:** The thickest granite walls on earth cannot hold an empire when the pasture dies and trade caravans sail away. Power built upon transit monopolies dissolves without violence the moment the world chooses another road.

### Truby 4-Corner Opposition Web
* **Corner 1 (The Establishment / Shona Paramount King of the Great Enclosure):** Sits beside the solid Conical Tower surrounded by his royal wives and gold-smiths; demands cattle tribute and forces masons to dress granite blocks for higher walls, denying that trade has moved north.
* **Corner 2 (The Zealot Opponent / Northern Zambezi Gold-Pannier Chieftain):** Secessionist warlord who has established new mining camps along the Zambezi River (nascent Mutapa); offers Swahili merchants lower tariffs and fresh pasture, actively diverting caravans away from Great Zimbabwe.
* **Corner 3 (The Thematic Shadow / Swahili Coastal Trade Factor):** Merchant agent residing in the valley compounds; hoards Persian glass beads and brass wire, secretly liquidating his granite storehouses to flee to Kilwa before the southern trade collapses entirely.
* **Corner 4 (The Tertiary Outcast / Hill-Complex Soapstone Carver):** Master artisan who sculpts royal eagle monoliths; perceives the omens in the dying grass and warns the royal council that the bateleur eagles have already flown north to the Zambezi.

## 3. Injected Tabletop Mechanics (adventure_framework_srd.md)

### 3.1 4-Level Dynamic Dial: Highveld Carrying Capacity & Commercial Siphon
* **Level 1 (Firewood Scarcity & Granite Chipping Dust / Light):**  
  * *Condition:* Woodland cleared for miles; fuel wood is scarce and expensive; granite masons work under cloudless skies; fine rock dust blankets the enclosure.
  * *Mechanics:* Procuring camp firewood costs triple. Chipping fresh granite ashlar requires DC 10 Crafting or Athletics to avoid sharp stone shards (1d4 slashing damage).
* **Level 2 (Pasture Erosion & Gold Panning Slump / Moderate):**  
  * *Condition:* Grasslands stripped down to red dirt; dust storms sweep through cattle kraals; gold panning yields in the river valleys decline by 50%.
  * *Mechanics:* Moving across dusty pasture requires DC 13 Survival. Purchasing provisions with debased gold dust requires DC 13 Appraisal to avoid being cheated by Swahili trade agents.
* **Level 3 (Cattle Murrain & Caravan Diversion / Severe):**  
  * *Condition:* Disease sweeps overgrazed cattle herds; dying cattle poison watering holes; Swahili merchant caravans bypass the city entirely, marching north.
  * *Mechanics:* Contaminated stream water requires a DC 16 Fortitude save or inflicts severe dysentery (takes 3d8 poison damage). Armed cattle rustlers prowl the valley outskirts (DC 16 Perception to avoid ambushes).
* **Level 4 (Total Highveld Abandonment & Empty Granite Bastion / Lethal Climax):**  
  * *Condition:* The royal court packs the sacred soapstone birds and marches north; the city’s 18,000 citizens peacefully disperse; the Great Enclosure is left completely deserted in the savannah.
  * *Mechanics:* Total systemic abandonment. The node loses its commercial status; trade goods and food supplies drop to zero. Characters stranded in the empty stone complex face DC 20 Survival checks to find game or water across the denuded plateau. The site becomes an uninhabited archaeological monument.

### 3.2 Threat Clocks
* **Pasture Carrying Capacity Depletion Clock [6 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks each turn cattle herds remain overstocked on un-rested pasture or drought shrivels grass.
  * *Consequence when full:* Pasture turns to desert; cattle die en masse; triggers Dial Level 3.
* **Swahili Trade Route Northward Diversion Meter [4 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks when northern Zambezi miners offer cheaper gold or royal tariffs in the Great Enclosure are raised.
  * *Consequence when full:* Caravan traffic hits 0; Swahili merchants abandon their valley compounds.

## 5. Scene Calculus & Tactical Tabletop Sparks (writing-generation)

* **Scene 1: The Cattle Stampede in the Parallel Passage (Arrival Collision):**
  * *Collision ($><$):* The party enters the Great Enclosure through the narrow, 1-meter-wide "Parallel Passage" between towering 11-meter granite walls, only to collide with a panicked herd of 50 royal long-horned cattle driven by desperate herders fleeing a dust storm; behind the cattle, armed guards block the exit with iron spears, accusing the party of spooking the king's sacred herd.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* The party expects a majestic royal audience, but is trapped in a claustrophobic stone canyon facing 1,000 pounds of charging horned beasts with nowhere to dodge.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The guard captain bellows that the king’s cattle are sacred gods of prosperity, but his ribs show through his skin and he hungrily eyes the dead calf trampled underfoot.
* **Scene 2: The Fire-Cracking of the Batholith (Resource Dilemma):**
  * *Collision ($><$):* At the royal quarry, desperate masons have built a massive bonfire of the city’s last seasoned timber logs against a sacred granite dome, preparing to pour cold well water over the red-hot stone to crack it into building blocks for the king's new wall, while local elders beg them to save the timber to cook corn for starving children.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* If the party quenches the fire to save the fuel, the royal taskmasters attack them for treason against the king’s building program; if they allow the cracking, the batholith shatters into fine building stone, but the last firewood in the district is gone.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The master mason argues that without stone walls, the people are mere wild animals in the bush, while the smoke from the fire chokes the very children he claims to protect.
* **Scene 3: The Secret Packing of the Soapstone Eagle (Severance Crisis):**
  * *Collision ($><$):* In the dead of night atop the Hill Complex, the royal high priest and a band of elite warriors are secretly dismantling the sacred soapstone bird monoliths, wrapping them in ox-hides to smuggle them out of the city toward the northern Zambezi, leaving the common citizens asleep in the valley below completely unaware that their rulers are abandoning them.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* If the party sounds the alarm to alert the townspeople, a bloody civil war will break out in the dark between the royal guards and abandoned commoners; if the party helps the priest slip away, the priest promises them an ancient ingot of pure gold and safe passage to the coast.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The priest holds the carved stone bird to his ear, whispering that the eagle has already spread its wings toward the north, and that a king who stays behind to die with his walls is no king at all.
