## 1. Executive Summary & Historical Research Dossier

### Archaeological Grounding (The Antikythera Shipwreck & The 30-Gear Differential Computer)
Discovered in 1900–1901 by Greek sponge divers from Symi off the rugged, storm-tossed cliffs of the tiny Aegean island of Antikythera, the Antikythera Shipwreck was a colossal Roman merchant galley (dated to circa 70–60 BCE) carrying a vast cargo of looted Hellenistic Greek treasures—bronze statues, glassware, marble sculptures, and amphorae. Among the recovered artifacts was an unassuming, heavily corroded, calcified bronze lump that would prove to be the most sophisticated technological artifact of the ancient world.

Key scientific realities uncovered by the Antikythera Mechanism Research Project (Tony Freeth, Mike Edmunds, Yanis Bitsakis, John Seiradakis via 3D X-ray computed tomography):
1. **The Pin-and-Slot Differential Mechanism**: Dating to circa 200–100 BCE (from the mechanical traditions associated with Archimedes of Syracuse, Posidonius of Rhodes, or Hipparchus), the shoebox-sized device contained **at least 30 precision bronze gears** cut with triangular 60-degree teeth, some with radii as small as 1.5 millimeters. Most astonishingly, it incorporated a **differential gear turntable**—a mechanical principle not reinvented in Europe until the 16th century CE—to subtract the motion of the Sun from the Moon to calculate the synodic lunar phase.
2. **Modeling Non-Linear Lunar Physics**: To account for the Moon’s variable orbital velocity (what Kepler would later identify as an elliptical orbit), the machine utilized an ingenious **pin-and-slot epicyclic gear mechanism** that reproduced the Hipparchian first lunar anomaly. A pin mounted eccentrically on one gear engaged a slot in a second revolving gear on a slightly offset axis, mechanically speeding up and slowing down the lunar pointer exactly matching celestial mechanics.
3. **The Multi-Dial Cosmic Dashboard**:
   * **Front Dial**: Displayed the 365-day Egyptian solar calendar (with an adjustable leap-year ring) and the position of the Sun and Moon through the twelve signs of the Zodiac. A miniature rotating black-and-white spherical bead mechanically displayed the exact phase of the Moon.
   * **Planetary Pointers**: Separate geared trains displayed the synodic cycles of the five known planets (Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn) using complex epicyclic gear ratios.
   * **Upper Back Dial**: A 5-turn spiral tracking the **235-month Metonic cycle** (linking 19 solar years to lunar months), with a subsidiary dial for the 76-year Callippic cycle.
   * **Olympiad Dial**: A 4-year cycle tracking the sacred Panhellenic games (Olympia, Pythia, Nemea, and Isthmia).
   * **Lower Back Dial**: A 4-turn spiral tracking the **223-month Saros eclipse cycle**. Engraved glyph inscriptions (*glyph indices*) specified the exact hour, color, wind direction, and celestial magnitude of both solar and lunar eclipses, alongside an *Exeligmos* dial to calibrate 54-year triple-Saros time shifts.
4. **The Tragic Technological Chasm**: The Antikythera Mechanism represents a shimmering, isolated technological pinnacle. When the Hellenistic kingdoms fell and Roman plunder fleets stripped Greek academies of their intellectual treasures, this mechanical knowledge was utterly severed from production. The ability to craft differential bronze clockwork vanished from the Earth for over 1,500 years, not re-emerging until the great astronomical cathedral clocks of Renaissance Europe. The device rested at a depth of 45 meters, crusted in sea salts and oxidized into green bronze canker, a silent monument to a lost scientific age.

## 2. Thematic Premise & Syllogism (writing-discovery)

* **The Stasis / The Lie:** "Mechanical gears are crude brute tools fit only for grinding grain, pumping bilges, and hoisting stones; the majestic dance of the planets and the terrifying eclipses of the heavens belong solely to the arbitrary whims of the gods."
* **The Crucible / Conflict:** A heavily loaded Roman plunder galley founders against the razor reefs of Antikythera in an Aegean gale; the Greek artisan who built the 30-gear astronomical computer and the Roman prize-master fight over the bronze casing as the splintering hull plunges toward the 45-meter seabed abyss.
* **Dramatic Syllogism:**  
  $$\text{Isolated Intellectual Mechanical Genius} + \text{Imperial Plunder Shipwreck Severance} = \text{Fifteen Centuries of Technological Amnesia}$$
* **The Truth / Synthesis:** Theoretical brilliance cannot survive without an institutional industrial ecosystem. When a masterwork of human science is treated merely as imperial loot rather than shared knowledge, a single shipwreck can plunge human civilization into a millennium and a half of darkness.

### Truby 4-Corner Opposition Web
* **Corner 1 (The Establishment / Roman Centurion Prize-Master):** Sees the clockwork box purely as an exotic gold-plated curio to present to Julius Caesar or Cicero to purchase political advancement; threatens to execute the crew if the bronze casing is scratched, completely oblivious to its mathematical function.
* **Corner 2 (The Zealot Opponent / Archimedean Master-Horologist):** The elderly Greek mathematician-artisan who spent twenty years cutting 30 bronze gears with hand-files; prefers to smash the mechanism into the sea rather than allow his sacred celestial calculus to become an ornament in a Roman tyrant's bathhouse.
* **Corner 3 (The Thematic Shadow / Sponge-Diver Smuggler Captain):** Unscrupulous Aegean salvager who cares nothing for astronomy or imperial politics; plans to crowbar open the bronze casing to melt down the gears for bronze ingot scrap and sell the zodiac gemstone dials to Syrian jewelers.
* **Corner 4 (The Tertiary Outcast / The Hermit Astronomer of Antikythera):** A solitary exile living in the island’s cliffside caves who reads the Saros inscription and realizes the machine has just predicted an impending total solar eclipse that will plunge the stormy reef into blackness within one hour.

## 3. Injected Tabletop Mechanics (adventure_framework_srd.md)

### 3.1 4-Level Dynamic Dial: Clockwork Precision & Shipwreck Abyss
* **Level 1 (Gear Tooth Click & Bronze Canker Stiffening / Light):**  
  * *Condition:* The hand-crank is turned; internal bronze gears click with mechanical precision; sea spray coats the bronze casing.
  * *Mechanics:* Operating the manual crank requires a DC 10 Intelligence or Crafting (Clockwork) check. Successful operation reveals the accurate position of the Sun and Moon, granting advantage on all Navigation and Astrological checks.
* **Level 2 (Pin-and-Slot Jam & Reef Gale / Moderate):**  
  * *Condition:* Ship pitches violently in Aegean breakers; salt water enters the wooden casing; the eccentric lunar pin slips from its slot, causing the differential gear train to bind.
  * *Mechanics:* Clearing the jammed pin requires a DC 13 Dexterity (Sleight of Hand) or Engineering check. Failing causes a gear tooth to shear, temporarily disabling the Metonic dial. Ship navigation across jagged reefs requires DC 13 Vehicles (Water).
* **Level 3 (Hull Fracture & Differential Escapement Slip / Severe):**  
  * *Condition:* The Roman galley strikes the reef; wooden hull timbers splinter; water surges into the hold; the mechanism’s Saros eclipse dial spins wildly as the gear casing is crushed under shifting amphorae.
  * *Mechanics:* Characters in the hold must pass DC 16 Reflex saves to avoid being pinned by rolling 100-pound wine amphorae (deals 4d8 bludgeoning damage). Extracting the 30-pound bronze box from the rising bilge requires DC 16 Athletics or Mechanics.
* **Level 4 (Catastrophic 45-Meter Plunge & Cosmic Eclipse Lock / Lethal Climax):**  
  * *Condition:* The ship breaks in two and plunges into the 45-meter seabed trench; simultaneously, the predicted total solar eclipse occurs, plunging the storm into freezing, pitch-black daytime darkness.
  * *Mechanics:* Absolute maritime disaster. Characters caught in the sinking hull must succeed on a DC 20 Athletics (Swimming) or Survival check or suffer 8d10 lethal crushing/drowning damage as the wreckage descends into the abyss. If the mechanism is lost to the sea floor, all technological knowledge of differential gearing is permanently wiped from the campaign setting.

### 3.2 Threat Clocks
* **Saros Total Eclipse Countdown [6 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks each turn the machine’s hand-crank is advanced toward the glyph inscription or as storm clouds darken the sun.
  * *Consequence when full:* Total solar eclipse triggers Dial Level 4 daytime darkness; panic sweeps the survivors.
* **Galley Hull Plunge to 45m Abyss [4 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks as reef rocks breach the hull and bilge pumps fail.
  * *Consequence when full:* Ship sinks immediately to the sea floor; the mechanism is entombed in underwater silt.

## 5. Scene Calculus & Tactical Tabletop Sparks (writing-generation)

* **Scene 1: The Turning of the Bronze Crank (Arrival Collision):**
  * *Collision ($><$):* In the pitching cabin of the Roman galley, the party discovers the bronze chest; as they touch the winding crown, the Greek horologist lunges with an iron chisel to defend his creation, while the Roman Centurion draws his gladius, shouting that anyone who touches the Emperor’s prize will have their hands chopped off.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* The party enters expecting a straightforward sea voyage or salvage job, but triggers an immediate lethal Mexican standoff over a delicate mechanical artifact that is clicking toward an imminent eclipse.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The Centurion speaks of imperial law and gold value, but his eyes reveal terror of the unnatural, clicking bronze gears that appear to move the stars without human breath.
* **Scene 2: The Reef Breakers of Antikythera (Hull Crisis / Environmental Dilemma):**
  * *Collision ($><$):* The galley’s steering oars smash against a hidden submerged reef; water erupts through the hull planks. The Master Horologist screams that the bronze mechanism must be wrapped in oiled sheepskin and carried to the ship’s dinghy, while the Roman crew demands the dinghy be reserved solely for the chest of stolen silver denarii and the captain's life.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* The party must choose between using their actions to secure the irreplaceable scientific masterwork of antiquity, or saving three drowning teenage rowers chained to their benches in the flooding hold.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The horologist shouts that a million men will be born to row boats, but only once in ten thousand years will a mortal hand forge the gears of the cosmos.
* **Scene 3: The 45-Meter Abyssal Plunge (Climax Salvage Dilemma):**
  * *Collision ($><$):* The ship snaps across a granite outcrop; the half containing the bronze mechanism slips over the edge of the continental shelf, sliding down a sheer underwater cliff toward the 45-meter seabed trench. As the sky turns to midday midnight during the total solar eclipse, the party has only three combat rounds to dive into the churn, hook a harpoon line into the wooden casing, and winch it to the surface before it is swallowed forever by the abyss.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* A successful winch haul recovers the greatest technological breakthrough in human history, but the winch cable is tangled around the anchor of a rival salvage skiff whose crew is furiously cutting the rope with machetes to save their own boat from being dragged under.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* Under the unnatural black sun of the eclipse, the sea turns silver-gray, and the ticking of the gears can be heard echoing through the taut hemp rope just before it begins to fray.
