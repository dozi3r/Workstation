# 01: Caloric & Water Scaffolding for Tabletop Worldbuilding

## 1. The Core Numbers (Rule of Thumb for GMs)
To give GMs believable verisimilitude without turning tabletop into an accounting spreadsheet:

1. **Daily Caloric Threshold**:
   * Average human: **2,000–2,500 kcal/day** (~1–1.5 lbs of dense grain bread, dried peas, or porridge).
   * Manual laborer / soldier / traveler: **3,000–3,500 kcal/day**.
2. **Daily Water Threshold**:
   * Survival minimum: **2 liters/day**.
   * Functional baseline (drinking, pot cooking, minimal wash): **8–10 liters/day** per adult.
   * Work beast (ox, mule, warhorse): **30–50 liters/day**.

## 2. The Agrarian Radius
* A standard pre-industrial settlement feeds itself from an agricultural radius extending **1 to 3 miles** from its perimeter.
* Beyond 3 miles, hauling manure and grain by ox-cart consumes a non-trivial percentage of the food being transported.
* This explains why Pointcrawl **Satellite Nodes** exist around a **Sovereign Hub**: the Hub cannot grow its own calories within its walls and must extract food along transit edges.

## 3. The Seasonal "Hunger Gap"
* Tabletop fantasy often treats food as constant. Historically, pre-industrial societies experienced the **Hunger Gap** in late spring:
  * Grain stores from last autumn are nearly empty or spoiling with weevils.
  * Winter crops are eaten, but spring crops have not yet matured.
* This creates an incredible narrative hook for mid-campaign adventures: tension, bread riots, hoarding, and desperate foraging.
