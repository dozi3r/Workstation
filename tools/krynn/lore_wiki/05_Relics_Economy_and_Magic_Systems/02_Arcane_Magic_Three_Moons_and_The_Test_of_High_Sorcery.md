## 1. Executive Summary & The Arcane Foundation

On Krynn, arcane magic does not originate from an ambient, disembodied "Weave" or cosmic accident. It is a gift granted directly by the **Three Gods of Magic**—[[Solinari]] (White Moon), [[Lunitari]] (Red Moon), and [[Nuitari]] (Black Moon)—who abandoned the outer celestial planes during the All-Saints War to dwell permanently in the skies of Krynn as visible satellites.

Arcane spellcasters on Krynn operate under two absolute physical and philosophical realities:
1. **The Celestial Tether:** A wizard’s magical potency, spell DC, and available spell slots wax and wane with the orbital phases of their patron moon.
2. **The Conclave's Absolute Invariant:** *"Magic is above all things."* The Art transcends nationality, race, familial ties, and moral allegiances. Any mortal who manifests arcane power beyond basic cantrips must swear allegiance to the **Orders of High Sorcery** and undergo the lethal **Test of High Sorcery** at the [[Tower of Wayreth]], or be hunted down and executed as a **Renegade**.

### 2.1 The Optical Invariant of Nuitari
As established in canonical lore and margin commentary (*The Annotated Chronicles*, p. 594), **Nuitari is physically invisible to common mortals and mages of the White and Red Robes**. 
- To normal eyes, the night sky shows only Solinari’s bright silver radiance and Lunitari’s crimson glow.
- Only those who have sworn the dark oath of the **Black Robes** or tapped into forbidden necrotic voids can perceive Nuitari. To these casters, Nuitari appears as a terrifying, pitch-black sphere drifting against the firmament—not an absence of light, but an active, star-devouring obsidian hole that blots out constellations as it traverses the sky.

### 2.3 Lunar Conjunctions & The Night of the Eye
When two or more moons align in the same celestial quadrant, arcane resonance multiplies geometrically:
- **Conjunction of Two:** Occurs frequently between Lunitari and Nuitari (due to Nuitari's rapid 8-day orbit). Caster bonuses stack: $+2$ to Spell Save DC, and all range/duration variables of aligned spells increase by $50\%$.
- **The Night of the Eye (Tri-Lunar Conjunction):** Occurs exactly once every **504 days** (the mathematical lowest common multiple of 36, 28, and 8 days: $\text{LCM}(36, 28, 8) = 504$). 
  - *The Cosmic Alignment:* Solinari, Lunitari, and Nuitari reach **High Sanction simultaneously**, appearing in the sky as a single colossal radiant eye—a silver pupil ringed in crimson fire, backed by an obsidian void.
  - *Campaign Mechanics:* During the 24 hours of the Night of the Eye, all Wizards of High Sorcery receive $+3$ to Spell Save DC, $+3$ to Spell Attacks, double spell slot regeneration on a Short Rest, and the ability to cast ritual spells across planar boundaries. It was during a Night of the Eye that the Dragon Orbs were forged during the Third Dragon War, and it is the only celestial window in which the Portal to the Abyss in the [[Temple of Neraka]] can be permanently unsealed.

+------------------------+------------------------------------------------------------------------------+

+------------------------+------------------------------------------------------------------------------+

### 3.1 The Cursed Alterations of Raistlin Majere
When Head of the Conclave [[Par-Salian]] subjected twenty-year-old Raistlin Majere to his premature Test of High Sorcery, he recognized both the boy’s colossal arcane genius and his catastrophic arrogance. To forge him into a weapon against the gathering darkness while teaching him compassion, Par-Salian inflicted permanent physical curses upon his body:
1. **The Golden Skin:** Raistlin’s epidermis was turned into an uncanny, metallic golden hue, reflecting arcane radiation and setting him apart as an unnatural freak among common folk.
2. **The Hourglass Eyes (The Eyes of Raelana):** Raistlin’s pupils were transformed into golden, slitted hourglasses. Through these eyes, **he sees all living things as they are affected by the passage of time**:
   - Fresh blooming flowers appear as rotting stalks dripping brown nectar.
   - Youthful, beautiful women (like [[Laurana]] or [[Tika Waylan]]) appear as wrinkled, toothless crones with sunken flesh and clouded cataracts.
   - Magnificent stone palaces appear as cracked, moss-grown ruins on the verge of collapse.
   - *Tabletop Law:* Raistlin gains permanent advantage on Wisdom (Perception) checks to detect illusions, decay, necrotic rot, or structural flaws, but suffers disadvantage on Charisma checks to form emotional or romantic attachments, trapped in an unending vision of universal mortality.

## 4. Fistandantilus and the Soulforge Blood-Pact

During the climax of his Test at the [[Tower of Wayreth]] (346 AC), Raistlin stood alone, his spell slots exhausted, facing the lethal sorcery of the spectral dark elf archmage [[Liam]]. At the point of agonizing death, Raistlin knelt upon the cold flagstones and called out into the void for aid.

The spirit of **Fistandantilus**—the ancient, arch-necromancer of the Age of Might who destroyed Zhaman—answered:
- **The Bargain:** Fistandantilus offered his ancient arcane memory, high-tier spell formulae, and invulnerable magical warding to save Raistlin’s life. In exchange, Raistlin surrendered his physical life-force: Fistandantilus acts as a metaphysical parasite, feeding upon Raistlin's vitality to anchor his consciousness in the mortal plane.
- **The Bloodstone Conduit:** The dark archmage’s consciousness was linked to the **Bloodstone of Fistandantilus**—a pulsating emerald talisman worn upon Raistlin’s throat or hidden within his robes.
- **The D&D Campaign Mechanic:** Whenever Raistlin casts a spell of 5th level or higher, he must make a DC 16 Constitution saving throw. On a failure, he gains 1 level of Exhaustion as Fistandantilus drains his marrow; on a success, the ancient archmage's whispered incantations grant $+2$ to the spell's damage rolls and cause the spell to bypass necrotic resistance.

### 5.1 The Shifting Forest of Wayreth
The Tower of Wayreth is defended by a living, shifting extradimensional barrier known as the **Forest of Wayreth**:
- The forest does not exist in a fixed geographical coordinate; it moves across hundreds of miles of wilderness to present its borders to those who are summoned by the Conclave, and hides itself completely from armies, invaders, or casual travelers.
- Entering the forest without an invitation causes the traveler to wander in endless recursive circles, eventually walking out the side they entered, or being driven to madness by optical illusions and whispering shadows.

### 5.2 The Curse of the Shoikan Grove
Surrounding the Tower of High Sorcery in Palanthas stands the **Shoikan Grove**:
- Planted during the final days of the Age of Might when the Black Robe archmage Rann detonated himself from the upper parapets, placing a death-curse upon the grounds.
- **The Terror Effect:** Any living creature setting foot beneath the black oak branches must succeed on a **DC 18 Wisdom saving throw** every round or become paralyzed with supernatural terror, turning to flee blindly. Those who fail by 5 or more have their hearts stop, dying of pure horror.
- **The Undead Minions:** The grove is infested with spectral guardians, animated skeletal roots, and weeping banshees who tear the flesh from living intruders.
- **The Key of Mastery:** Only one who possesses the Staff of Magius, knows the secret words of power, and bears the ancient mark of Fistandantilus can walk through the Shoikan Grove without harm.

## 6. The Modular Test of High Sorcery: D&D Campaign Subsystem

The Test of High Sorcery is the ultimate rite of passage for any arcane caster. A DM can run the Test as a high-stakes, 4-stage modular scenario:

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                    THE THREE AXIOMS OF THE TEST                                       |
| 1. All who enter must pass, or die. There is no honorable retreat or surrender.                       |
| 2. Magic must be valued above all mortal loyalties, nations, and personal loves.                      |
| 3. The trial measures the soul's crack; the initiate must confront their deepest moral shadow.        |
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 6.1 Eligibility, Summons & The Escort Protocol
- **Level Threshold:** An apprentice is summoned when they reach 3rd to 5th level (capable of casting 2nd- or 3rd-level spells). Practicing magic beyond this point without taking the Test marks the caster as a Renegade.
- **The Summons:** A spectral courier or a master of the order delivers a sealed token with an appointed date at the edge of the Forest of Wayreth.
- **The Companion Mandate:** In rare instances involving twin souls or extreme emotional attachments (such as Raistlin and [[Caramon Majere]]), the Conclave demands that the companion accompany the initiate into the Tower to isolate and test their psychological dependencies.

---

### 6.2 The Four-Stage Trial Gauntlet Engine

#### Stage I: The Spell-Depletion Attrition Matrix
The initiate begins with their full daily allotment of spell slots. Over a sequence of escalating environmental hazards, gravity shifts, and puzzle chambers, the initiate is forced to expend slots rapidly:
- *Atmospheric Mana Flux:* Before casting any spell in the testing vaults, the caster must make a **DC 13 Intelligence saving throw**. On a failure, volatile mana fluctuations cause the spell to consume a slot one tier higher than intended.
- *Objective:* The candidate must enter Stage II with no more than two 1st-level spell slots and one 2nd-level spell slot remaining.

#### Stage II: The Combat Crucible
The Conclave conjures a mortal adversary specifically engineered to counter the initiate's preferred combat tactics (e.g., a spell-resistant dark elf sorcerer, an armored anti-mage inquisitor, or a summoned planar fiend):
- *The Battle:* The foe attacks with lethal intent. There are no non-lethal illusions in this chamber; hits deal real damage.
- *The Exhaustion Drain:* At the conclusion of this combat, the candidate automatically gains **1 level of Exhaustion** and is stripped of all spell slots save one final 1st-level slot.

#### Stage III: The Soul-Mirror Dilemma
The Conclave’s masters weave an ultra-realistic illusion targeted at the candidate’s **Primal Wound** and **Core Lie**:
- For Raistlin: An illusion of Caramon casting spells effortlessly, proving that his beloved brother was secretly superior to him in magic, triggering Raistlin's murderous rage and burning his twin with illusory magic.
- *Tabletop Scenario:* The candidate is presented with a loved one, mentor, or helpless innocent who is being tortured or holding a key item. To pass, the candidate must choose whether to sacrifice their loved one to protect the Art, or abandon magic to save the mortal life.
- *The Judgment:* Hesitation or cowardly surrender results in immediate lethal injury from the surrounding testing constructs.

#### Stage IV: The Sovereign Resolution & Robe Assignment
Surviving candidates are brought before the **Council of the Three Robes** in the Chamber of the Great Eye:
- **White Robes ([[Solinari]]):** The candidate demonstrated self-sacrifice, mercy, and devotion to protecting the innocent.
- **Red Robes ([[Lunitari]]):** The candidate demonstrated pragmatism, intellectual curiosity, tactical adaptability, and dedication to the cosmic balance.
- **Black Robes ([[Nuitari]]):** The candidate demonstrated cold self-preservation, ruthless ambition, and willingness to sacrifice others for power.

## 7. The Renegade Status & Inquisitorial Execution Protocols

Any wizard who casts spells of 2nd level or higher and refuses to present themselves to the Tower of Wayreth within one lunar cycle of their summons is branded a **Renegade Mage**:
1. **The Brand of the Outlaw:** The Conclave issues a magical death writ. Renegades are denied sanctuary in any town, university, or guild hall across Ansalon.
2. **The Inquisitorial Hunters:** The Conclave dispatches a strike team comprising:
   - One White Robe abjurer (to counterspell and dispell wards).
   - One Red Robe transmuter (to lock terrain and neutralize escape).
   - One Black Robe assassin (to execute the renegade without trial).
3. **The No-Mercy Mandate:** Even White Robe mages participate in the execution of renegades with absolute calm; on Krynn, an untrained, undisciplined rogue wizard is viewed as an existential threat capable of tearing down city walls or summoning apocalyptic extraplanar entities.
