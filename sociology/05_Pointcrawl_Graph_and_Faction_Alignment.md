### 1. The Pointcrawl Sociological Mapping Principle

Every pointcrawl network—regardless of genre, tech level, or node count—can be systematically mapped to organizational sociology. By assigning each strategic node to a **Cultural Quadrant** (Clan, Adhocracy, Market, Hierarchy) and an **Operational Profile** (Deliver, Produce, Design, Service), the Game Master gains immediate, consistent intuition for how settlements govern themselves, react to crises, and enforce order.

            [GREEN / CLAN]           │           [BLUE-RED / ADHOCRACY]

      Ops: Service (Decentralized)   │              Ops: Design (Specialized)

            [WHITE / HIERARCHY]      │            [BLACK / MARKET]
         Fortified Bastions          │          Corporate Syndicates & Slag Mills

### 3. Step-by-Step Graph Alignment Workflow

1. **Classify Nodes by Elevation & Function:**
   * Perimeter / Gate Nodes $\rightarrow$ Typically **Hierarchy (White)** (must control traffic and quarantine threats).
   * Energy / Industrial Generation Nodes $\rightarrow$ Typically **Market / Adhocracy (Blue/Black)** (driven by technical skill and production quotas).
   * Wilderness / High Canopy Refuges $\rightarrow$ Typically **Clan (Green)** (survival depends on mutual trust and local ecology).
   * Maritime / Smuggling Anchors $\rightarrow$ Typically **Adhocracy / Clan (Red)** (informal contracts and swift adaptation).
2. **Assign Tension Edges:**
   * When an edge connects a **Hierarchy Hub** and a **Market Hub**, expect friction over tariffs, resource quotas, and legal authority.
   * When an edge connects a **Clan Hub** and a **Hierarchy Hub**, expect ideological tension over conscription, taxes, and boundary encroachments.
3. **Trigger Sociological Shifts During Play:**
   * **Sudden Crisis:** Under severe resource famine, stable Hierarchies may fragment into cutthroat Market competition or radical Adhocracies.
   * **External Siege:** When faced with an existential outside threat, feuding Clans and Adhocracies may accept the harsh shelter and discipline of a central Hierarchy.
