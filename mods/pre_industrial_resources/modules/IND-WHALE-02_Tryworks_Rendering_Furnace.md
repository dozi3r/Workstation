# Tryworks Rendering Furnace (`IND-WHALE-02`)
> **Industry Chain**: `CHAIN-WHALE` (Stage 2: `refining`)

## 1. Architectural Overview & Operational Loop
The tryworks facility houses double copper rendering vats set into massive brick masonry furnaces. Raw whale-blubber is diced and boiled relentlessly using wood-fuel and crackling scraps as secondary combustion fuel. The rendered whale-oil is skimmed, cooled, and funneled directly into cooperage casks for global export as lamp fuel and lubricant. The works consumes `Animal: Whale-Blubber`, `Fuel: Wood-Fuel`, `Timber: Cooperage-Casks`, `Metal: Forged-Copper` and `Chem: Pine-Pitch`.

Rendering is called "trying out" and works like rendering lard: strips are cut small, melted in the pots, and the solid residue removed when each pot is full. The oil is ladled into cold water to cool and purify before barreling; its color runs from honey yellow to dark brown with the state of the blubber. Shore stations built several fireboxes in a row backed by a heavy stone wall, with the cooper's house standing close above.
- **Firebox Row:** Brick fireboxes with a copper pot in each and a stone back wall.
- **Cooling Trough:** Cold water where hot oil is ladled to cool and purify.
- **Cooper's Shed:** Where casks are made, pitched and filled.
