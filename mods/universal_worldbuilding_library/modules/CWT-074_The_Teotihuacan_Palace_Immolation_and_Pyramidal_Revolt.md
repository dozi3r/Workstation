### 1. Mechanical Architecture & Archaeological Grounding
Flourishing between 100 BCE and 650 CE within the volcanic highland Basin of Mexico, Teotihuacan ("The City of the Gods") was the largest pre-Columbian urban metropolis in the Western Hemisphere, boasting a peak population between 100,000 and 125,000 inhabitants. Unlike the dynastic Maya city-states of the Petén lowlands dominated by individualized god-kings, monumental royal stelae, and personal funerary cults, Teotihuacan engineered an unprecedented, hyper-egalitarian corporate urban model. For centuries, this corporate order suppressed individual ruler glorification in favor of collective civic institutions, universal high-quality public housing, and an industrial-scale manufacturing monopoly over Mesoamerica's most vital strategic resource: **green Pachuca obsidian**.

However, between 550 and 650 CE, this egalitarian social pact suffered catastrophic systemic collapse. Exhaustive archaeological excavations and micro-stratigraphic forensic analyses (synthesized by Linda Manzanilla, René Millon, and George Cowgill) demonstrated that Teotihuacan did not fall to nomadic northern invaders (*Chichimeca*). Instead, the metropolis imploded through an internally coordinated, surgical populist revolution: the common citizens rose in armed rebellion to systematically de-sacralize, loot, and incinerate the seats of concentrated aristocratic authority, while leaving their own residential neighborhoods completely untouched.

The physical, industrial, and sociological systems of Teotihuacan rest upon four foundational realities:

1. **The Corporate Apartment Compound and Talud-Tablero Architecture**: Teotihuacan was organized not into sprawling palaces and squalid shantytowns, but into over 2,000 standardized, multi-family apartment compounds (*barrios*). Each compound housed 60 to 100 individuals organized around kinship, craft specialization (obsidian knapping, pottery, shell-working), and ritual altars, featuring plastered floors, private drainage, and multi-chambered living quarters. Public monumental structures—most notably the Pyramid of the Sun, the Pyramid of the Moon, and the Temple of the Feathered Serpent inside the sprawling Ciudadela plaza—were built in the distinctive **talud-tablero** style (sloping basal *talud* supporting a cantilevered framed panel *tablero*), plastered in bright red lime stucco and adorned with aquatic deities, storm serpents, and feathered fangs.
2. **The Green Pachuca Obsidian Industrial Monopoly**: Teotihuacan's immense economic power rested on its absolute control over the high-grade green obsidian mines of nearby Sierra de las Navajas (Pachuca). Controlled by elite state managers, thousands of master flintknappers processed millions of razor-sharp prismatic blades within specialized industrial workshops. These green obsidian blades were exported across Mesoamerica, fueling elite trade networks from the Gulf Coast to the Maya lowlands and Honduras, generating the immense wealth that financed the city's monumental architecture and imported exotic luxuries.
3. **Environmental Stress and the Tainter Sclerosis Trap**: By 550 CE, centuries of intensive stucco plaster production (which required burning millions of green oak trees to bake limestone mortar) and agricultural over-expansion created severe ecological degradation. Deforestation triggered severe soil erosion, declining agricultural yields in the valley's chinampa irrigation systems, and climatic drought. To maintain tribute flows and elite consumption in the face of diminishing returns, the ruling hierarchy imposed heavier taxes, restricted obsidian workshops, and attempted to transition from corporate egalitarianism to a centralized monarchy. This provoked profound ideological betrayal among the commoner compound residents.
4. **The Selective Palace Immolation and Systematic De-Sacralization**: The revolution of 650 CE was characterized by chillingly deliberate architectural violence. Insurgents did not sack random houses or burn commoner barrios; instead, they formed coordinated militias that marched directly down the 2.5-kilometer Avenue of the Dead to target exclusively the seats of state power. Administrative palaces, elite temples, and sculpture facades were systematically smashed, defaced, and put to the torch. Basalt serpent heads were hurled from pyramidal platforms, stone statues of ruling figures were shattered into unrecognizability, and palace roofs were collapsed in roaring conflagrations. Once the state apparatus was incinerated, the aristocracy vanished, and the 100,000-citizen metropolis peacefully de-urbanized, splitting into autonomous regional polities over the following two generations.

### 2. Thematic Premise & Syllogism

* **The Stasis / The Lie:** "Our city is the eternal center of the cosmos, bound together by equal housing, sacred red stucco walls, and the divine monopoly of green obsidian traded across all lands."
* **The Crucible / Conflict:** Severe drought and ecological degradation drive the ruling priesthood to abandon corporate egalitarianism and tax the apartment compounds into starvation; in response, thousands of commoner artisans march down the Avenue of the Dead with obsidian spears and pine torches, systematically burning the palaces to the ground while sparing their own neighborhoods.
* **Dramatic Syllogism:**  
  $$\text{Elite Over-Extraction \& Ideological Betrayal} + \text{Coordinated Urban Insurgency} = \text{Selective Palatial Immolation \& Corporate De-Sacralization}$$
* **The Truth / Synthesis:** An egalitarian urban contract, once broken by elite tyranny and ecological collapse, invites its own targeted destruction. When the heart of an empire becomes a cage for its people, the people will burn the palace to free the city.

* **Corner 1: The Establishment (The Ciudadela High Priest):**  
  The senior administrative potentate commanding the Temple of the Feathered Serpent. Clad in resplendent green quetzal-feather headdresses and jade ear-spools, he views the rising popular unrest as demonic insolence. Desperate to salvage the collapsing tribute economy, he militarizes the Avenue of the Dead with mercenary guards and attempts to consolidate absolute monarchical power over the independent apartment compounds.
* **Corner 2: The Zealot Opponent (The Barrio Insurgent Leader):**  
  A master obsidian knapper and militia commander from the San Juan multi-family compound. Having watched his fellow artisans starve while elite storehouses brimmed with maize and jade, he organizes a clandestine revolutionary network across twenty residential barrios. Armed with razor-sharp green obsidian blades and pine-pitch torches, he leads the surgical assault that reduces the Avenue of the Dead to smoking ruins.
* **Corner 3: The Thematic Shadow (The Obsidian Smuggler Cartel):**  
  A syndicate of rogue merchants and corrupt workshop foremen who established clandestine obsidian-working camps in the barrancas outside the city walls. Exploiting the revolutionary chaos, they bypass state monopolies to sell high-grade green obsidian blades directly to insurgent militias and rival highland polities, making immense profits off the city's self-destruction.
* **Corner 4: The Tertiary Outcast (The De-Urbanized Migration Scout):**  
  A quiet pragmatist who abandoned compound politics early, establishing scouting trails through the volcanic hills surrounding the dying metropolis. Rejecting both the dying priesthood and the fiery chaos of the insurrection, she guides exhausted families away from the burning ruins, leading them toward new agrarian settlements around Lake Xochimilco and Tula.

### 4. Injected Tabletop Mechanics & Runtime Primitives

#### 4.1 4-Level Dynamic Dial: Insurgency & Palatial Conflagration
The political tension, civil unrest, and architectural destruction of Teotihuacan are tracked along a 4-level dial strictly calibrated to Universal Hazard Tiers:

* **Level 1 (Compound Plaster Rationing & Obsidian Export Tax / Light Severity Tier - DC 10):**  
  * *Condition:* State lime plaster distribution is restricted; guards levy heavy transit taxes on green obsidian shipments entering barrios; market discontent simbers among artisans.
  * *Mechanics:* Negotiating trade tariffs or navigating bureaucratic checkpoints along the Avenue of the Dead requires a **DC 10 Haggling or Bureaucracy check**; failure incurs minor financial penalties or delays.
* **Level 2 (Talud-Tablero Temple Barricades & Street Clashes / Moderate Severity Tier - DC 13, 2d8 damage):**  
  * *Condition:* Elite mercenary patrols clash with compound militias in the open plazas; the Ciudadela gates are barricaded with basalt blocks; fires break out in administrative storehouses.
  * *Mechanics:* Traversing contested plazas or dodging street skirmishes requires a **DC 13 Reflex or Athletics check**; failure inflicts **2d8 bludgeoning damage** from flying stones and stampeding crowds, or 1 level of Exhaustion from blocked transit routes.
* **Level 3 (Avenue of the Dead Palatial Conflagration / Severe Severity Tier - DC 16, 4d10 damage):**  
  * *Condition:* Coordinated barrio militias hurl pine-pitch torches into administrative palaces along the Avenue of the Dead; painted stucco walls collapse in roaring fires; shattered god effigies litter the smoking stone terraces.
  * *Mechanics:* Navigating burning palatial complexes or escaping collapsing roof beams requires a **DC 16 Reflex or Fortitude check**; failure inflicts **4d10 fire and suffocation damage** and temporary blindness from toxic stucco smoke.
* **Level 4 (Total De-Sacralization & Mass Compound Exodus / Lethal Climax Tier - DC 20, 8d12 damage):**  
  * *Condition:* The aristocracy is thoroughly annihilated; monumental statues are smashed to dust; the 100,000-citizen metropolis is systematically abandoned as populations disperse into autonomous regional polities.
  * *Mechanics:* Absolute political and urban dissolution. Remaining inside un-defended palace ruins exposes characters to violent insurgent purging: surviving a night in the burning city requires a **DC 20 Survival check**, failing which inflicts **8d12 violent riot damage** and forces immediate flight from the valley.

#### 4.2 Segmented Threat Clocks

```
[ ][ ][ ][ ]          Palatial Selective Immolation Clock (4 Ticks)
[ ][ ][ ][ ][ ][ ]    Aristocratic Monarchy Consolidation Meter (6 Ticks)
```

* **Palatial Selective Immolation Clock [4 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks forward (+1) each time aristocrats execute barrio delegates, royal guards seize artisan food rations, or populist anger boils over into targeted compound raids.
  * *Escalation Events:*  
    * *Tick 1:* The administrative palace of Tetitla is attacked by torch-wielding artisans; records rooms are put to the torch.
    * *Tick 2:* The Jaguar Palace along the Avenue of the Dead is breached and systematically gutted from floor to ceiling.
    * *Tick 3:* The high priests barricade themselves inside the Sun Pyramid precinct as thousands of armed commoners surround the base.
  * *Consequence when full (4/4):* Triggers **Dial Level 3: Avenue of the Dead Palatial Conflagration** immediately. All major administrative palaces are simultaneously torched in a surgical revolution.
  * *Player Mitigation:* Negotiating compromise tribute relief, smuggling food into starving barrios, or assassinating corrupt palace tax collectors (**DC 16 Diplomacy or Stealth check**) clears 1 tick.
* **Aristocratic Monarchy Consolidation Meter [6 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks forward (+1) when the priesthood successfully levies emergency obsidian taxes, mercenary battalions reinforce the Ciudadela, or rebel leaders are publicly executed in the Plaza of the Moon.
  * *Escalation Events:*  
    * *Tick 2:* All independent obsidian workshops are nationalized under direct palatial guard control.
    * *Tick 4:* Martial law is declared across the Avenue of the Dead; curfew violations are punished by summary execution.
  * *Consequence when full (6/6):* The priesthood successfully crushes corporate egalitarianism, establishing an autocratic monarchy and plunging the city into brutal civil tyranny.
  * *Player Mitigation:* Sabotaging military armories, arming barrio militias, or leaking palace corruption documents to the public (**DC 16 Thieves' Tools or Persuasion check**) clears 1 tick.

  * `Agriculture: Valley-Chinampa-Irrigation`: Intensive wetland agricultural systems (*chinampas*) providing the caloric surplus necessary to sustain 100,000 non-farming urban residents.
* **Output Yields (`provides`):**
  * `Structure: Talud-Tablero-Pyramidal-Citadel`: Monumental architectural engineering standards defining sacred urban geography across Mesoamerica.
  * `Industry: Green-Pachuca-Obsidian-Blade-Cores`: Industrial-scale high-grade prismatic obsidian blades serving as the primary cutting tool and economic currency of the hemisphere.
  * `Tension: Selective-Palatial-Immolation`: A socio-political forensic blueprint for dismantling totalitarian state structures through targeted popular uprising.
* **Yields Resource (`yields_resource`):**
  * `Talud-Tablero Pyramidal Architecture`: Master engineering knowledge for constructing earthquake-resistant stepped pyramids and plastered plazas.
  * `Green Pachuca Prismatic Obsidian Cores`: Pervasive cutting tools and trade currency capable of penetrating armor and cutting hides with absolute precision.
  * `Selective Burn Forensic Horizon`: Historical precedent and tactical framework for executing surgical, anti-oligarchic revolutions without destroying foundational commoner infrastructure.
* **Standalone Fallback Mode:**
  * When administrative palaces along the Avenue of the Dead are incinerated, the module enters **Autonomous Barrio De-Urbanization**:
    * Centralized tribute collection and state-managed obsidian monopolies vanish permanently.
    * Commoner apartment compounds self-govern autonomously for two generations, maintaining local craft traditions without central rulers.
    * The regional population peacefully disperses across Central Mexico, founding new independent highland polities (such as Xochicalco, Cacaxtla, and Tula).

### 5. Scene Calculus & Tactical Spatial Encounters

* **Encounter 1: The Obsidian Toll Gate on the Avenue of the Dead (Arrival Collision)**
  * *Collision ($><$):* Arriving at the northern gateway of the Avenue of the Dead, the party attempts to transport a pack mule laden with trade goods into the city. A squad of mercenary palace guards clad in feathered armor halts them, demanding an exorbitant 50% "Sovereign Preservation Tax" in green obsidian blade cores. Before negotiations can conclude, a mob of fifty angry artisan stonemasons surges from a side alley, hurling basalt slingshots at the guards and screaming revolutionary slogans.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* The party expects to pay standard customs duties, but is instantly caught in the crossfire of an exploding urban insurrection, forced to choose between defending tyrannical state tax collectors or siding with armed populist insurgents.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The palace captain demands absolute submission in the name of the divine cosmic order, but his sandals are caked in charcoal dust and soot from an arson attack he barely survived in the neighboring barrio this morning.
* **Scene 2: The Firebrand in the Mural Courtyard (Resource Dilemma)**
  * *Collision ($><$):* Inside a burning administrative palace off the Avenue of the Dead, compound rebels have cornered a dozen teenage scribes and are pouring flammable ocote pine pitch over the walls to incinerate a vast archive of painted deerskin and amatl bark-paper tax rolls, debt registers, and trade agreements. The rebels view these scrolls as instruments of their generational debt bondage.
  * *The Dilemma:*
    * *Save the Archives:* The party can battle the impassioned populist rebels to rescue the bark-paper codices (preserving the irreplaceable written history, astronomical charts, and architectural blueprints of the greatest metropolis in the Americas), but doing so alienates the commoner compounds and marks the party as defenders of elite tyranny.
    * *Let the Fire Burn:* Standing aside ensures safe alliance with the barrio militia, but destroys the historical memory of the civilization forever, leaving future generations with no record of who built the pyramids.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The militia leader shouts that no child should grow up reading the debt-titles that sold their parents into the quarries, holding the roaring pine torch inches from the painted genealogies while a weeping apprentice scribe clutches a pot of cinnabar ink to his chest.
* **Scene 3: The Toppling of the Feathered Serpent (The Severance Crisis)**
  * *Collision ($><$):* At the summit platform of the Temple of the Feathered Serpent inside the Ciudadela, populist insurgents have looped thick maguey-fiber hauling ropes around a 4-ton basalt serpent head jutting from the *tablero* facade. Fifty warriors heave in unison to topple the stone monster down the steep 45-degree stairway into the sunken courtyard below, where barricaded palace nobles and their families are trapped.
  * *The Dilemma:*
    * *Sever the Ropes:* Intervening to cut the hauling ropes saves the trapped patricians from being crushed by the rolling monolith, but risks the party being thrown down the 60-meter pyramidal steps by the furious crowd.
    * *Aid the Toppling:* Helping the insurgents heave the monolith smashes the courtyard gates, allowing the mob to butcher the aristocracy and opening a path to the secret subterranean offering vault beneath the pyramid, packed with pristine green Pachuca blade cores and sacred jade.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* As the 4-ton stone effigy groans and cracks against its mortar, the air vibrates with rhythmic chanting from ten thousand commoners packing the plaza below, marking the exact acoustic second that an empire dies and freedom is reclaimed.
