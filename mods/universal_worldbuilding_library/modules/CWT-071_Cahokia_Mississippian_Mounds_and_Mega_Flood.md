### 1. Mechanical Architecture & Alluvial Hydrogeological Grounding
Drawing from the urban metropolis of Cahokia in the American Bottom floodplain of the Mississippi River (flourishing c. 1050–1250 CE opposite modern St. Louis), this module models the apex pre-Columbian urban civilization north of the Rio Grande. Spanning over 16 square kilometers and supporting an estimated population of 20,000 to 40,000 citizens—surpassing contemporary London or Paris—Cahokia was an astonishing theocratic state founded upon intensive flint maize monoculture, cosmic solar architecture, and colossal earthen engineering. Modern archaeological research, led by Timothy Pauketat, Thomas Emerson, and Samuel Munoz, reveals both the scale of Cahokia’s monumental achievements and the severe environmental tipping points that triggered its rapid collapse.

The physical, hydrogeological, and monumental systems of Cahokia rest upon four foundational realities:

1. **Monks Mound and the Colossal Earthen Pyramid**: Dominating the site is Monks Mound, the largest pre-Columbian earthen structure in the Americas north of Mexico. Rising 30 meters high across four massive terraces, the mound covers 14 acres at its base and contains over 22 million cubic feet of earth. Built entirely by human labor without beasts of burden or wheeled carts, construction required workers to fill woven baskets with local silty clay, loess, and sand, hoisting them up wooden ramps on their backs. This staggering civilizational effort was governed by theocratic coercion, religious ideology, and elite redistribution of maize harvests. However, because the mound was constructed by piling contrasting loads of wet and dry soils without stabilizing stone revetments, it was inherently susceptible to internal geotechnical failure when saturated by torrential rains.
2. **The Deforestation of Upland Bluffs and Silt Runoff**: To fuel the megalithic construction projects, stoke elite feasting hearths, and erect the massive defensive palisade walls, Cahokia's labor corps clear-cut thousands of acres of upland oak and hickory forests on the adjacent bluffs. This aggressive deforestation triggered severe environmental feedback loops: rainwater rushed unchecked down the bluff slopes, eroding the fragile loess topsoil and washing millions of tons of muddy silt down into the American Bottom floodplain. This accelerated sedimentation choked natural stream channels, raised riverbeds, and transformed shallow backwater lakes into stagnant, mosquito-infested sloughs that compromised public health and drowned adjacent maize fields.
3. **The Palisade Wall and Mass Retainer Sacrifices**: As population density peaked and climate instability generated social friction, Cahokia's ruling elites constructed an imposing, two-mile-long fortification wall (*stockade*) featuring bastions every 80 feet, requiring the felling of over 20,000 mature oak logs. Rebuilt three times over a century, the wall served a dual purpose: defending the sacred precinct from external raiders and coercing the subject populace. Excavations of Mound 72 revealed horrifying evidence of state terror and cosmic propitiation: mass graves containing over 270 individuals, including execution pits of dozens of young women ritually strangled as retainers for an elite chieftain laid out upon a massive beaded cape fashioned from twenty thousand marine shell beads imported from the Gulf Coast.
4. **The Mississippi Mega-Flood and Socio-Environmental Collapse**: Paleoclimatic sediment cores extracted from backwater lakes in the American Bottom reveal that Cahokia's collapse coincided with a catastrophic cluster of environmental shocks. Between 1150 and 1250 CE, severe multi-year droughts stunted maize harvests, immediately followed by unprecedented multi-year mega-floods. Swollen by torrential spring rains and upper-basin snowmelts, the Mississippi River burst its natural levees, sending a 10-meter wall of water across the alluvial plain. The floodwaters drowned the low-lying maize fields, saturated the foundations of Monks Mound until its clay terraces slumped in massive rotational mudslides, and rotted the ground-line footings of the defensive palisade. Starving commoners, disillusioned by the failing divine kingship of the "Great Sun," rioted, burned the palisade gates, and abandoned the ruined metropolis, scattering into autonomous riverine tribal bands across the Mississippi and Ohio valleys.

### 2. Thematic Premise & Syllogism

* **The Stasis / The Lie:** "Our Great Sun king commands the movements of the sun through the cedar posts of Woodhenge; as long as we offer basket-loads of earth to Monks Mound and blood to the elite ancestors, the river will nourish our maize and shield our city from harm."
* **The Crucible / Conflict:** Relentless upland deforestation chokes the floodplain with silt while multi-year mega-floods breach the natural levees, drowning the maize fields in stagnant water; as starving commoners riot and refuse further retainer sacrifices, the priesthood watches in horror as Monks Mound begins to slump in a massive mudslide.
* **Dramatic Syllogism:**  
  $$\text{Theocratic Deforestation Silt Runoff} + \text{Mississippi Mega-Flood Inundation} = \text{Earthen Mound Slumping \& Urban Secession}$$
* **The Truth / Synthesis:** An empire built upon an engineered floodplain and sustained by ecological over-extraction cannot survive when the river reclaims its basin. Monumental pyramids of mud cannot hold back the rising tide of ecological reckoning when the soil itself gives way.

* **Corner 1: The Establishment (The Great Sun Sovereign):**  
  The absolute theocratic ruler of Cahokia, dwelling within a towering cedar temple on the uppermost summit of Monks Mound. Adorned in feather cloaks and sacred copper breastplates, he views the metropolis as the exact center of the cosmos. When the spring floods rise and maize granaries rot, he orders increased labor levies to raise Monks Mound higher and commands public executions in Mound 72 to appease angry sky spirits, utterly blind to the systemic collapse of his agrarian base.
* **Corner 2: The Zealot Opponent (The Palisade Timber Sapper):**  
  A disillusioned master carpenter and former militia commander who directed the construction of the defensive palisade walls. Having watched his own family starve while elite priests feasted on venison and maize beer atop the mounds, he turns his engineering expertise against the state. Leading an armed faction of commoners, he sets fire to the rotted timber curtain walls, breaches the elite compound, and drags corrupt tribute collectors into the rising floodwaters.
* **Corner 3: The Thematic Shadow (The Cahokia Shell-Bead Merchant):**  
  An opportunistic elite trader who controls the dry storage warehouses on the upper bluff terrace. Sensing the impending ecological collapse years before the commoners, he hoarded vast quantities of Gulf Coast marine shell beads, copper plates, and dried maize in elevated dry silos. Operating a lucrative black market during the famine, he trades scarce rations to desperate nobles in exchange for ancestral land titles and enslaved artisans.
* **Corner 4: The Tertiary Outcast (The Riverboat Silt-Nomad Outcast):**  
  A scarred wilderness hunter who abandoned the urban core to live aboard cypress dugout canoes navigating the shifting backwater sloughs of the Mississippi. Rejecting both the tyranny of the mound-builders and the violence of the palisade rioters, she guides displaced refugee families to safety on the remote upland bluffs, demonstrating that human survival requires adaptation to the dynamic river rather than futile attempts to cage it beneath artificial mountains of mud.

### 4. Injected Tabletop Mechanics & Runtime Primitives

#### 4.1 4-Level Dynamic Dial: Alluvial Flooding & Monumental Collapse
The hydrological stability, structural integrity, and civil order of the metropolitan basin are tracked along a 4-level dial strictly calibrated to Universal Hazard Tiers:

* **Level 1 (Creek Backwater Swell & Palisade Rot / Light Severity Tier - DC 10):**  
  * *Condition:* Heavy spring rains saturate the alluvial floodplain; Cahokia Creek runs swollen and turbid; ground-line oak palisade timbers begin to soften with fungal rot; commoners mutter about corrupt tribute demands.
  * *Mechanics:* Traversing waterlogged plaza mud or inspecting slippery palisade scaffolding requires a **DC 10 Acrobatics or Survival check**; failure results in awkward slips, minor gear fouling, and accumulated fatigue. Food rations stored in damp ground pits spoil, reducing party inventory by 20%.
* **Level 2 (Levee Slumping & Silt-Drowned Maize Fields / Moderate Severity Tier - DC 13, 2d8 damage):**  
  * *Condition:* The Mississippi River crests at record heights; natural silt levees begin to slough into the current; thousands of acres of low-lying maize fields drown in stagnant, silt-laden backwaters; food rations are slashed by half.
  * *Mechanics:* Moving across flooded agricultural bottoms or navigating swamped causeways requires **DC 13 Athletics or Land Vehicles checks**; failure traps characters in waist-deep sucking mud, inflicting **2d8 bludgeoning damage** and 1 level of Exhaustion from strenuous wading. Negotiating with starving commoners requires a **DC 13 Persuasion or Intimidation check** to avoid food riots.
* **Level 3 (Mega-Flood Breach & Palisade Bastion Inferno / Severe Severity Tier - DC 16, 4d10 damage):**  
  * *Condition:* A 10-meter flood surge smashes through riverbank levees, inundating the lower urban districts with rushing brown water; rebel commoners hurl pitch-soaked torches, setting fire to the two-mile oak palisade wall and trapping panicked residents.
  * *Mechanics:* Surviving the roaring flood breach and burning wall complex requires a **DC 16 Fortitude or Reflex check**; failure inflicts **4d10 drowning, crushing, or fire damage** and sweeps characters 300 feet into submerged debris fields. Escaping collapsing wooden bastions requires immediate strength and swimming coordination.
* **Level 4 (Monks Mound Rotational Slump & Total Urban Desertion / Lethal Climax Tier - DC 20, 8d12 damage):**  
  * *Condition:* Saturated silty clay triggers a catastrophic rotational slope failure of Monks Mound; the Great Sun’s summit temple slides into the plaza below; the 40,000-citizen metropolis fragments into fleeing bands as ruins sink into permanent backwater marsh.
  * *Mechanics:* Complete urban dissolution and geotechnical disaster. Navigating the collapsing terraces of Monks Mound requires a **DC 20 Athletics or Survival check**; failure subjects characters to a massive mudslide and tumbling masonry, inflicting **8d12 lethal crushing and mudslide damage**. The metropolis is permanently abandoned, converting the once-thriving civic heartland into an uninhabitable wilderness swamp.

#### 4.2 Segmented Threat Clocks

```
[ ][ ][ ][ ][ ][ ] Mississippi Alluvial Mega-Flood Meter (6 Ticks)
[ ][ ][ ][ ]       Palisade Conflagration & Mutiny Clock (4 Ticks)
```

* **Mississippi Alluvial Mega-Flood Meter [6 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks forward (+1) each season torrential rains continue, upstream snowmelt swells the river basin, or upland deforestation silt runs unchecked into tributary channels.
  * *Escalation Events:*  
    * *Tick 2:* Cahokia Creek overflows its banks, flooding the grand plaza and rotting stored maize in subterranean pits.
    * *Tick 4:* Natural river levees fracture in three places; rushing water isolates Monks Mound from the residential suburbs.
  * *Consequence when full (6/6):* The Mississippi River unleashes a catastrophic mega-flood, immediately triggering **Dial Level 3: Mega-Flood Breach & Palisade Bastion Inferno**.
  * *Player Mitigation:* Digging diversion canals, reinforcing earthen levees, or dredging choked silt channels (**DC 16 Athletics or Engineering check**) clears 1 tick.
* **Palisade Conflagration & Mutiny Clock [4 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks forward (+1) whenever sun priests demand human retainer sacrifices during famine, guards seize food stores from commoner quarters, or floodwaters destroy public granaries.
  * *Escalation Events:*  
    * *Tick 1:* Commoners scrawl anti-priestly graffiti on timber bastions and refuse to carry basket-loads of earth to Monks Mound.
    * *Tick 2:* Armed rioters clash with royal guards at the sacred precinct gate, leaving twelve dead in the plaza mud.
    * *Tick 3:* The Palisade Timber Sapper organizes a systematic underground sabotage campaign, greasing and weakening key structural supports of the eastern wall.
  * *Consequence when full (4/4):* Total social revolution. Commoners set the 20,000-log oak palisade ablaze, storm the elite compounds, and drag the priesthood into the floodwaters, triggering **Dial Level 4: Monks Mound Rotational Slump**.
  * *Player Mitigation:* Distributing emergency grain reserves, negotiating prisoner releases, or executing corrupt tax collectors (**DC 16 Diplomacy or Insight check**) clears 1 tick.

#### 4.3 Logistical & Resource Flow Contract

* **Input Prerequisites (`requires`):**
  * `Labor: Basket-Carried-Alluvial-Earth`: Millions of woven baskets filled with silty clay, loess, and sand, hoisted on human backs to construct monumental mounds, plazas, and defensive earthworks.
  * `Forest: Upland-Oak-Palisade-Timber`: Vast stands of mature upland oak and hickory timber clear-cut from nearby bluffs to erect defensive palisades, fuel elite feasting hearths, and construct temple substructures.
* **Output Yields (`provides`):**
  * `Agriculture: Intensive-Floodplain-Maize`: High-yield flint maize harvests grown across fertile alluvial bottomlands, sustaining tens of thousands of urban inhabitants.
  * `Structure: Monumental-Earthen-Monks-Mound`: A staggering 30-meter-high earthen pyramid complex anchoring divine kingship, astronomical tracking, and theological legitimacy.
  * `Ritual: Shell-Bead-Cosmic-Sacrifice`: Elaborate mortuary complexes and long-distance trade networks centered on marine shell regalia, copper plates, and ritual retainer sacrifices.
* **Yields Resource (`yields_resource`):**
  * `Intensive Floodplain Maize Surplus`: High-calorie grain stores capable of sustaining large urban workforces or financing regional trade expeditions.
  * `Monumental Earthen Pyramid Bastions`: Massive platform mounds and fortified timber walls providing impregnable redoubts against tribal raiders.
  * `Sacred Shell-Bead Regalia`: Exquisite marine shell capes and carved gorgets conferring immense spiritual authority and diplomatic prestige across the continent.
* **Standalone Fallback Mode:**
  * When mega-floods drown the alluvial bottom and commoners burn the palisade, the module enters **Riverine Tribal Dispersal**:
    * Divine kingship collapses permanently; Monks Mound is abandoned to backwater swamps and migratory wading birds.
    * The centralized metropolis shatters into autonomous riverine tribal bands (*Illiniwek, Osage, Quapaw*) practicing decentralized swidden agriculture and mobile hunting.
    * Monumental earthwork construction ceases entirely across the Mississippi Valley, replaced by egalitarian clan structures and flexible river-canoe trade networks.

### 5. Scene Calculus & Tactical Spatial Encounters

* **Encounter 1: The Sinking Basket-Ramp Collapse (Arrival Collision)**
  * *Collision ($><$):* Arriving at the base of Monks Mound to trade or secure an audience with the priesthood, the party finds the colossal earthen pyramid under frantic emergency repair. Heavy spring rains have liquefied the outer clay cladding. As hundreds of commoners stagger up the steep wooden ramps carrying heavy basket-loads of silt, the waterlogged retaining terrace shears away with a deafening sucking sound. An avalanche of muddy earth and heavy wicker baskets crashes down the slope, threatening to bury a sacred procession of sun priests and the party.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* The party approaches an awe-inspiring center of imperial civilization, but is instantly plunged into a frantic geotechnical rescue operation amid slipping mud and screaming laborers.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* An elite overseer whips exhausted commoners to force them back onto the slipping slope, screaming that the mudslide is divine punishment for insufficient maize tribute, while a dying laborer whispers that the mound is drowning because it is built upon stolen river silt.
  * *Tactical Resolution:* Characters must coordinate a **DC 13 Athletics or Survival check** to pull trapped workers from the mudslide and shore up slipping timber shoring before secondary slope failures inflict **2d8 bludgeoning damage**.
* **Encounter 2: The Mound 72 Retainer Dilemma (Moral Crisis / Theocratic Severance)**
  * *Collision ($><$):* Seeking safe passage or diplomatic credentials during the height of the food crisis, the party is summoned to the summit temple of Monks Mound by the Great Sun. He offers the seals of passage on one terrifying condition: the party must assist temple guards in rounding up twelve orphaned commoner children from the flooded suburbs to serve as human retainers in a live burial ritual within Mound 72 to appease the rain spirits.
  * *The Dilemma:*
    * *Comply with the Sacrifice:* Assisting the priesthood secures the official diplomatic seals immediately, granting the party unhindered travel through all allied river chiefdoms. However, participating in human sacrifice inflicts permanent moral corruption and guarantees a violent confrontation with the Palisade Timber Sapper's rebel faction.
    * *Refuse and Defy the Priesthood:* Refusing the decree brands the party as blasphemous outlaws. The Great Sun orders elite archers to execute them on the spot, triggering an immediate rooftop combat encounter against thirty temple guards atop Monks Mound (**DC 16 combat challenge**).
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* One of the chosen children presses a string of cheap freshwater pearl beads into a character's hand, whispering that her older brother is down at the palisade with a torch and a hatchet.
* **Encounter 3: The Palisade Wall Inferno (Escape Collision / Outcast Rescue)**
  * *Collision ($><$):* Mutinous warriors led by the Palisade Timber Sapper have ignited the 20-foot-tall oak logs of the Eastern Palisade with pitch-soaked reed torches. The blaze has trapped a squad of royal archers and forty civilian artisans inside a three-story wooden bastion tower, while floodwaters 6 feet deep surround the burning curtain wall.
  * *Tactical Resolution & Outcast Sanctuary:* The party must navigate the inferno—either scaling the burning timber scaffolding (**DC 16 Reflex/Athletics**) to rig lowering ropes for the trapped civilians, or engaging the Sapper’s skirmishers who are spearing survivors as they leap into the swirling water. As the bastion collapses with a roar of embers, the Riverboat Silt-Nomad Outcast arrives alongside the wall in massive cypress dugout canoes, offering to pull the party and surviving refugees aboard and paddle through a drowned palisade gap into the safety of the open river.
