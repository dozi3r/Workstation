# MODULE HIST-01: THE CROSSING OF THE RUBICON & THE SENATE'S FLIGHT

### 1. Mechanical Architecture & Core Premise
In January of 49 BC, the constitutional machinery of the Roman Republic breaks down under the weight of an unresolvable legal paradox. Proconsul Gaius Julius Caesar stands at the northern boundary of Italy—the shallow stream of the Rubicon—with a single hardened legion, the XIII Gemina. To cross in arms is treason; to disband is political destruction and criminal prosecution by his factional enemies in the Senate. 

In Rome, the Senate passes the *Senatus Consultum Ultimum* (the ultimate decree of state emergency), stripping the tribunes of the plebs of their sacrosanct protections. The pointcrawl network is split along the axis of the Apennine spine: Caesar controls the transit speeds of the Via Flaminia and Cisalpine recruitment corridors, while Pompeius Magnus and the senatorial Optimates command the maritime anchor of Brundisium and the provincial grain supplies of the East.

* **The Physical Anchor (The Curia Hostilia & The Aerarium Saturni):** The Roman Senate house and the subterranean Temple of Saturn housing the Republic's emergency reserve gold (*aerarium sanctius*). When the Senate flees, the consuls forget the key to the vault, leaving the lifeblood of the empire unguarded.
* **The Dynamic Engine at Stake:** The module governs constitutional legitimacy versus martial momentum. Every day Caesar advances down the Adriatic coast without battle, Italian towns open their gates to him, flipping neutral bastions into Caesarian supply nodes.
* **The Escalation Trigger:** If the *Caesar's Rapid Column Advance* clock fills [4 Ticks], the XIII Legion reaches Ariminum and Picenum before Pompey can organize Italian levies, triggering the total panic evacuation of Rome.

### 2. Dramatic Opposition Web (4-Corner Matrix)

* **1. Establishment / Hegemony (The Senatorial Optimates & Pompeius Magnus):**
  * *Worldview & Goal:* Defend the aristocratic Republic, ancestral senatorial privilege (*mos maiorum*), and the rule of law against military tyranny.
  * *Leverage:* Legal constitutional authority, personal client-kingdom fleets across Greece and Asia, two veteran Spanish armies.
  * *Blind Spot / Fatal Flaw:* Paralyzed by procedural legalism, slow to mobilize, deeply divided between hardline aristocrats (Cato, Metellus Scipio) and military realists (Pompey).

* **2. Main Opponent / Challenger (Gaius Julius Caesar & Legio XIII Gemina):**
  * *Worldview & Goal:* Protect personal aristocratic honor (*dignitas*), champion the rights of the plebeian tribunes, and force a negotiated settlement from a position of absolute martial speed.
  * *Leverage:* Fanatically loyal combat veterans, rapid forced-march mobility (cavalry moving 50 miles per day), overwhelming populist appeal in the Italian municipalities.
  * *Blind Spot / Fatal Flaw:* Woefully outnumbered in long-term manpower; lacks naval transport; deeply exposed to supply line severance if Italian towns resist.

* **3. Thematic Shadow / Profiteer (The Publicani Equestrian Tax-Farming Syndicates):**
  * *Worldview & Goal:* Maximize financial returns on provincial tax contracts, hoard silver and gold coin, and hedge loans between Caesar and Pompey to secure shipping monopolies.
  * *Leverage:* Control of the private courier networks, vast stockpiles of grain in Ostia warehouses, ability to fund emergency mercenary cohorts.
  * *Blind Spot / Fatal Flaw:* Vulnerable to asset seizure; panic easily during bank runs; despised by both the senate nobility and the street mobs.

* **4. Tertiary Outcast / Victim (The Disenfranchised Plebeians & Enslaved Laborers):**
  * *Worldview & Goal:* Secure affordable grain rations, escape debt slavery, and survive the impending street battles between private patrician gladiator gangs.
  * *Leverage:* Overwhelming numerical presence in the slums of the Subura; ability to block city gates with burning carts; vital intelligence on patrician escape plans.
  * *Blind Spot / Fatal Flaw:* Starving, unarmored, easily manipulated by demagogues, completely vulnerable to food blockades.

### 3. GM Placement & Tactical Synergies

* **Recommended Placement:** Placed on **Node 1 (Sovereign Hub - Imperial Capital)** or as the overarching **Tier 1 Macro Governance Rule** spanning the entire campaign graph.
* **Supply Chain Interlocks:**
  * *Consumes:* `Material: Cisalpine-Silver-Denarii` and `Authority: Tribunician-Sacrosanct-Veto`.
  * *Feeds:* Yields `Authority: Consular-Imperium-Warrant` to loyal military outposts, and `Power: Mediterranean-Grain-Tithe` to allied urban bastions. If downstream shipping at Node 20 (Maritime Salvage / Brundisium) is blockaded, the capital enters immediate grain famine.
* **Arrival / Disembark Integration:**
  * *Upright Boon (The Chariot):* Imperial Dispatch Courier Pass—the party receives requisition horses, rapid transit through town gates, and a military travel visa.
  * *Reversed Debt (The Tower):* Proscription Suspicion—the party is stopped at a military barricade, accused of being Caesarian spies or Optimate defectors, and their baggage is searched for treasonous dispatches.
* **Standalone Fallback:** When played in isolation as a tactical historical one-shot, the party must choose whether to help the tribunes smuggle the Temple of Saturn gold out of Rome, or help Pompey’s rearguard hold the Milvian Bridge long enough to evacuate the consular archives.
