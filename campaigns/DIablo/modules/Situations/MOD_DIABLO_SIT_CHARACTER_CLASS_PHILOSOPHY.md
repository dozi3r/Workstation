# MODULE MOD_DIABLO_SIT_CHARACTER_CLASS_PHILOSOPHY: Character Class Philosophy

The guide's view that the three hero classes are archetypes, that the Sorcerer is the pivot point of character abilities, and that players should build a primary and a secondary configuration instead of specializing narrowly.

### Dramatic Premise
Heroes who lean on a single proven style + foes that shrug it off = a lesson in cultivating a second approach.

### Core Premise
The guide treats the three classes as archetypes, with Sorcery as the pivot point of character abilities. Any class can develop the strengths of the others to a lesser extent. Heroes should keep a primary and a secondary configuration and avoid pouring everything into a couple of traits. Warrior and Rogue are alike in purely non-magical combat, so both should cultivate a magical attack.

### GM Placement & Synergies
Place early in a campaign as a mentor scene or between delves, then reinforce it with an encounter that punishes narrow builds. It consumes a quiet moment and feeds the Magical Resistance situation, since resistant foes are exactly where a secondary configuration matters. Arrival goes well if heroes diversify and test a backup. It goes badly if they cling to one method and meet an enemy that renders it useless.

### 5D Sensory Pentagon
- **Physical environment:** A hero's gear, skills and spells laid out side by side, reflecting a primary and a secondary configuration.
- **Behaviors:** Players experiment outside their comfort class and plan a fallback.
- **Relationships:** Three archetypes, Warrior, Rogue and Sorcerer, relate as distinct talents that can each borrow from the others.
- **Attitudes:** Treat the character as having a split personality, and refuse to be a one-trick pony.
- **Values:** Versatility, replayability, and the fun found by experimenting beyond one class.

### Dramatic Opposition Web (4-Corner Matrix)
* **Establishment (The three archetypes):** Warrior, Rogue and Sorcerer are uniquely talented heroes with an inherent ability that dictates the best fighting style. *Leverage:* Each has a clear strength that makes play legible and effective. *Blind spot:* Leaning only on that inherent strength leaves the hero exposed.
* **Main opponent (Foes immune to a hero's usual attack):** Some enemies cannot be handled by non-magical combat alone. *Leverage:* They punish a character built too narrowly. *Blind spot:* They fall to a hero who has prepared a second, magical option.
* **Thematic shadow (The one-trick pony):** Pour everything into a couple of traits and be superb at one thing. *Leverage:* Great strength in a single narrow area. *Blind spot:* Total failure when that area does not apply.
* **Tertiary outcast (The player who never leaves the Warrior class):** The familiar path is enough. *Leverage:* Reliable mastery of a single style. *Blind spot:* Misses two-thirds of the fun.

### Output Dial
- **Level 1:** A hero is comfortable in their main style and a second option is a pleasant extra.
- **Level 2:** The party meets a foe that resists their main approach and must lean on a secondary option.
- **Level 3:** A narrowly built hero is stranded against enemies they cannot hurt, and the party scrambles to compensate.
- **Level 4:** An over-specialized party meets an enemy their only methods cannot touch, forcing retreat or ruin.

### Threat Clocks
- **Over-Specialization Exposed [4 Ticks]:** advances when The party meets foes their primary method cannot handle and has no fallback ready.

### Yields
A shared understanding of how to build a flexible hero, giving the party a secondary approach before it is needed.

### Standalone Fallback
Run it as a short mentor scene where a veteran advises the party to prepare a second approach for foes their usual method cannot handle. The scene ends with each hero naming a backup method they will cultivate.
