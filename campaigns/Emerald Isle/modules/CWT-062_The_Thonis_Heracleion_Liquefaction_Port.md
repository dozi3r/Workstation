# MODULE CWT-062: THE THONIS-HERACLEION LIQUEFACTION PORT

### 1. Mechanical Architecture & Geotechnical Grounding
Straddling the mouth of the Canopic branch of the Nile at the threshold of the Mediterranean Sea (in the ancient Bay of Aboukir), **The Thonis-Heracleion Liquefaction Port** models the pre-industrial world's premier maritime customs monopoly built upon an unstable estuarine foundation. Operating as the sole mandatory port of entry for all foreign merchant galleys entering the empire, the city-complex sprawls across an intricate 110-square-kilometer archipelago of waterlogged alluvial silt islands. Interconnected by dredged navigational canals, cedar mooring basins, heavy ashlar quayside retaining walls, and colossal religious sanctums—dominated by the monolithic Grand Temple of Amun-Gereb—this port functions as an extractive economic funnel that converts sea-borne trade into royal imperial bullion.

However, the entire installation rests upon a catastrophic geotechnical vulnerability: **soil liquefaction and bearing capacity failure under cyclic dynamic stress**.

The site's geotechnical architecture is governed by four interrelated structural realities:
1. **Pore-Water Overpressure & Sudden Soil Liquefaction**: The port is founded on low-density, unconsolidated alluvial silt and high-plasticity marine clay under constant tidal saturation. When the region experiences cyclic shear stresses—originating from subduction earthquakes along the offshore Hellenic arc combined with catastrophic hydrostatic head during peak Nile flood inundations—the water trapped between soil grains cannot escape. Pore-water pressure spikes until it equals the total overburden weight. In a fraction of a minute, effective stress drops to zero: the soil's internal shear resistance vanishes completely, transforming load-bearing clay into an amorphous, boiling quicksand slurry.
2. **Structural Bearing Capacity Failure ("Pancaking")**: Upon this precarious foundation, imperial architects erected staggering structural dead loads: 5-meter red granite colossi of Amun-Gereb and the Ptolemaic monarchs (each exceeding 20 to 30 metric tons), massive limestone temple pylons, and solid ashlar retaining revetments along the docks. The downward bearing stress dramatically exceeds the depleted shear strength of liquefied silt. During liquefaction events, the clay stratum buckles laterally. Unsupported from beneath, monolithic temples pancake vertically, their foundation slabs fracturing while columns, colonnades, and bronze-plated gates topple outward into the harbor basins, bodily dragging the surrounding city blocks down with them.
3. **The 64-Shipwreck Maritime Graveyard**: When retaining quays liquefy and slide into the maritime basins, moored foreign cargo galleys are pinned against fracturing stone revetments or crushed beneath falling granodiorite masonry. Over centuries of cyclic collapses, more than **64 ancient merchant vessels** and **700 stone and lead-stocked anchors** have been dragged into the anaerobic floor of the bay, buried under 10 meters of mud alongside royal treasury vaults, Athenian tetradrachms, Phoenician oil amphorae, and sacred bronze reliquaries.
4. **The Chained Harbor Boom Customs Chokepoint**: A massive iron-linked harbor chain, forged from 4-inch wrought links and winched between twin ashlar bastion towers, seals off the Canopic estuary. Foreign vessels cannot enter Egyptian riverwaters without anchoring in the inspection basin, submitting their manifests to the Royal Customs Arch-Scribe, and paying a mandatory 10% tax in silver bullion, frankincense, or olive oil. This chokepoint produces extreme bureaucratic gridlock: dozens of triremes and round-hulled freighters remain penned in the precarious basin for days, completely vulnerable to sudden seismic ground failure.

* **The Grand Temple of Amun-Gereb**: The religious and administrative hub of the archipelago, constructed from massive nummulitic limestone blocks and flanked by colossal granodiorite effigies. It holds the divine treasury where centuries of imported tax gold and consecrated offerings are deposited in subterranean crypts directly above the liquefying clay stratum.
* **The Royal Customs Toll-House & Archive**: A multi-tiered stone administration complex set on the central island quay. It houses the papyrus tax rolls, standard lead measurement weights, and the mechanical windlasses controlling the harbor boom chains.
* **The Hellenic Galley Wharves & Transshipment Slips**: A sprawling network of timber docks and limestone revetments where foreign merchantmen moor to discharge trade amphorae, barter with native factors, and await clearance tablets.
* **The High-Tide Barrier Jetties & Silt Weirs**: Outer boulder-mound breakwaters designed to break Mediterranean storm swells and redirect Nile silt deposition away from the primary harbor basins.

### 2. Thematic Premise & Syllogism

* **The Stasis / The Lie:** *"Pharaoh's customs collectors sit upon eternal granite quays; every foreign galley pays homage in silver, and our sacred colossi will anchor the mouth of the Nile forever against any sea."*
* **The Crucible / Conflict:** A violent offshore seismic tremor strikes during peak Nile inundation; pore-water pressure spikes, instantly liquefying the water-saturated clay foundations. Stone plazas turn into boiling quicksand while temple columns topple into the harbor, dragging tied-up merchant galleys to the sea floor.
* **Dramatic Syllogism:**  
  $$\text{Monumental Imperial Hubris on Alluvial Silt} + \text{Cyclic Seismic Soil Liquefaction} = \text{Instant Maritime Inundation \& Submerged Silt Graveyard}$$
* **The Truth / Synthesis:** No quantity of granite masonry can resist the collapse of the earth beneath it. Commercial empires built on unstable ecological foundations are swallowed whole when the ground gives way.

* **Corner 1: The Establishment (The Royal Customs Arch-Scribe — Hatshepsut-re):**  
  A dogmatic senior functionary of the Crown Treasury who regards royal customs edicts as sacred cosmic law. Clothed in bleached linen and carrying an inkwell carved from serpentine stone, Hatshepsut-re refuses to grant sailing clearance or winch open the iron harbor boom without stamped clay receipts and exact tax payments. Even as gray silt slurry bubbles up through his office flagstones and walls tilt at steep angles, the Arch-Scribe continues inspecting cargo manifests, threatening foreign captains with impoundment while ordering guards to secure the gold vaults.
* **Corner 2: The Zealot Opponent (The Hellenic Galley League Captain — Navarch Nikostratos):**  
  Commander of a locked-in merchant-mercenary flotilla hailing from the Aegean city-states. Having paid exorbitant mooring fees while watching his vessels sit idle behind the chained harbor boom, Nikostratos recognizes the geotechnical doom approaching. Viewing the Egyptian toll system as legalized piracy, he rallies two hundred Greek oarsmen and peltasts to arm the ships' ballistas, prepare incendiary pitch pots, and ram the iron harbor chain before the port drags their hulls into the mud.
* **Corner 3: The Thematic Shadow (The Silt-Scavenger Diver Guild — Guild-Master Kenna):**  
  Leader of an indigenous guild of reed-divers and wreckers who inhabit the squalid waterfront alleys. Intimately familiar with the subsoil instability, the scavengers do not fear the city's submersion—they actively accelerate it. Guild divers covertly remove iron pins from stone quay revetments and saw through wooden foundation pilings, anticipating the moment the royal treasury and foreign merchant galleys slip beneath the waves so their divers can monopolize underwater salvage operations.
* **Corner 4: The Tertiary Outcast (Canopic Papyrus-Raft Foragers — Elder Ta-Miu):**  
  Indigenous marsh folk who live upon flexible, buoyant papyrus-reed rafts in the outer delta lagoons. Disdained by imperial authorities and foreign merchants alike, they understand the river's dynamic ecology and have warned for generations that piling granite upon soft mud invited catastrophe. When the islands begin slumping into quicksand, their unsinkable craft maneuver effortlessly through the churning slurry, offering extraction to drowning scribes and merchants in return for ancestral land charters and emancipation tablets.

### 3. Injected Tabletop Mechanics & Runtime Primitives

#### 3.1 4-Level Dynamic Dial: Soil Liquefaction & Estuarine Subsidence Dial
The geotechnical integrity, pore-water overpressure, and physical stability of the island archipelago are tracked along a 4-level dial strictly calibrated to Creator Studio's Universal Hazard Tiers:

* **Level 1 (Mud Slurry Seepage & Tilting Pavement / Light Severity Tier):**  
  * *Condition:* Minor foreshocks shudder through the delta; groundwater tables spike; gray silt slurry bubbles up through paving seams; quayside walkways tilt at 5-degree angles; moored galley hawsers hum under tension.
  * *Mechanics:* Wet, shifting mud imposes a **DC 10 Balance (Acrobatics) or Athletics check** when moving faster than half speed; failure causes characters to stumble, dropping held items or falling prone. Navigating narrow harbor canals requires a **DC 10 Vehicles (Sailing) check** to avoid grounding on fresh silt bars. Mooring lines groan under surging currents; DC 10 Insight detects abnormal maritime recession along outer beaches.
* **Level 2 (Liquefaction Sand Boils & Quayside Collapse / Moderate Severity Tier):**  
  * *Condition:* Steady cyclic tremors vibrate the islands; localized liquefaction creates sand boils and erupting mud geysers across temple courtyards; ashlar quay walls fracture and belly outward; wooden mooring posts snap under rotational soil displacement, dragging small skiffs under.
  * *Mechanics:* Ground counts as difficult terrain across all paved surfaces. Characters wearing heavy metal armor have their base walking speed halved. Traversing fracturing docks or running across sinking plazas requires a **DC 13 Reflex or Athletics check**; on a failure, the character sinks waist-deep into quicksand slurry, suffering **2d8 bludgeoning/crushing damage** from shifting masonry blocks and becoming restrained (requires an action and a DC 13 Athletics check by the character or an ally to pull free). Moored vessels suffer hull stress (**2d8 structural damage** unless lines are severed or slackened).
* **Level 3 (Colossal Monolith Pancake & Inundation Wave / Severe Severity Tier):**  
  * *Condition:* Soil shear strength plummets; bearing capacity failure causes the Grand Temple’s 30-ton granodiorite colossi, stone pylons, and customs warehouses to pancake vertically and slide bodily into the harbor basin; an offshore slump generates a 3-meter tsunami wave that crashes over the waterfront quays.
  * *Mechanics:* High-velocity structural collapse and inundation. Characters in affected sectors must succeed on a **DC 16 Fortitude or Reflex check**; on a failure, they are swept into a swirling liquefaction vortex, suffering **4d10 water and crushing damage** from submerged granite chunks, and are pinned underwater beneath debris (suffocating until freed with a DC 16 Athletics check). Ships in the basin must make a **DC 16 Vehicles (Sailing) or Piloting check**; failure capsizes the vessel or smashes it against the harbor boom, trapping passengers below decks.
* **Level 4 (Catastrophic Estuarine Slumping & Total Submersion / Lethal Climax Tier):**  
  * *Condition:* High-plasticity marine clay stratum liquefies completely into an offshore mudslide; the entire 110-square-kilometer island archipelago slides downward along a rotational fault plane, plunging 10 meters beneath the Mediterranean Sea as the Canopic mouth swallows the ruins.
  * *Mechanics:* Absolute environmental catastrophe. All solid land vanishes into churning marine depths. Every creature on foot or caught within sinking structures must succeed on a **DC 20 Survival or Athletics (Swimming) check**; failure inflicts **8d12 lethal crushing and drowning damage** as masonry collapses, dragging victims into the benthic silt bed (instant character death or permanent catastrophic maiming). Surviving vessels are tossed into turbulent whirlpools. The port node is permanently destroyed and converts into an underwater archaeological necropolis.

#### 3.2 Segmented Threat Clocks

```
[ ][ ][ ][ ][ ][ ] Sediment Pore-Pressure Clock (6 Ticks)
[ ][ ][ ][ ]       Quayside Riot & Galley Breakout Clock (4 Ticks)
```

* **Sediment Pore-Pressure Clock [6 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks forward (+1) whenever an offshore seismic tremor occurs, the Nile flood crest reaches peak inundation stage, or heavy monumental cargo (granite statuary, lead ballast, gold chests) is concentrated on unreinforced quay sectors.
  * *Escalation Events:*  
    * *Tick 2:* Sand boils erupt in the customs courtyard; basement treasuries flood with liquid silt; the waterfront tilt increases to 10 degrees.
    * *Tick 4:* Western temple colonnade shears at the plinth and topples into the channel; harbor depth drops by 2 meters as the basin floor buckles upward.
  * *Consequence when full (6/6):* Soil shear strength drops to zero across all islands. Instantly triggers **Dial Level 4: Catastrophic Estuarine Slumping & Total Submersion**. The archipelago collapses bodily into the Mediterranean.
* **Quayside Riot & Galley Breakout Clock [4 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks forward (+1) each operational watch that foreign galleys are refused harbor departure clearance, when customs scribes impound foreign merchant cargoes during active tremors, or when Greek crews clash with royal port archers.
  * *Escalation Events:*  
    * *Tick 1:* Foreign crews muster on their decks with drawn swords, shouting curses and refusing to allow scribes to board.
    * *Tick 2:* Greek skirmishers loose fire-arrows at the Customs Toll-House roof; Arch-Scribe orders port archers to form a shield wall along the docks.
    * *Tick 3:* Two heavily armed quinqueremes cut their mooring hawsers and steer into the harbor basin, mounting ballistas to target the boom winch towers.
  * *Consequence when full (4/4):* Mercenary galleys ram the harbor boom at ramming speed while assault squads storm the winch towers. The customs wharf is set ablaze, creating an all-out three-way naval melee across the sinking harbor basin.

### 4. Logistical & Resource Flow Contract

* **Input Prerequisites (`requires`):**
  * `Geography: Alluvial-Nile-Silt-Islands`: A multi-island deltaic network formed by millennia of rich river silt deposition, providing sheltered deep-water anchorages at the interface between riverine and maritime navigation.
  * `Hydrology: High-Tide-Barrier-Jetties`: Engineered stone jetties, barrier groynes, and wooden groins designed to buffer the fragile islands against Mediterranean storm surges and stabilize the navigation channel against silt choking.
* **Output Yields (`provides`):**
  * `Transit: Delta-Maritime-Gateway`: Primary maritime corridor connecting Mediterranean blue-water trade fleets to the riverine highway of the Nile interior, controlling passage into the imperial heartland.
  * `Commerce: Royal-Customs-Toll-Office`: An institutional customs infrastructure that inspects cargo, enforces tax collection, collects tariffs in gold and silver bullion, and registers foreign travelers.
  * `Relic: Submerged-Amun-Gereb-Treasury`: Colossal submerged temple vaults containing centuries of divine votive offerings, gold ceremonial plate, Ptolemaic bronzes, and sacred inscribed hieroglyphic stelae.
* **Standalone Fallback Mode:**
  * When cyclic liquefaction drops the islands beneath 10 meters of water, the node permanently converts into **Sunken Silt Graveyard Mode**:
    * All customs offices, stone quays, and boom winch towers are obliterated; imperial toll revenues drop to zero, reducing regional maritime commerce by **90%**.
    * Deep-draft merchant galleys cannot navigate the uncharted shallows; maritime transit requires shallow-draft catamarans or continuous diver soundings to avoid submerged temple pylons and toppled statues.
    * Predatory wreck-scavenger syndicates and renegade corsairs establish floating barge cities over the sunken ruins, diving with bronze bells and reed tubes to strip gold from the drowned colossi while ambushing stranded merchant craft.
    * Navigating the sunken shoals without a local diver pilot requires a **DC 16 Vehicles (Sailing) check**; failure runs the hull aground upon a submerged granite monument, inflicting **4d10 hull damage**.

### 5. Scene Calculus & Tactical Spatial Encounters

* **Encounter 1: The Tilting Customs Wharf (Arrival Collision)**
  * *Collision ($><$):* The party arrives aboard an incoming foreign galley carrying vital cargo or pursuing an urgent passage into the Nile. As their vessel ties up at the Grand Customs Wharf, the Arch-Scribe Hatshepsut-re boards with a squad of thirty royal spearmen. He demands that the party surrender all weapons, open their sealed containers, and pay an extortionate 10% tariff in silver coin before receiving clearance tablets. Simultaneously, an offshore tremor strikes: the stone quay tilts at a 15-degree angle, seawater geysers through the flagstones, and mooring lines begin snapping like bowstrings.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* The party arrives anticipating formal legal admission and safe harbor, only to find themselves ensnared in an authoritarian confiscation dispute while the physical quay beneath their feet disintegrates into boiling mud.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* Arch-Scribe Hatshepsut-re reads formal bureaucratic decrees from a gold-fringed papyrus with unyielding imperial arrogance, but his sandals are submerged in bubbling gray sludge, his scribal desk is sliding toward the edge of the wharf, and his bodyguards cast terrified glances at the groaning temple walls above.
  * *Tactical Mechanics:* Characters on the tilting quay must make a **DC 10 Balance check** each round to stand upright. De-escalating the guards requires a **DC 13 Persuasion or Intimidation check**, while physically uncoupling the vessel's stressed mooring hawsers requires a **DC 13 Athletics check** before the quay wall collapses into the hull (**2d8 bludgeoning damage** on failure).
* **Encounter 2: The Fall of the Colossus of Amun-Gereb (The Severance Crisis / Dilemma)**
  * *Collision ($><$):* As the harbor integrity deteriorates to Dial Level 3, the monumental 20-ton black granodiorite colossus of Amun-Gereb topples from the temple platform. It crashes directly across the narrow deep-water exit channel, wedging its massive torso between two sunken merchant hulls and choking off the only escape route to the sea. Trapped behind the fallen colossus is a wide wooden transport barge laden with two hundred civilian refugees, temple acolytes, and wounded port laborers. Behind them, the Grand Temple's limestone pylon is actively pancaking into the water.
  * *The Dilemma:*
    * *Option A (Rig Towing Cables & Drag the Colossus Clear):* The party can secure heavy hemp tow-lines from a war galley to the colossus's granite head, using rowing power and a **DC 16 Athletics or Engineering check** to pull the monolith off the channel bed. Success clears the passage and saves the refugee barge; however, working directly beneath the collapsing temple terrace exposes the party to falling ashlar blocks (**DC 16 Reflex save** or take **4d10 crushing damage**), and risks snapping the galley's oars, leaving the vessel crippled in the harbor.
    * *Option B (Abandon the Barge & Ram the Collapse Barricade):* The party can cut loose, abandon the refugee barge to its fate, and use their own ship's bronze ram or shallow draft to force a passage over the submerged breakwater before the iron harbor boom sinks permanently into the silt. This guarantees the party's immediate survival and preserves their vessel, but condemns the two hundred refugees to be crushed under the falling temple pylons and drown in the liquefaction slurry.
* **Encounter 3: The Drowned Sanctuary & The Wreckers' Chasm (Outcast Sanctuary / Ambush)**
  * *Collision ($><$):* Cut off from the outer harbor, the party must navigate through the submerged colonnades of the sunken lower temple district to recover a crucial imperial relic (or secure an alternate exit to the Nile). Silt-Scavenger divers surface from the murky waters, wielding barbed spears and line-cutting sickles. The wreckers intend to drown the party and strip their bodies for bullion. Simultaneously, Elder Ta-Miu and her Canopic papyrus-raft foragers appear from the reed channels, offering to toss buoyant reed floats and guide the party through stable water pockets—if the party protects them from the wreckers and hands over the Arch-Scribe's royal registry seal.
  * *Tactical Environment:* Half-submerged temple hall; water depth varies from waist-deep mud slurry to 4-meter submerged pools. Liquefied silt grants total concealment beyond 10 feet underwater. Wreckers attempt to drag party members beneath the surface (grapple against **DC 13 Athletics**; submerged targets suffer suffocation rules unless freed).
  * *Resolution:* Driving off the wreckers requires neutralizing their champion or severing their diving lines. Aligning with Elder Ta-Miu provides an agile papyrus-skiff escort capable of traversing the liquefaction mudflats without grounding.
