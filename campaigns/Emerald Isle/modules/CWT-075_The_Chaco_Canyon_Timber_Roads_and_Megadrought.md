# MODULE CWT-075: THE CHACO CANYON TIMBER ROADS AND MEGADROUGHT

### 1. Mechanical Architecture & Archaeological Grounding
Flourishing between 850 and 1150 CE in the hyper-arid, treeless sandstone badlands of the San Juan Basin in northwestern New Mexico, the Chaco Phenomenon represents one of the most astonishing logistical, architectural, and ceremonial networks in North American prehistory. Anchored by monumental Great Houses such as **Pueblo Bonito**, **Chetro Ketl**, **Pueblo Del Arroyo**, and **Pueblo Alto**, Chaco Canyon served as the theocratic, administrative, and astronomical heart of the Ancestral Puebloan world.

The engineering, logistics, and sudden systemic abandonment of this desert civilization rest upon five core archaeological and dendroclimatological realities documented by Julio Betancourt, Christopher Guiterman, and Stephen Lekson:

1. **The 240,000-Timber Hand-Carried Relay**: Chaco Canyon is a barren, windswept badland that natively supported only scrub pinyon and juniper—species structurally incapable of spanning massive Great House halls. Dendrochronological and strontium isotope fingerprinting proves that **over 240,000 ponderosa pine, Douglas fir, and white fir timbers** were harvested from distant high-altitude forests in the Chuska and Zuni Mountains, located **50 to 75 miles (80 to 120 km) away**. Every single multi-ton beam was felled using stone axes, peeled of bark, seasoned in mountain groves, and **carried entirely on human shoulders** across scorched desert plateaus without pack beasts, wheeled wagons, or river transport.
2. **The Radial 30-Foot Engineered Roadway Grid**: Over 150 outlier Great Houses across a 40,000-square-mile domain were stitched together by straight, engineered roadways—most notably the 35-mile Great North Road. These corridors were cleared down to native sandstone bedrock **30 feet (9 meters) wide**, lined with low earthen curbs, and leveled with earthen causeways. When encountering sheer 100-foot vertical sandstone cliffs, the roadways did not detour; builders carved colossal rock staircases and masonry ramps directly into the cliff faces, functioning simultaneously as high-volume timber transit arteries and sacred cosmic processional tracks.
3. **Core-and-Veneer Masonry Architecture**: Chacoan master builders developed multi-story "core-and-veneer" ashlar stonework: a central structural core of rough sandstone rubble bonded with clay mortar, clad on both faces with paper-thin tabular sandstone veneers fitted with microscopic precision. This dual-envelope system distributed enormous vertical dead-loads, allowing Great Houses like Pueblo Bonito to reach 4 to 5 stories in height, containing over 650 interconnected rooms and dozens of subterranean circular Great Kivas.
4. **Astronomical Solstice & Lunar Standstill Geometry**: Atop Fajada Butte at the canyon mouth, three massive 2-meter sandstone slabs channeled sunlight and moonlight onto spiral petroglyphs (the **Sun Dagger**). This installation tracked summer and winter solstices, solar equinoxes, and the complex 18.6-year lunar standstill cycle with mathematical perfection. The primary walls and sightlines of Chetro Ketl and Pueblo Bonito mirrored these exact celestial azimuths, validating theocracy through cosmic synchronization.
5. **The 1130 CE Fifty-Year Megadrought & Ritual Sealing**: Tree-ring records (*Pinus ponderosa* cores) prove that beginning in 1130 CE, an unprecedented 50-year megadrought gripped the Southwest. Winter snowpack in the peripheral mountains collapsed, drying the ephemeral summer flash floods of the Chaco Wash. The wash channel incised 15 feet into the alluvium, dropping the water table out of reach of canal gates and rendering floodwater farming impossible. Mountain logging communities, facing their own starvation, halted timber relays. Deprived of food, timber, and divine legitimacy, the theocracy fractured. The inhabitants systematically dismantled the ponderosa roofs, ritually burned or sealed the Great Kivas with mortar plugs, and dispersed northward into defensible sandstone cliff alcoves (Mesa Verde) and southward toward permanent river basins.

### 2. Thematic Premise & Syllogism

* **The Stasis / The Lie:** "The Sun and the Moon obey the stone sightlines of our Great Kivas; as long as the peripheral clans bear two hundred thousand mountain pines across the desert sands, our Great Houses will summon the monsoons and hold the four horizons in celestial order."
* **The Crucible / Conflict:** A fifty-year megadrought desiccates the Chaco Wash into an incised gravel ditch; peripheral mountain logging clans mutiny and refuse to carry beams across the desert; canyon sun-priests demand that commoners strip the remaining roof timbers to fuel sacrificial fires while starving families riot for access to the sealed maize vaults.
* **Dramatic Syllogism:**  
  $$\text{Theocratic Architectural Siphon} + \text{Multi-Decadal Climatic Megadrought} = \text{Supply Line Severance \& Ritual Abandonment of the Great Houses}$$
* **The Truth / Synthesis:** Celestial monuments cannot compel rain from an exhausted sky. When a theocratic elite extracts unsustainable sweat and timber from an arid ecology, the people will dismantle the sacred roofs, break the turquoise jars, and seek refuge in the living rock.

* **Corner 1: The Establishment (The Kiva Astronomical Sun-Priest):**  
  Presides from the fifth-story terraces of Pueblo Bonito and the high altar of Fajada Butte. Believes that the solar and lunar daggers are the literal heartbeat of the cosmos, requiring ever-larger Great Kivas roofed in colossal ponderosa pine to capture celestial favor. Under the megadrought, the Sun-Priest becomes fanatical: rather than opening the sealed underground maize silos, they proclaim the drought is punishment for spiritual laxity, demanding that logging brigades double their quotas across the sunbaked plains.
* **Corner 2: The Zealot Opponent (The San Juan Mesa Cliff-Dwelling Separatist):**  
  A former outlier great house war-leader who has renounced the authority of the canyon. Convinced that the Great Houses are cursed parasites that devour human lives and sacred timber, they have fortified defensive sandstone cliff alcoves along the northern mesa rims. Their raiders fell ponderosa trees across the 30-foot road corridors, ambush timber brigades, and strip masonry veneer stones to build high, narrow-access bastion towers.
* **Corner 3: The Thematic Shadow (The Turquoise-Macaw Trade Caravaner):**  
  A cosmopolitan merchant operating along the long-distance trade routes reaching deep into Mesoamerica. Carrying scarlet macaws in wicker baskets, copper bells, and marine shell mosaics, the Caravaner capitalizes on societal panic: they trade prestigious ritual ornaments and bird plumes to desperate canyon priests for hoarded emergency corn, while covertly buying up young timber haulers as bonded caravan porters before the valley collapses entirely.
* **Corner 4: The Tertiary Outcast (The Exhausted Timber Hauler):**  
  A veteran laborer whose collarbones and lumbar vertebrae have been permanently crushed and deformed by thirty years of bearing 30-foot ponderosa logs across seventy-five miles of scorching badlands. Cast aside by the temple overseers when no longer able to lift heavy wood, the Hauler lives in an ephemeral brush shelter near the arroyo mouth. They know the hidden subsurface sand-seeps where moisture still pools beneath the dry wash and understand that the valley was killed not by the gods, but by masonry dams that choked the natural flood silt.

### 4. Injected Tabletop Mechanics & Runtime Primitives

#### 4.1 4-Level Dynamic Dial: Megadrought Desiccation & Structural Dismantling
The environmental, hydrological, and architectural integrity of the canyon is monitored along a 4-level dial strictly calibrated to Universal Hazard Tiers:

* **Level 1 (Arid Wash Flash Flooding & Timber Silt Drift / Light Severity Tier):**  
  * *Condition:* Summer heat reaches 100°F; high desert dust devils scour the canyon; the Chaco Wash experiences brief, violent flash floods carrying silt and floating timber scraps.
  * *Mechanics:* Long-distance travel along open sandstone mesas requires a **DC 10 Athletics or Survival check** each watch; failure incurs 1 level of heat fatigue/exhaustion. Navigating rock-cut cliff stairways during sudden dust gusts requires a **DC 10 Acrobatics check** to avoid dropping heavy equipment.
* **Level 2 (Chaco Roadway Sand-Choke & Timber Hauler Heatstroke / Moderate Severity Tier):**  
  * *Condition:* The dry season extends into its third consecutive failed monsoon; the 30-foot engineered roads are half-buried under red sand drifts; timber hauler brigades suffer widespread heat collapse; peripheral springs turn alkaline.
  * *Mechanics:* Water consumption doubles for all travelers. Foraging or locating potable subsurface seeps requires a **DC 13 Survival check**. Manually hauling multi-ton timbers up rock-cut ramps or clearing drifted sand berms requires a **DC 13 Athletics or Fortitude check**; failure inflicts **2d8 bludgeoning/heat damage** from shifting logs or heat cramps.
* **Level 3 (Multi-Decadal Megadrought & Kiva Roof Dismantling / Severe Severity Tier):**  
  * *Condition:* The 1130 CE megadrought sets in; winter snowpack across the Chuska and Zuni mountains drops to zero; the Chaco Wash incises 15 feet into the canyon floor, leaving agricultural diversion sills suspended high above the dry streambed; clans begin unroofing the Great Houses to reclaim timbers.
  * *Mechanics:* Acute regional starvation and water exhaustion. Characters traveling without curated grain and water supplies must make a **DC 16 Fortitude check** every 12 hours or suffer **4d10 famine/thirst damage** and disorientation. Outlier militias drop boulders and fire obsidian arrows from mesa-top road rims: descending the rock stairs under hostile fire requires a **DC 16 Reflex or Athletics check** to evade falling masonry.
* **Level 4 (Catastrophic Regional Desiccation & Mesa Warfare / Lethal Climax Tier):**  
  * *Condition:* Complete environmental and societal collapse. The Great Houses are abandoned; priests ignite pitch across the remaining kiva roofs and seal the stone doorways with mortar; starving factions conduct scorched-earth raids across the canyon floor; population disperses into fortified cliff dwellings.
  * *Mechanics:* Lethal hyper-arid warfare. The canyon is completely devoid of surface water. Characters exposed to the daytime badlands without magical or massive logistical hydration must make a **DC 20 Survival or Fortitude check** every 6 hours; failure inflicts **8d12 lethal dehydration and heatstroke damage**. Collapsing 5-story core-and-veneer masonry walls inflict **4d10 crushing damage** across plaza areas. The settlement permanently dissolves into an archaeological ruin.

#### 4.2 Segmented Threat Clocks

```

[ ][ ][ ][ ]       Pueblo Great House Roof Dismantling Meter (4 Ticks)
```

* **Fifty-Year Megadrought Desiccation Clock [6 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks forward (+1) each operational cycle that dendrochronological tree-rings record zero winter snowpack across the San Juan Basin, or when arroyo headcutting incises the Chaco Wash deeper into its bed.
  * *Escalation Events:*  
    * *Tick 2:* Ephemeral side-canyon floodwater canals run dry; maize yields drop by 50%; priests issue ration tokens.
    * *Tick 4:* Chuska and Zuni timber supply brigades mutiny; logging camps in the mountains refuse canyon emissaries.
  * *Consequence when full (6/6):* Triggers **Dial Level 4: Catastrophic Regional Desiccation** immediately. Groundwater drops past the root zone; all agricultural cultivation fails; the canyon is permanently abandoned.
* **Pueblo Great House Roof Dismantling Meter [4 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks forward (+1) whenever clans strip ponderosa roof beams to construct defensive cliff alcoves, when priests ritually burn sacred kiva roofs, or when structural veneers shear away from lack of maintenance.
  * *Escalation Events:*  
    * *Tick 1:* Outlying second-tier rooms at Chetro Ketl are unroofed; timber beams hauled north to mesa rims.
    * *Tick 2:* Multi-story veneer walls crack as horizontal tie-beams are extracted; exterior plazas are showered with tabular stone debris.
    * *Tick 3:* The Great Kiva of Pueblo Bonito is stripped of its primary 30-foot support pillars; subterranean chambers open to the scorching sky.
  * *Consequence when full (4/4):* Total structural decommissioning. The central Great Houses lose structural integrity; inner ceilings collapse in cascade failures; remaining rooms are ritually sealed with ash and stone plugs.

### 5. Logistical & Resource Flow Contract

* **Input Prerequisites (`requires`):**
  * `Forest: High-Altitude-Ponderosa-Pine-Timbers`: Intact sub-alpine forests in the Chuska and Zuni mountain ranges capable of supplying hundreds of thousands of straight, load-bearing roof beams across a 75-mile haul radius.
  * `Hydrology: Ephemeral-Wash-Runoff-Harvesting`: Predictable summer convective thunderstorms generating flash floods down side-canyon rincons, diverted through stone weirs and earthen canals onto alluvial fan fields.
* **Output Yields (`provides`):**
  * `Topography: Prehistoric-Chaco-Road-Radial-Grid`: A monumental network of 30-foot-wide engineered highways cut down to sandstone bedrock with rock-cut stairways and causeways, enabling high-speed overland transit across 40,000 square miles.
  * `Structure: Multi-Story-Core-and-Veneer-Great-House`: Monumental D-shaped multi-story ashlar masonry complexes (up to 5 stories and 650+ rooms) providing massive fortified storage, astronomical alignment, and high-density shelter.
  * `Ritual: Great-Kiva-Cosmic-Alignment`: Subterranean circular sanctuaries and petroglyphic calendar markers (such as the Fajada Butte Sun Dagger) providing hyper-accurate tracking of solar and lunar standstill cycles.
* **Standalone Fallback Mode:**
  * When multi-decadal megadrought desiccates wash runoff and pine forests recede past 75 miles, the installation enters **Cliffside Refuge Fallback**:
    * Great House kivas are ritually decommissioned and sealed with clay mortar.
    * Ponderosa roof beams are extracted and hauled to northern sandstone mesas to frame defensible, multi-room cliff dwellings nestled under natural rock overhangs (Mesa Verde mode).
    * Centralized theocracy and long-distance road transit collapse; regional society splinters into autonomous, self-sufficient mesa farming clans subsisting on sand-dune moisture farming and natural cave-seep springs.

### 6. Scene Calculus & Tactical Tabletop Sparks

* **Encounter 1: The Dropped Ponderosa on the North Stair (Arrival Collision)**
  * *Collision ($><$):* As the party ascends the colossal 30-foot-wide rock-cut staircase cut into the sheer sandstone canyon rim along the Great North Road, they collide with a 50-person timber brigade struggling to haul a 2-ton, 40-foot seasoned ponderosa pine trunk down the precipice. A lead hauler collapses from heatstroke and spinal exhaustion; the massive timber slips its braided yucca hauling ropes and begins sliding down the steep stone stairs directly toward the party and a descending caravan of water amphorae.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* The party arrives at an engineered imperial highway expecting unobstructed passage, only to be trapped on a sheer vertical cliff face in a split-second athletic crisis where dodging the rolling log risks sending the precious water supply smashing into the canyon floor.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The taskmaster cracks a rawhide quirt over the fallen hauler’s shoulders, screaming that the Sun Dagger will blind any clan that delays the timber; but as he turns, his own waterskin hangs bone-dry and his lips are split and bleeding from dehydration.
* **Encounter 2: The Sun Dagger Solstice Standoff (The Severance Crisis / Dilemma)**
  * *Collision ($><$):* At the summit of Fajada Butte, the party seeks an audience with the chief calendar-keeper to consult sacred astronomical charts. Instead, they find the high priest and their acolytes barricaded between the colossal sandstone slabs of the Sun Dagger. Starving outlier farmers have surrounded the fissure, demanding that the priests open the sealed subterranean grain rooms of Chetro Ketl.
  * *The Dilemma:*
    * *Protect the Solstice Sanctuary:* The party can defend the astronomers against the starving mob, preserving the irreplaceable celestial calendar and gaining the high priest's star maps; however, doing so forces the acolytes to fire stone-tipped arrows into their own starving kin, sparking an all-out riot that sweeps the canyon.
    * *Force the Grain Sealed Kiva:* The party can help the farmers breach the Great Kiva's sealed mortar hatches to distribute the five-hundred-year-old seed corn. This prevents immediate mass starvation, but desecrates the central sanctuary, prompting the enraged priests to take stone sledgehammers and deliberately shatter the Fajada Butte spiral petroglyphs, blinding the civilization's cosmic clock forever.
* **Encounter 3: The Burning of the Great Kiva Roof (The Climax Conflagration)**
  * *Collision ($><$):* Amidst a howling high-desert dust storm in the central plaza of Pueblo Bonito, desperate zealots have poured pinyon pitch over the ponderosa timber roof of the primary subterranean Great Kiva. They intend to ignite the sanctuary and seal the stone roof hatch with mortar to prevent "unclean famine refugees" from entering, while sixty refugee families and their children are trapped in the adjacent inner rooms behind the smoking timber colonnade.
  * *Tactical Resolution:* The party must simultaneously combat the suicidal temple guard on the plaza terraces (**DC 16 Reflex** to dodge hurled fire-pots and masonry blocks) while levering open the jammed ponderosa ceiling beams (**DC 16 Athletics**) to evacuate the trapped families before the multi-story core-and-veneer wall above them shears and collapses into the flaming pit.
