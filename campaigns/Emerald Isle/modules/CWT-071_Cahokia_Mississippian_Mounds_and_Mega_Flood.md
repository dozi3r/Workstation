# MODULE CWT-071: CAHOKIA MISSISSIPPIAN MOUNDS AND MEGA-FLOOD

### 1. Mechanical Architecture & Alluvial Hydrogeological Grounding
Drawing from the urban metropolis of Cahokia in the American Bottom floodplain of the Mississippi River (flourishing c. 1050–1250 CE opposite modern St. Louis), this module models the apex pre-Columbian urban civilization north of the Rio Grande. Spanning over 16 square kilometers and supporting an estimated population of 20,000 to 40,000 citizens—surpassing contemporary London or Paris—Cahokia was an astonishing theocratic state founded upon intensive flint maize monoculture, cosmic solar architecture, and colossal earthen engineering. Modern archaeological research, led by Timothy Pauketat, Thomas Emerson, and Samuel Munoz, reveals both the scale of Cahokia’s monumental achievements and the severe environmental tipping points that triggered its rapid collapse.

The civilization's rise and systemic demise rest upon five interconnected sociotechnical and geomorphic pillars:

1. **Monks Mound & Basket-Carried Earthen Architecture**: The political and sacred center of Cahokia was **Monks Mound**, a colossal four-tiered earthen pyramid rising 30 meters (100 feet) above the floodplain, covering 14 acres at its base (a footprint larger than the Great Pyramid of Giza), and containing more than **22 million cubic feet (600,000 cubic meters)** of earth. Every pound of dense alluvial clay, sandy loam, and river silt was excavated from borrow pits and transported on human backs in woven reed baskets. Atop the highest terrace stood a massive 5,000-square-foot timber temple where the Great Sun sovereign conducted solar rites, shielded from the common population living in the surrounding lowlands.
2. **The Maize Monoculture Machine & Woodhenge Astronomy**: The city’s demographic explosion was fueled by intensive flint maize cultivation across the silty American Bottom, complemented by shell-tempered pottery that allowed efficient grain boiling. Agricultural and ritual timing was orchestrated via **Woodhenge**—monumental circles of up to 48 massive red cedar posts calibrated to equinox and solstice sunrises over Monks Mound. By demonstrating precise alignment between solar cycles and seasonal planting, the ruling priesthood claimed divine cosmological control over rain, harvest, and life itself.
3. **Severe Theocratic Stratification & Mound 72 Retainer Sacrifices**: Mississippian divine kingship was reinforced through terrifying mortuary violence. Excavations at Mound 72 revealed the infamous "Beaded Burial," where a central elite was laid on an effigy blanket composed of **over 20,000 marine shell beads** imported through vast trade routes from the Gulf of Mexico. Flanking this grave were mass sacrificial pits containing **more than 250 executed individuals**: 53 young women strangled simultaneously, four young men decapitated and missing their hands, and dozens of buried retainers. This theatrical violence bound the subordinate clans to the sacred solar lineage through sheer spiritual and physical terror.
4. **Deforestation and the 20,000-Log Palisade**: As regional social tensions and resource strains mounted in the late 12th century, Cahokia’s elite fortified the sacred precinct with a **2-mile-long defensive palisade** featuring raised bastions and arrow slits. Excavations show this wall was rebuilt at least three times, with each iteration requiring more than **20,000 mature oak, hickory, and red cedar timbers**. Clear-cutting the upland bluffs and river bottoms for palisade posts, dwellings, and cooking fuel stripped the watershed of root cover. When heavy rains fell, uninhibited erosion flushed millions of tons of bluff sediment into Cahokia Creek, reducing floodplain drainage capacity.
5. **The 1200 CE Mega-Flood Horizon**: High-resolution sediment cores from Horseshoe Lake (Munoz et al., *PNAS* 2015) identified a catastrophic Mississippi River **mega-flood around 1200 CE**. The river crested more than 10 meters above baseline, bursting through natural levees and transforming the entire American Bottom into an inland sea of churned mud. The flood destroyed unharvested maize crops across tens of thousands of acres, washed away residential thatch quarters, and waterlogged the base of Monks Mound. Because the theocracy had staked its entire legitimacy on solar control over weather and fertility, this environmental disaster broke the priests' spiritual authority. Starving commoners revolted; the palisade was burned in repeated insurgencies; and by 1350 CE, the once-bustling metropolis had fractured into dispersed, autonomous riverine bands.

* **The Four-Tiered Summit of Monks Mound**: The supreme sanctum of the Sun-Priest Mound Sovereign. Rising 30 meters above the plain, its earthen terraces provide absolute line of sight over the entire 16-square-kilometer basin. However, prolonged saturated clay conditions make the southern slope highly susceptible to catastrophic rotational slips.
* **The Woodhenge Red Cedar Calendar**: A 410-foot-diameter circle of red cedar trunks set into deep pits. The central observation post aligns with specific perimeter posts to predict the exact morning of the summer solstice, winter solstice, and equinoxes. Priests use this astronomical machine to dictate labor shifts and food distribution.
* **The Bastioned Oak Palisade**: A monumental timber curtain wall 4 to 5 meters in height, studded with rectangular towers every 20 meters. Designed to shield the elite mounds and central plaza from insurgent commoners and rival chiefdoms, its massive timber requirements drive continuous upland deforestation.
* **The Grand Plaza & Borrow Pit Lagoons**: A vast, leveled 40-acre ceremonial expanse surfaced with compacted clay and yellow sand, utilized for mass chunkey games, religious ceremonies, and grain redistributions. Flanking the plaza are enormous sunken borrow pits that fill with stagnant floodwaters, becoming malarial backwater ponds during wet cycles.

### 2. Thematic Premise & Syllogism

* **The Stasis / The Lie:** "The Great Sun Chief commands the sky, the seasons, and the river; the Mississippi swells only to nourish our holy maize, and our towering earthen pyramids and bastioned timber palisades make Cahokia an eternal, untouchable holy city."
* **The Crucible / Conflict:** A historic 10-meter Mississippi mega-flood breaches the natural river levees, turning the American Bottom into an ocean of brown mud that drowns the harvest; as starvation grips the city, commoners burn the palisade bastions and demand the execution of the priests whose cosmic prayers failed.
* **Dramatic Syllogism:**  
  $$\text{Theocratic Hegemony on Vulnerable Floodplain} + \text{Alluvial Mega-Flood Ecological Shock} = \text{Palisade Conflagration \& Total Metropolis Desertion}$$
* **The Truth / Synthesis:** Rulers who base their legitimacy on claiming supernatural command over natural cycles are doomed the moment nature turns violent. When the waters rise higher than the pyramid, the temple becomes the altar where the rulers are sacrificed.

* **Corner 1: The Establishment (The Sun-Priest Mound Sovereign):**  
  The absolute theocratic ruler of Cahokia, dwelling in the great cedar temple atop the summit of Monks Mound. Wrapped in feather mantles and adorned with copper sun-plates, he claims to be the living brother of the solar deity. As floodwaters lap the lower terrace of his pyramid, he refuses to acknowledge ecological reality, instead ordering dozens of captive retainers and commoners marched to Mound 72 to be ritually executed and buried upon marine shell blankets, fanatically asserting that only blood sacrifice will force the river gods to recede.
* **Corner 2: The Zealot Opponent (The Palisade Timber Sapper):**  
  Formerly a decorated war captain responsible for constructing and defending the 2-mile oak palisade, he has witnessed his own timber-cutting crews starve while the priesthood feasted on stored grain. Turning against the theocracy, he leads a desperate coalition of armed commoners, woodsmen, and mutinous garrison warriors. Carrying pitch-soaked torches and flint celts, his objective is to burn down the palisade that separates the elite from the starving masses, dismantle the temple atop Monks Mound, and execute the priesthood.
* **Corner 3: The Thematic Shadow (The Floodplain Maize Guildmaster):**  
  An influential oligarch controlling the subterranean stone-and-pottery seed grain caches situated on intermediate earthen terraces. Having recognized the fragility of the annual harvest, he stockpiled thousands of bushels of dried flint corn sealed in pitch-coated jars. Rather than releasing food during the flood, he trades handfuls of maize for priceless marine shell beads, rare copper celts, and hereditary clan lands, forcing starving families to pledge their surviving children into generational domestic debt-bondage.
* **Corner 4: The Tertiary Outcast (The Riverboat Silt-Nomad Outcast):**  
  A former calendar-keeper of Woodhenge who was cast out after warning the council that the alignment of river silt bars and cedar post watermarks foretold a catastrophic 100-year deluge. Now living along the braided river sloughs in heavy dugout canoes carved from bald cypress, he and his kin navigate the drowned canopies with ease. Rejecting both the tyranny of the priests and the bloody vengeance of the mutineers, the Silt-Nomads scavenge floating grain and dismantle fallen Woodhenge cedar trunks to construct buoyant evacuation rafts for surviving refugees.

### 4. Injected Tabletop Mechanics & Runtime Primitives

#### 4.1 4-Level Dynamic Dial: River Inundation & Theocratic Collapse
The operational stability, hydrostatic flood head, and civil integrity of Cahokia are tracked across a 4-level dial strictly calibrated to Universal Hazard Tiers:

* **Level 1 (Creek Backwater Swell & Palisade Rot / Light Severity Tier):**  
  * *Condition:* Unbroken spring rains saturate the American Bottom; Cahokia Creek swells and backs up; the ground-level footings of the 20,000-log oak palisade soften with wood rot; mud covers the Grand Plaza.
  * *Mechanics:* Sprinting or tactical maneuvering across the waterlogged clay plaza requires a **DC 10 Acrobatics check**; failure results in slipping prone. Woodhenge observation posts become slick with algae; characters attempting to read celestial alignments or predict weather trends must pass a **DC 10 Survival or Nature check**. Minor travel friction and fatigue.
* **Level 2 (Levee Slumping & Silt-Drowned Maize Fields / Moderate Severity Tier):**  
  * *Condition:* The Mississippi River crests against natural alluvial levees; levee banks begin to slough and fissure; over 4,000 acres of low-lying maize crops drown in knee-deep stagnant silt; municipal food rations are halved.
  * *Mechanics:* Navigating flooded lower residential districts requires a **DC 13 Athletics or Vehicles (Water) check**; failure results in being caught in hidden silt-sinkholes, imposing **2d8 bludgeoning/strain damage** and 1 level of Exhaustion. Purchasing food requires triple value or a **DC 13 Persuasion/Intimidation check**; civil discontent requires a **DC 13 Insight check** to avoid mob confrontations in public sectors.
* **Level 3 (Mega-Flood Breach & Palisade Bastion Inferno / Severe Severity Tier):**  
  * *Condition:* A catastrophic 10-meter flood crest breaches the riverbanks, drowning the lower city in violent brown torrents; mutinous commoners led by the Timber Sapper set fire to the 2-mile oak palisade; burning watchtowers collapse into the floodwaters.
  * *Mechanics:* Churning, debris-choked floodwaters sweep through streets. Characters caught in the open torrent must succeed on a **DC 16 Fortitude or Athletics (Swimming) check** or be swept downstream, suffering **4d10 drowning and crushing damage** from uprooted trees and floating timber. Dodging falling burning palisade timbers requires a **DC 16 Reflex check**; failure inflicts lasting burn trauma and entrapment beneath blazing logs.
* **Level 4 (Monks Mound Rotational Slump & Total Urban Desertion / Lethal Climax Tier):**  
  * *Condition:* Saturated clay strata at the base of Monks Mound suffer a massive rotational slope failure; hundreds of thousands of tons of wet earth slide away, collapsing the Great Sun temple atop the summit; the entire 40,000-citizen metropolis fragments into panic as ruins submerge into backwater marsh.
  * *Mechanics:* Cataclysmic mass-movement and environmental destruction. Characters caught on or beneath the slopes of Monks Mound must succeed on a **DC 20 Athletics or Survival check**; failure inflicts **8d12 lethal crushing and suffocating mudslide damage** (instant burial under wet clay). The urban node suffers total structural and social collapse; agricultural output drops to zero, and the settlement permanently dissolves into an unmapped swamp ruin.

#### 4.2 Segmented Threat Clocks

```
[ ][ ][ ][ ][ ][ ] Mississippi Alluvial Mega-Flood Meter (6 Ticks)
[ ][ ][ ][ ]       Palisade Conflagration & Mutiny Clock (4 Ticks)
```

* **Mississippi Alluvial Mega-Flood Meter [6 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks forward (+1) each operational cycle that unceasing monsoon-like downpours continue, when heavy snowmelt discharges from the northern headwaters, or when deforested bluff runoff washes out Cahokia Creek drainage channels.
  * *Escalation Events:*  
    * *Tick 2:* Natural river levees breach along the western marsh; all dugout canoe docks are submerged; lower maize fields drown under 3 feet of brown silt.
    * *Tick 4:* Cahokia Creek reverses flow, flooding the Grand Plaza up to waist height; commoners abandon mudbrick-and-thatch hovels to camp on the lower terraces of elite mounds.
  * *Consequence when full (6/6):* The Mississippi River crests 10 meters above baseline. The alluvial bottom is completely inundated, immediately triggering **Dial Level 3: Mega-Flood Breach & Palisade Bastion Inferno**.
* **Palisade Conflagration & Mutiny Clock [4 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks forward (+1) whenever the Sun-Priest orders public retainer executions, when royal granaries refuse emergency rations to starving flood refugees, or when characters engage in violent skirmishes along the palisade bastions.
  * *Escalation Events:*  
    * *Tick 1:* Angry crowds gather in the flooded plaza; copper celt-wielding guards clash with stone-hoe-wielding maize farmers.
    * *Tick 2:* Mutineers sabotage the southern palisade gates, allowing starving mobs to raid royal granary cellars.
    * *Tick 3:* Smoke rises from the eastern bastions as the Palisade Timber Sapper begins setting fire to the outer timber curtain.
  * *Consequence when full (4/4):* Total popular insurrection. Mutineers torch the entire 2-mile oak palisade, breach the inner courtyard, and storm the steps of Monks Mound to execute the Sun-Priest hierarchy.

### 5. Logistical & Resource Flow Contract

* **Input Prerequisites (`requires`):**
  * `Labor: Basket-Carried-Alluvial-Earth`: A sustained, immense labor base capable of moving over 22 million cubic feet of heavy clay and sandy silt in woven reed baskets to construct and maintain monumental earthen terraces and mounds.
  * `Forest: Upland-Oak-Palisade-Timber`: Access to thousands of mature oak, hickory, and red cedar trunks cut from surrounding bluffs to build and periodically rebuild the 2-mile defensive curtain wall and astronomical Woodhenge circles.
* **Output Yields (`provides`):**
  * `Agriculture: Intensive-Floodplain-Maize`: Vast agricultural surpluses generated from fertile alluvial silt fields, capable of sustaining an urban population of 40,000 citizens and supplying trans-regional trade networks.
  * `Structure: Monumental-Earthen-Monks-Mound`: A 30-meter-tall elevated defensive and ceremonial earthen redoubt offering supreme tactical line of sight, flood insulation for elite leadership, and massive architectural prestige.
  * `Ritual: Shell-Bead-Cosmic-Sacrifice`: A centralized theocratic prestige economy backed by astronomical solar calendars, thousands of carved marine shell beads, and institutional retainer mortuary rites.
* **Standalone Fallback Mode:**
  * When the 1200 CE Mississippi mega-flood submerges the floodplain and mutineers burn the palisade, the module enters **Alluvial Wetland Fallback Mode**:
    * Centralized theocracy and the Great Sun lineage dissolve completely; Monks Mound becomes an eroded, abandoned clay island rising out of backwater swamps.
    * Agricultural surplus falls by **95%**; surviving inhabitants disperse into small, autonomous canoe-foraging clans subsisting on wild rice, catfish, and migratory waterfowl.
    * The ruined urban core becomes an impassable, malaria-ridden morass, imposing **DC 13 Fortitude checks** against stagnant waterborne infections on any travelers lingering longer than 24 hours.

### 6. Scene Calculus & Tactical Spatial Encounters

* **Encounter 1: The Plaza Conscription at Monks Mound (Arrival Collision)**
  * *Collision ($><$):* The party arrives at the flooded Grand Plaza seeking supplies, boat transport, or audience with regional leaders. Instead of an orderly market, they find the plaza submerged in ankle-deep silt and ringed by armored royal guards armed with polished copper celts and flint-tipped spears. High priests descending the monumental clay stairway of Monks Mound surround the party and seize their retainers and mounts, declaring them "celestial offerings appointed by the Great Sun to stem the rising Mississippi."
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* The party enters what appeared to be the apex commercial capital of the river basin, only to be classified as human sacrificial cattle destined for the mortuary pits of Mound 72.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The high priest proclaims in ringing, majestic tones that the Sun deity has bestowed divine favor upon the foreign travelers by choosing them for eternal glory; however, his hands shake, his feather cape is splattered with mud, and junior acolytes behind him are frantically loading royal shell-bead caskets into evacuation canoes hidden in the rising creek.
* **Encounter 2: The Sinking Grain Cache at the Broken Levee (Resource Dilemma)**
  * *Collision ($><$):* A natural levee collapses along the western boundary ditch, unleashing a waist-deep torrent of churning water directly toward an underground grain cellar containing 500 bushels of dry, water-sealed seed maize. The Floodplain Maize Guildmaster offers the party a chest filled with 10,000 polished marine shell beads if they use their weapons and muscle to help him load his private seed grain onto high-ground sledges. At the same time, two hundred starving local farmers armed with digging sticks and reed baskets arrive, begging the party to help them breach the cellar door to feed drowning refugee families huddled on the levee crest.
  * *The Dilemma:*
    * *Aid the Guildmaster:* The party secures immense regional wealth (the shell beads) and earns the gratitude of the urban merchant elite. However, the private grain is hauled away, leaving the farming families to starve, triggering an immediate riot where desperate peasants attack the party with agricultural implements (**DC 13 Athletics/Combat in deep mud**).
    * *Aid the Starving Farmers:* The party smashes the doors, distributing grain to save hundreds of lives. In response, the enraged Guildmaster alerts the palisade archers that the party is leading a peasant mutiny, directing volleys of flint-tipped arrows at them from the nearby bastion platform (**DC 16 Reflex check** to seek cover behind drowned maize cribs).
* **Encounter 3: The Burning of the Eastern Palisade Bastion (The Severance Crisis / Escape)**
  * *Collision ($><$):* Mutinous warriors led by the Palisade Timber Sapper have ignited the 20-foot-tall oak logs of the Eastern Palisade with pitch-soaked reed torches. The blaze has trapped a squad of royal archers and forty civilian artisans inside a three-story wooden bastion tower, while floodwaters 6 feet deep surround the burning curtain wall.
  * *Tactical Resolution & Outcast Sanctuary:* The party must navigate the inferno—either scaling the burning timber scaffolding (**DC 16 Reflex/Athletics**) to rig lowering ropes for the trapped civilians, or engaging the Sapper’s skirmishers who are spearing survivors as they leap into the swirling water. As the bastion collapses with a roar of embers, the Riverboat Silt-Nomad Outcast arrives alongside the wall in massive cypress dugout canoes, offering to pull the party and surviving refugees aboard and paddle through a drowned palisade gap into the safety of the open river.
