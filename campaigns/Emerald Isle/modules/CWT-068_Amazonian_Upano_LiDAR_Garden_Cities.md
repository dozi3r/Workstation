# MODULE CWT-068: THE AMAZONIAN UPANO LIDAR GARDEN CITIES

### 1. Mechanical Architecture & Archaeological LiDAR Grounding
Grounded in the landmark high-resolution airborne LiDAR discoveries directed by Stéphen Rostain and colleagues across the Upano Valley of eastern Ecuador (published in *Science*, 2024), alongside complementary investigations in the Llanos de Moxos of southwestern Amazonia (Casarabe culture, Heiko Prümers et al., *Nature*, 2022), this module operationalizes the sophisticated spatial, agricultural, and hydrologic engineering of ancient Amazonian agro-urbanism.

For more than a century, Western academic dogma—exemplified by Betty Meggers’ model of "environmental determinism"—characterized the Amazon Basin as a "counterfeit paradise": a pristine, untouched wilderness whose heavily leached, acidic, nutrient-deficient soils (*oxisols* and *ultisols*) were fundamentally incapable of sustaining sedentary, dense, complex urban societies. Ancient Amazonians were assumed to be forever constrained to small, mobile, slash-and-burn hunter-gatherer bands. The Upano LiDAR surveys permanently disproved this narrative, revealing a sprawling, interconnected regional metropolis of garden cities that flourished from roughly 500 BCE to 600 CE (with episodic re-occupations through 1200 CE)—predating Classic Maya urban complexes and matching or exceeding contemporaneous Andean civilizations in demographic density and earthwork coordination.

The physical architecture and ecological viability of the Upano civilization function across five interconnected engineering systems:

1. **The 6,000-Platform Monumental Mound Network (*Tolas*)**: Across a contiguous 300-square-kilometer swath along the Upano River bluffs, the population did not excavate monumental stone temples; instead, they modified the landscape through monumental earthen architecture. Over six millennia of collective labor, communities raised more than **6,000 rectangular earthen mounds (*tolas*)**. Measuring up to 20 meters wide by 10 meters long and rising 2 to 3 meters above grade, these platforms were grouped in geometric quadrangles of three to six mounds surrounding sunken communal plazas. The mounds served as elevated foundations for timber-and-thatch domestic dwellings, communal longhouses, craft workshops, and ceremonial halls, protecting inhabitants from torrential rains, venomous insects, surface damp, and seasonal sheet-wash flooding.
2. **The Colossal Sunken Causeway-Canal Arteries**: Individual platform settlements were not isolated forest clearings; they were tied together into a hyper-connected regional macro-grid by massive, straight sunken roads extending for up to 10 to 25 kilometers. Excavated 2 to 3 meters deep into the volcanic alluvium and spanning 5 to 15 meters in width, these sunken causeways were flanked by continuous lateral ditch revetments. They served a brilliant, dual-purpose hydraulic and infrastructural role: during the dry months, they formed shaded, wind-buffered pedestrian expressways and defensible transit trenches; during the torrential equatorial wet season, they filled with runoff, transforming into high-speed navigable canoe canals that channeled surplus water away from domestic mounds directly into agricultural retention basins.
3. **The Alchemy of Anthropogenic *Terra Preta de Índio***: The primary caloric engine was not destructive shifting cultivation, but the deliberate, inter-generational manufacture of anthrosols—anthropogenic black soils known as *Terra Preta*. Traditional tropical soils leach their nutrients within two seasons under heavy rain. Upano agronomists overcame this through controlled, subterranean smoldering pits that pyrolyzed agricultural waste into porous biochar. By blending this charcoal with calcined river shell, fish bones, crushed ceramic pottery sherds (which provided mechanical aeration and surface area for mycorrhizal fungi), and fermented compost, they created a living soil matrix. This biochar sponge fixed nitrogen and phosphorus permanently, neutralized soil acidity, prevented nutrient leaching, and maintained self-regenerating microbiological fertility for centuries without fallow periods.
4. **Domesticated Agroforestry Canopies & Raised-Field Wetlands (*Camellones*)**: The surrounding forest was an anthropogenic food-manufacturing machine. Upano populations cultivated structured, multi-tier polycultures dominated by peach palm (*Bactris gasipaes*—yielding calorie-dense starches, fats, and edible heart-of-palm), cacao, manioc (*cassava*), sweet potatoes, chili peppers, and nitrogen-fixing *Inga* canopy trees. In the waterlogged river bottomlands, engineers cut intricate grids of raised fields (*camellones*)—elevated farming ridges flanked by deep drainage ditches where trapped floodwaters were stocked with river fish, turtles, and edible snails in integrated wetland aquaculture systems.
5. **The Volcanic Piedmont Crucible (Mount Sangay)**: Looming directly west of the valley is Mount Sangay, one of the most active and explosive stratovolcanoes on Earth. This geographic proximity presented an existential paradox. Periodic showers of fine volcanic tephra and andesitic ash enriched the Upano soils with rare micronutrients, rejuvenating the land's fertility. However, violent plinian eruptions generated catastrophic tephra loads that collapsed thatched platform roofs, blanketed fruit canopies in sulfurous ash, and mobilized deadly, boiling mudflows (*lahars*) through the sunken causeway arteries, ultimately forcing the abandonment and dispersal of the core settlements around 600–800 CE.

#### Physical & Architectural Organs of the Garden City Node:
* **The High Tola of Kunguints**: The primary civic-ceremonial mound of the northern sector, rising 10 meters above the central sunken plaza. Built of compacted clay-silt layers interbedded with volcanic gravel, its summit hosts the Clan Council Longhouse and ancestral bone caches.
* **The Subterranean Biochar Smolder Kilns**: A sprawling perimeter complex of stepped pits covered with green turf and clay flaps, where municipal composters slowly bake manioc stalks and river timber into black biochar under oxygen-starved conditions, venting sweet, pungent smoke across the plaza.
* **The Sunken Grand Causeway Corridor (15-Kilometer Trunk Route)**: A 3-meter-deep, 12-meter-wide earthen trench paved with beaten clay and lateral gravel conduits, piercing straight through the primary forest canopy to connect the settlement clusters of Kilamope and Kunguints.
* **The Wetland Camellones & Aquaculture Weirs**: A geometric maze of alternating 2-meter-high earthen ridges and flooded perimeter ditches in the alluvial floodplains, teeming with cultivated manioc tubers and bamboo-trapped *paiche* and catfish.

### 2. Thematic Premise & Syllogism

* **The Stasis / The Lie:** "The rainforest is an untamed, primeval wilderness that swallows human ambition; mortal men can only survive here as scattered, primitive nomads bending to nature's cruel whim."
* **The Crucible / Conflict:** An extractive mercantile expedition arrives intending to strip timber and clear "virgin land" for pasture, only to discover their saws and fires are wrecking an ancient, highly engineered 300-square-kilometer garden metropolis defended by entrenched clan confederacies.
* **Dramatic Syllogism:**  
  $$\text{Extractive Exploitative Arrogance} + \text{Interconnected Anthropogenic Agro-Ecology} = \text{Devastating Ecological Entanglement or Humbling Integration}$$
* **The Truth / Synthesis:** The Amazon is not an unpopulated wilderness, but a legacy garden created by inter-generational human engineering. Survival in the jungle requires learning to manufacture living soil and respect the forest canopy, not clear-cutting it into barren mud.

* **Corner 1: The Establishment (The Tola Soil-Matriarch of Kilamope):**  
  The elder matriarch and head of the unified clan council presiding over the Kilamope platform cluster. Wearing robes of woven wild cotton dyed with achiote and adorned with carved river-shell gorgets, the Soil-Matriarch views the 6,000-platform earthwork grid as a living, ancestral body. She commands thousands of clan garden-tenders, coordinates the communal biochar burning schedule, regulates water flow through the sunken causeway floodgates, and enforces strict agroforestry edicts that prohibit the cutting of mature peach palms or the pollution of aquaculture ditches. She views external intruders as reckless children blind to the delicate balance required to keep the jungle from turning into toxic mud.
* **Corner 2: The Zealot Opponent (The Extractive Timber Conquistador / Clear-Cut Factor):**  
  An aggressive mercenary entrepreneur and timber factor backed by highland mercantile syndicates. Clad in sweat-stained buff coats and armed with steel crosscut saws, broadaxes, and fire-pots, the Conquistador sees the Upano Valley not as a civilization, but as an obstacle to progress: "empty, overgrown jungle" ripe for timber extraction and conversion to grazing pastures. He damns the sunken causeways as "swamp ditches," clear-cuts ancient peach palm and cacao groves to fuel portable timber mills, and laughs at warnings of soil degradation, entirely confident that steel, oxen, and iron will conquer the wilderness.
* **Corner 3: The Thematic Shadow (The Biochar Siphon-Poachers / Black Soil Cartel):**  
  A syndicate of opportunistic younger clansmen and rogue traders who have realized that foreign highland estates will pay astronomical sums in gold and silver for *Terra Preta de Índio* to use as miracle fertilizer on exhausted mountain terraces. Operating at night beneath the canopy, the poachers use stolen iron shovels to hollow out ancient cemetery mounds and ancestral biochar pits, bagging the dark anthrosols for illicit export. They rationalize their actions as economic liberation from the Matriarch's conservative communal rationing, indifferent to the fact that strip-mining the biochar destroys the structural foundation of the platform mounds and permanently leaches the valley's soil buffer.
* **Corner 4: The Tertiary Outcast (The Mount Sangay Ash-Nomad / Volcanic Forager):**  
  A solitary scout and mineral forager who dwells in the high cloud forests and pumice wastes on the active slopes of Mount Sangay. His skin is scarred by sulfurous steam vents and stained gray by volcanic ash. He understands the profound geochemical symbiosis between volcanic tephra showers and the fertility of the valley lowlands. The Ash-Nomad monitors the volcano's seismic tremors and fumarole vents, warning both the Matriarch and the Conquistador that the root systems of the garden canopy are the only physical barrier preventing monsoon rains from mobilizing millions of tons of volcanic tephra into catastrophic lahar mudflows that will bury the entire civilization.

### 4. Injected Tabletop Mechanics & Runtime Primitives

#### 4.1 4-Level Dynamic Dial: The Living Agro-Urban Canopy Dial
The environmental stability, hydrostatic balance, and canopy integrity of the Upano garden metropolis are monitored across a 4-level dial strictly calibrated to Universal Hazard Tiers:

* **Level 1 (Sunken Causeway Silt & Swarm Pester / Light Severity Tier):**  
  * *Condition:* Seasonal tropical drizzle; sunken causeways fill with knee-deep water; humidity surges to 95%; clouds of biting blackflies, midges, and stinging sweat-bees swarm the flowering fruit canopies.
  * *Mechanics:* Movement along sunken causeways on foot is reduced by 5 feet per round without a watercraft or stilts. Ambient insect clouds impose disadvantage on passive Perception checks. Characters attempting to maintain spell concentration or complete intricate manual tasks must succeed on a **DC 10 Constitution or Survival check** each hour or gain minor fatigue and suffer disadvantage on initiative.
* **Level 2 (Biochar Pit Smolder & Flash Causeway Inundation / Moderate Severity Tier):**  
  * *Condition:* Dense, pungent biochar smoke from dozens of underground pyrolysis kilns blankets the sunken plazas; a sudden equatorial squall drops three inches of rain in an hour, converting sunken causeways into roaring drainage flumes 4 feet deep.
  * *Mechanics:* Visibility in low-lying plazas and sunken corridors is restricted to 20 feet due to swirling smoke and torrential rain. Traversing sunken causeways against the current requires a **DC 13 Athletics or Vehicles (Water) check**; failure sweeps travelers backward 30 feet, inflicting **2d8 bludgeoning/strain damage** against lateral ditch revetments. Sinking into unpaved, water-saturated biochar composting trenches requires a **DC 13 Dexterity or Athletics check** to extract gear and footing without becoming immobilized.
* **Level 3 (Platform Mound Erosion & Trench Mudslide / Severe Severity Tier):**  
  * *Condition:* Extensive deforestation or neglected drainage causes soil liquefaction; the earthen flanks of ancient platform mounds begin to slump; flash floods breach raised-field aquaculture dikes, contaminating drinking cisterns; hostile clan war-bands mobilize blowgunners in the canopy blinds.
  * *Mechanics:* Ascending or descending the flanks of earthen mounds during rains requires a **DC 16 Balance or Athletics check**; failure triggers a localized 30-foot slope failure dealing **4d10 bludgeoning damage** from heavy earthen mud and pinning the victim under collapsed clay (requiring a DC 16 Athletics check to break free). Clan skirmishers firing poisoned darts from concealed tree platforms impose **DC 16 Fortitude saves** against curare-laced alkaloids; failure inflicts 1 level of Exhaustion and temporary neuromuscular paralysis for $1\text{d}4$ hours.
* **Level 4 (Sangay Ash Deluge & Canopy Meltdown / Lethal Climax Tier):**  
  * *Condition:* Mount Sangay enters a paroxysmal explosive phase, raining millions of tons of heavy volcanic ash and pumice across the valley; acidic rainfall dissolves thatched roofs; catastrophic volcanic mudflows (*lahars*) roar down the sunken causeway arteries at 30 miles per hour, engulfing platform settlements.
  * *Mechanics:* Absolute environmental cataclysm. Ambient air is toxic and corrosive: breathing unprotected air inflicts **2d6 acid damage** per turn. Navigating through lahar-threatened plazas or rescuing trapped villagers requires a **DC 20 Athletics (Swimming) or Survival check**; failure catches the character in a surging torrent of boiling volcanic mud, inflicting **8d12 lethal bludgeoning and suffocation damage**. Platform mounds collapse permanently into the mud; the entire 300-square-kilometer garden network dissolves as the jungle and volcanic tephra reclaim the site into an uninhabitable swamp waste.

* **Mount Sangay Tephra Accumulation Clock [6 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks forward (+1) whenever volcanic tremors rattle the Andean foothills, fumaroles erupt with heavy sulfur plumes, or characters detonate heavy explosives or destabilize spiritual ward-mounds near the mountain flank.
  * *Escalation Events:*  
    * *Tick 2:* A fine dusting of gray andesitic ash covers the peach palm canopy; water in the sunken causeways turns chalky and bitter; minor livestock respiratory distress.
    * *Tick 4:* Tephra fallout thickens to four inches; the weight of wet volcanic ash snaps limbs on ancient fruit trees; thatched roofs of domestic tolas begin collapsing under dead-weight.
  * *Consequence when full (6/6):* Paroxysmal eruption of Mount Sangay. Massive pyroclastic column collapses, triggering widespread acidic ash rain and boiling lahar mudflows down all valley corridors, immediately unleashing **Dial Level 4: Sangay Ash Deluge**.
* **Agroforestry Siltation & Biochar Leaching Clock [4 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks forward (+1) whenever external invaders clear-cut an agroforestry sector, biochar smolder kilns are extinguished or neglected during wartime, or biochar-poaching syndicates strip ancestral *Terra Preta* deposits.
  * *Escalation Events:*  
    * *Tick 1:* Lateral drainage ditches along the sunken causeways fill with unmanaged silt, causing road flooding during even light showers.
    * *Tick 2:* Soil in stripped clearings turns pale gray and acidic (*oxisol* reversion); manioc crops in adjacent raised fields suffer root-rot; agricultural yield drops by 30%.
    * *Tick 3:* Massive sheet-wash erosion strips topsoil from exposed earthen mounds, causing structural fissures in the foundations of the central longhouses.
  * *Consequence when full (4/4):* Total ecological exhaustion. *Terra Preta* biological activity ceases permanently; nutrients leach into the bedrock; fields convert into sterile, brick-hard laterite crusts; settlement population is forced to abandon the garden city.

### 5. Logistical & Resource Flow Contract

* **Input Prerequisites (`requires`):**
  * `Ecology: Managed-Canopy-Agroforestry`: A multi-tiered food forest composed of peach palms, cacao, inga, and timber species maintained through selective pruning, seed selection, and canopy shading to buffer soils against equatorial rain.
  * `Labor: Community-Biochar-Composters`: An organized civic labor caste dedicated to the continuous collection of organic waste, bone-crushing, and controlled low-temperature charcoal pyrolysis in anaerobic smolder-pits.
* **Output Yields (`provides`):**
  * `Agriculture: Anthropogenic-Terra-Preta`: Millions of metric tons of deep, pitch-black, self-regenerating anthrosol capable of supporting continuous, multi-crop agriculture without soil exhaustion or fallow periods.
  * `Topography: Sunken-Canal-Causeway-Grid`: Hundreds of kilometers of engineered sunken transit highways and canoe canals that facilitate regional trade, tactical interior communication, and rapid troop redeployment.
  * `Engineering: Earthen-Platform-Tolas`: Thousands of geometric earthen platform foundations elevated above floodwaters and soil moisture, providing stable foundations for longhouses, fortifications, and ceremonial plazas.
* **Additional Yield Resources:**
  * `Anthropogenic Terra Preta Anthrosols`: Sacks of dark, crumbly, charcoal-infused biochar soil that accelerates agricultural growth by 300% when applied to depleted farmland in foreign nodes.
  * `High-Yield Peach Palm Harvests`: Massive seasonal yields of oily, starchy, highly nutritious *chonta* fruit clusters, convertible into staple flour, fermented chicha beer, and durable iron-hard wood for weapons.
  * `Sunken Canal-Causeway Wayfinding Grids`: Detailed navigational knowledge and waterway charts enabling swift, unintercepted boat transit through the jungle canopy, bypassing trackless primary rainforest.
* **Standalone Fallback Mode:**
  * When communal biochar composting collapses or the agroforestry canopy is destroyed, the complex enters **Jungle Inundation Mode**:
    * Within three rainy seasons, torrential equatorial rains leach all nitrogen and phosphorus from unprotected fields, dropping crop yields by **90%** and triggering regional famine.
    * Unmaintained sunken causeways silt solid and transform into stagnant, mosquito-infested mud sloughs, imposing **DC 13 Fortitude checks** against tropical swamp fevers on all travelers.
    * Fast-growing primary rainforest vines and pioneer trees (*Cecropia*) overgrow the earthen tolas, dissolving their geometric crests into forested hills and hiding the entire city beneath dense green canopy within two generations.

### 6. Scene Calculus & Tactical Spatial Encounters

* **Encounter 1: The Sunken Causeway Ambush (Arrival Collision)**
  * *Collision ($><$):* Searching for the legendary Upano trade network, the party travels down what appears to be a deeply eroded dry streambed—a 12-foot-wide, 8-foot-deep trench cutting straight through towering canopy trees. As they round a curve, they collide with a massive barricade composed of spiked peach-palm trunks and woven bamboo hurdles defended by thirty clan blowgunners wearing red achiote war-paint. Simultaneously, heavy timber sluice gates are hoisted open 100 yards upstream by concealed scouts, unleashing a waist-deep, roaring torrent of trapped canal water directly down the trench into the party’s position.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* The party expects a straightforward overland route to a trade hub, but suddenly finds themselves trapped inside an engineered subterranean killbox that is actively filling with violent, muddy flash-flood waters while poisoned darts rain from above.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The Clan War-Leader stands atop the trench embankment leaning on a peach-palm spear, politely welcoming the party in the formal ceremonial dialect of the garden cities and praising their bravery; however, his warriors systematically aim blowguns at the party’s pack animals, deliberately seeking to drown or poison the mounts to seize their steel tools and trade goods.
  * *Tactical Mechanics & Resolution:*
    * Standing ground in the flooded causeway requires a **DC 13 Athletics check** each round to avoid being knocked prone and swept 20 feet downstream. Climbing the vertical, slick clay trench walls requires a **DC 13 Athletics or Dexterity (Acrobatics) check**.
    * Warriors in the canopy blinds fire darts coated in tree-frog toxins (**DC 13 Fortitude check**; failure inflicts 1 level of Exhaustion and halving movement speed).
    * Characters can resolve the encounter through violence, or by succeeding on a **DC 13 Haggling or Persuasion check** accompanied by a substantial offering of iron chisels, steel machetes, or volcanic salt blocks, prompting the War-Leader to drop rope ladders and divert the floodwater into side agricultural flumes.

* **Encounter 2: The Biochar Smolder-Pit Sabotage (Resource Dilemma)**
  * *Collision ($><$):* Reaching the outer platform cluster of Kunguints at dusk, the party investigates plumes of thick, acrid smoke rising from the central plaza. They discover that a band of rogue Biochar Siphon-Poachers has cracked open the primary communal pyrolysis kilns beneath the High Council Tola, attempting to strip-mine high-grade *Terra Preta* to pack into wicker hampers for highland export. In their reckless digging, the poachers breached an underground draft flue, igniting the dry heart-timbers of the ancient ceremonial longhouse atop the mound.
  * *The Dilemma:*
    * *Salvage the Sacred Cartographic Slates:* The party rushes into the burning longhouse to rescue the clan's irreplaceable ancestral territory slates—carved ceramic tablets mapping the hidden LiDAR causeways, secret granaries, and gold-bearing river gravels across 300 square kilometers. However, doing so takes precious time, allowing the underground biochar fire to spread through the dry root network, permanently combusting the living *Terra Preta* bed and causing the entire 20-meter earthen platform to collapse into a glowing, smoldering sinkhole.
    * *Extinguish the Subterranean Kiln Fire:* The party mobilizes water buckets and earth to seal the biochar draft vents and extinguish the smoldering foundation. This preserves the ancestral soil factory and saves the structural integrity of the platform mound, but the fire above completely consumes the longhouse, incinerating the priceless historical maps and causing the loss of ancient knowledge forever.
  * *Tactical Mechanics & Resolution:* 
    * Entering the smoke-choked longhouse requires a **DC 13 Fortitude save** against smoke inhalation (2d6 fire/poison damage on failure) and a **DC 13 Athletics check** to navigate falling timber rafters.
    * Sealing the smolder kilns requires three successful **DC 13 Athletics or Engineering checks** within 3 rounds to shovel clay and breach irrigation ditches into the vents. Failure allows the fire to reach the platform foundation, inflicting structural failure on the tola.

* **Encounter 3: The Mudslide on the Sangay Platform (Severance Crisis)**
  * *Collision ($><$):* While the party is negotiating with the Soil-Matriarch atop the central tola of Kilamope, Mount Sangay rumbles with a deafening subterranean boom. A violent tremor shakes the valley, followed by torrential rain laden with gray volcanic ash. The saturated, deforested southeastern flank of the platform begins to liquefy: an 80-ton mudslide begins slumping downward directly toward the canal docks below.
  * *The Dilemma:*
    * *Brace the Seed-Vault Revetments:* The party deploys their physical might, spells, or structural engineering to reinforce the timber revetments holding back the mud, preventing the destruction of the subterranean seed vault containing the genetic heritage of the valley—thousands of rare, blight-resistant manioc tubers, ancestral cacao varieties, and domesticated peach palm seeds. However, holding this revetment requires all available hands, leaving an evacuation fleet of thirty wooden dugouts tied at the base of the canal unable to escape, resulting in forty wounded villagers and children being crushed beneath collapsing clay terraces.
    * *Sever the Canoe Lines & Evacuate the Villagers:* The party rushes down to the canal docks, cutting the tangled mooring ropes and fending off tumbling boulders to push the canoes out into the center of the deep causeway canal, saving all forty villagers from death. However, without shoring, the platform revetments catastrophically buckle: the seed vault is obliterated and buried beneath 15 feet of liquid mud, condemning the surviving population to catastrophic genetic seed loss and future agricultural collapse.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The Soil-Matriarch herself stands between the two crises, screaming with fierce authority for the party to let the canoes go and save the seeds: *"Human flesh can be born again from any belly in the forest! But if the ancestral seeds die beneath the mud, the people of the tolas are extinct forever!"*
  * *Tactical Mechanics & Resolution:*
    * Shoring the revetments requires two consecutive **DC 16 Athletics or Crafting/Engineering checks** against sliding earthen mass, taking **4d10 bludgeoning damage** on any failure as timbers buckle.
    * Rescuing the canoes requires a **DC 16 Reflex or Athletics check** to dive through collapsing debris and untangle ropes before the mud wave hits the water.
