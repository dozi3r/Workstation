# MODULE CWT-070: THE RAPA NUI MOAI QUARRY AND RODENT ECOCIDE

### 1. Mechanical Architecture & Archaeological Grounding
Rooted in the isolated volcanic ecosystem of Rapa Nui (Easter Island, 1200–1650 CE), located over 3,500 kilometers from the nearest continental landmass, this module models the fragile intersection of competitive monumental architecture, hyper-insular ecological overshoot, and the catastrophic trophic collapse wrought by introduced invasive species.

Challenging outdated moral fables of purely self-inflicted anthropogenic ecocide (e.g., the narrative that islanders mindlessly cut down every tree solely to roll stone statues), modern archaeological, paleoethnobotanical, and zooarchaeological field research—synthesized by Terry Hunt and Carl Lipo—reveals a far more complex and insidious socio-ecological disaster. The collapse of the island's colossal forest biome was driven primarily by an unconstrained biological multiplier: the Polynesian rat (*Rattus exulans*), whose catastrophic predation on palm nuts terminated forest regeneration decades before the final adult trees were felled.

The physical, mechanical, and logistical systems of this insular civilization rest upon five empirical realities:

1. **The Rano Raraku Volcanic Tuff Foundry**: Nearly all of the island’s 887 recorded colossal statues (*Moai*) were extracted from the volcanic crater of Rano Raraku, an extinct basaltic cinder cone composed of yellowish-brown consolidated lapilli tuff (sideromelane-rich volcanic ash). Using handheld, flaked basalt chisels (*toki*), specialized guild carvers chiseled monolithic figures directly from horizontal and vertical bedrock faces. Statues averaged 4 meters in height and 14 metric tons in mass, with the largest unfinished monolith (*Te Tokanga*) measuring 21.6 meters and weighing roughly 160 metric tons. Finished statues were carved in negative relief, detached from their keel spires, slid down steep volcanic scree on guide ropes, and temporarily erected into deep excavation pits on the crater slopes for intricate facial carving and dorsal detailing.
2. **Upright "Walking" Biomechanics & Cable Strain**: To transport these 10-to-80-ton monoliths miles across undulating volcanic terrain to coastal platforms (*ahu*), the islanders utilized dynamic upright transit mechanics rather than passive timber log rollers. The statues were engineered with low, forward-leaning centers of gravity, protuberant bellies, and curved, D-shaped base perimeters. By anchoring three braided vegetative hawsers to the statue's brow and head, pulling teams of 40 to 80 laborers rhythmically tilted the colossus from side to side, "walking" (*neke*) it forward in a controlled lateral waddle. This method exerted staggering tensile strain upon plant-fiber ropes woven from *hau hau* bark (*Triumfetta semitriloba*) and giant palm fibers, requiring continuous cordage manufacturing and immense daily caloric inputs.
3. **The Rodent Seed-Predation Engine (*Rattus exulans*)**: The existential driver of deforestation was not human axes alone, but the introduced Polynesian rat. Introduced by early voyagers as a food animal and inadvertent stowaway, the species encountered an insular paradise devoid of natural mammalian predators, raptors, or snakes. The rodent population exploded exponentially into millions. Microscopic botanical analysis of preserved fossil endocarps from the extinct giant Easter Island palm (*Paschalococos disperta*, a colossal relative of the Chilean wine palm) demonstrates that **over 99% of all excavated palm nuts bear deep incisor gnaw marks**. The rats devoured virtually every seed before it could take root, paralyzing the reproductive cycle of the forest. When older palms died naturally or were cleared for swidden sweet-potato gardens and hauling cables, zero saplings replaced them.
4. **The Loss of the Pelagic Sea & Trophic Severance**: The extinction of the giant palm triggered an immediate, structural maritime collapse. *Paschalococos disperta* trunks, reaching diameters of 2 meters, were the only local timber capable of producing large, ocean-going outrigger voyaging canoes (*vaka*). Zooarchaeological excavations of early middens reveal that deep-sea porpoises and open-ocean tuna accounted for over **30% of the islanders' total caloric intake**. As giant timber vanished, canoe construction ceased entirely. Deep-sea fishing collapsed to zero; porpoise bones vanished from the archaeological strata. Trapped on a windswept rock without escape vessels, the society was violently thrust back onto depleted terrestrial soils, relying exclusively on sweet potatoes, domestic fowl, and rat meat.
5. **Lithic Mulching Agriculture & The *Huri Moai* Fracture**: In response to wind erosion and nutrient depletion, the islanders engineered an advanced agronomic survival technology: **lithic mulching**. By quarrying, breaking, and distributing millions of vesicular basalt stones across thousands of acres of garden plots, farmers stabilized soil moisture, sheltered sprouts from salt winds, moderated diurnal temperature swings, and slowly released leached minerals (phosphorus, potassium, and calcium) into the root zones. Yet as malnutrition intensified and ecological buffers dissolved, competitive ancestral display mutated into savage internecine war. Clans abandoned Rano Raraku, armed themselves with flaked obsidian spearheads (*mata'a*), and initiated the **Huri Moai** ("Toppling of the Statues")—systematically pulling rival ancestral colossi face-down off their coastal *ahu* to break their necks on the paving stones, replacing ancestor worship with the militaristic **Tangata Manu** (Birdman) cult centered on seasonal cliff-diving egg hunts at the Orongo caldera rim.

* **The Rano Raraku Caldera (+150m to +250m elevation)**: A sheer volcanic crater containing the island's only fresh reed-lake and soft tuff faces. Unfinished statues lie frozen in the rock face in every stage of production—from rough-hewn outlines to nearly detached monoliths.
* **The Transit Roadways (5km to 15km corridors)**: Concave, compacted earthen paths cleared of loose stones. Hauling crews advance at a grueling pace of a few hundred meters per day, dependent on flawless rope tension and rhythmic chanting.
* **The Coastal Ahu Platforms (0m to +10m shoreline)**: Megalithic retaining walls of fitted basalt slabs holding up to fifteen towering Moai gazing inward over clan villages, their eyes inset with white coral and red scoria topknots (*pukao*) quarried from the separate Puna Pau volcanic crater.
* **The Lithic Mulch Grids**: Vast geometric fields covered in 10-to-30-centimeter basalt cobblestones, forming a bleak, jagged landscape where every inch of arable dirt is shielded beneath volcanic rock.

### 2. Thematic Premise & Syllogism

* **The Stasis / The Lie:** "Our ancestors live within the towering stone faces of the Moai; as long as we carve the tuff larger and walk them to the coastal platforms, divine *mana* will protect our palm groves, calm the ocean surges, and feed our children forever."
* **The Crucible / Conflict:** The last stands of giant palm trees fail to produce a single living seedling as millions of invasive rats devour every nut; unable to fell ocean-going canoe trunks or hunt pelagic porpoises, starving clans take up flaked obsidian spears, march upon their neighbors' sacred platforms, and heave their gods into the dirt.
* **Dramatic Syllogism:**  
  $$\text{Competitive Ancestral Monumentalism} + \text{Invasive Rodent Ecosystem Devastation} = \text{Total Deforestation \& Violent Statuary Iconoclasm}$$
* **The Truth / Synthesis:** Spiritual grandeur and monumental stone effigies cannot compensate for the ruin of an ecosystem's foundation. When an insular world consumes its living lifelines and allows its ecological web to unravel, the statues of the ancestors are smashed upon the earth by their own starving worshipers.

* **Corner 1: The Establishment (The Quarry Master Sculptor):**  
  High Priest and chief engineer of the Rano Raraku tuff works. Wearing a headpiece woven from fading bird-feathers and brandishing a ceremonial basalt *toki*, he views the Moai as living conduits of cosmic authority. Convinced that the ongoing famine and tree die-off are divine punishments for insufficient architectural ambition, he orders the commencement of *Te Tokanga*—a colossal 160-ton monolith—demanding that every remaining spool of palm-bark rope and every bushel of sweet potatoes be redirected to the quarry pits.
* **Corner 2: The Zealot Opponent (The Palm-Timber Deforestation Hegemon):**  
  An aggressive coastal war-chief leading a confederation of dispossessed clans. Armed with flaked obsidian daggers and barbed *mata'a* spears, he denounces the Moai as stone parasites that devoured the island's timber and starved the ocean canoes. His raiders cut down the final remnant groves of giant palms to build defensive palisades and heavy levers, using those levers to topple rival *ahu* statues face-down into the dirt while hunting down the priests of Rano Raraku.
* **Corner 3: The Thematic Shadow (The Lithic-Mulch Peasant Gardener):**  
  An opportunistic agricultural patriarch who controls the largest basalt scree fields in the windward lowlands. Having recognized decades ago that the trees were doomed, he accumulated vast reserves of volcanic stone mulch and engineered intricate stone-pile rat-traps. He charges extortionate prices in heirloom nephrite, obsidian blades, and ancestral land rights for a single basket of stone-grown sweet potatoes or roasted rat carcasses, exploiting the island's slow starvation to build a mercantile dynasty upon the rocky wastes.
* **Corner 4: The Tertiary Outcast (The Cliffside Birdman Ascetic):**  
  A scarred, solitary diver who inhabits the windswept stone cells of Orongo, perched precariously between the 300-meter cliffs and the boiling surf below. Rejecting both the dying stone gods of the interior and the agrarian hoarding of the gardeners, he champions the nascent *Tangata Manu* religion: a harsh, meritocratic doctrine of survival where divine mandate belongs solely to the champion who can climb down the precipice, swim the shark-infested ocean channel to the offshore islet of Motu Nui, and return with the sacred first egg of the migrating sooty tern.

### 4. Injected Tabletop Mechanics & Runtime Primitives

#### 4.1 4-Level Dynamic Dial: Ecological Deforestation & Iconoclastic Warfare
The ecological integrity, food security, and social stability of Rapa Nui are tracked along a 4-level dial strictly calibrated to Universal Hazard Tiers:

* **Level 1 (Rat Gnawing & Cordage Fraying / Light Severity Tier - DC 10):**  
  * *Condition:* Thousands of small Polynesian rats (*Rattus exulans*) rustle through the tussock grass; camp supplies are raided under cover of darkness; palm timber is scarce and heavily rationed; hauling cables fray along their braided strands.
  * *Mechanics:* Camp supplies and food rations must be suspended from stone tripods or sealed in basalt bins; failing a **DC 10 Survival check** destroys 25% of the party's perishable provisions overnight. Inspecting hauling cordage, bridge lines, or climbing gear requires a **DC 10 Crafting or Perception check** to spot internal rodent teeth damage before lines snap under tension.
* **Level 2 (Moai Walking Instability & Rat Swarm Raids / Moderate Severity Tier - DC 13, 2d8 damage):**  
  * *Condition:* Moving a 14-ton Moai along unpaved volcanic roadways; timber levers splinter; bold rat swarms raid garden patches and seed silos in broad daylight; clan tension flares along platform boundaries.
  * *Mechanics:* Participating in or navigating around an upright "walking" Moai requires coordinated **DC 13 Athletics or Engineering checks**; failure results in the colossus listing violently, snapping a lateral guy-wire and whipping nearby characters for **2d8 bludgeoning damage** (or inflicting 1 level of Exhaustion from desperate stabilizing labor). Foraging for edible flora requires a **DC 13 Survival check**, with failure yielding only rodent-fouled tubers.
* **Level 3 (Quarry Rockslide & Ahu Desecration / Severe Severity Tier - DC 16, 4d10 damage):**  
  * *Condition:* Quarry retaining terraces collapse within Rano Raraku under hasty excavation; warbands armed with flaked obsidian *mata'a* raid coastal ceremonial sanctuaries, using timber levers to topple rival monoliths; food riots erupt in the stone gardens.
  * *Mechanics:* Scaling the unstable volcanic tuff scree of Rano Raraku requires a **DC 16 Athletics check**; failure triggers a 30-foot talus slide dealing **4d10 bludgeoning damage** and burying climbing lines. Ambushing raiders discharge obsidian-tipped throwing spears and slings; characters caught without cover must pass a **DC 16 Reflex save** or take **4d10 piercing/slashing damage** from jagged obsidian glass, suffering persistent bleeding (1d6 damage per round until treated).
* **Level 4 (Complete Forest Extinction & Total Statue Carnage / Lethal Climax Tier - DC 20, 8d12 damage):**  
  * *Condition:* The last *Paschalococos* palm is felled; ocean-going canoe building is permanently extinct; deep-sea porpoise hunting hits zero; every coastal Moai has been toppled face-down, shattering its neck on the paving stones; society collapses into desperate cannibalism and scorched-earth civil war.
  * *Mechanics:* Absolute insular ecological collapse. Characters cannot leave the island without external seafaring vessels. Foraging yields zero wild tree nuts or timber; subsisting on stone mulch gardens requires a **DC 20 Survival check** each week or characters suffer starvation. Megalithic statue collapses and coordinated obsidian mob attacks inflict **8d12 lethal crushing and slashing damage** across all contested *ahu* platforms. Ancestor worship dissolves permanently into the brutal, martial *Tangata Manu* cult.

* **Rapa Nui Palm Seed Extinction Clock [6 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks forward (+1) each time rat populations swarm an unprotected seed cache, a grove of remaining palms is clear-cut for hauling timber, or farmers burn brush to plant un-mulched sweet potatoes.
  * *Escalation Events:*  
    * *Tick 2:* Palm nut germination rate drops to absolute zero across the central plateau; forest understory dries out into brittle scrub.
    * *Tick 4:* Outrigger canoe construction yards shut down for lack of trunks; deep-sea fishing yields drop by 80%; porpoise meat disappears from tribal banquets.
  * *Consequence when full (6/6):* The giant palm *Paschalococos disperta* is biologically extinct. Ocean-going navigation is severed permanently; the island becomes a closed prison with zero heavy timber.
  * *Player Mitigation:* Constructing predator-proof stone nurseries, trapping rat warrens with poisoned bait, or hand-pollinating quarantined saplings (**DC 16 Survival or Animal Handling check**) clears 1 tick.
* **Huri Moai Statue-Toppling Meter [4 Ticks]:**  
  * *Trigger:* Ticks forward (+1) whenever a clan suffers fatal food riots, an *ahu* perimeter is overrun by obsidian raiders, or a high priest of Rano Raraku executes laborers for failing to walk a statue.
  * *Escalation Events:*  
    * *Tick 1:* The western *ahu* platforms are desecrated; coral eye-insets are gouged out and cast into the sea.
    * *Tick 2:* Three 20-ton statues at Ahu Akivi are levered off their pedestals, snapping in half across their necks; stone carvers abandon the outer crater slopes.
    * *Tick 3:* Red scoria *pukao* topknots are rolled into the surf and used as stepping stones; Rano Raraku is declared a cursed, profane graveyard.
  * *Consequence when full (4/4):* Triggers **Dial Level 4: Complete Forest Extinction & Total Statue Carnage** immediately. Every single standing Moai on the island is toppled face-down into the dirt; ancestral religion is extinguished, and the island enters the despotic reign of the *Tangata Manu* warlords.
  * *Player Mitigation:* Negotiating a truce between sculptors and garden leaders, defending an *ahu* against an obsidian assault, or repairing an intact platform (**DC 16 Diplomacy or Athletics check**) clears 1 tick.

  * `Food: Deep-Sea-Tuna-Porpoise-Catch`: High-protein marine caloric surplus harvested from open-ocean voyaging canoes, necessary to sustain the intense physical demands of quarry carvers and transport gangs.
* **Output Yields (`provides`):**
  * `Structure: Megalithic-Moai-Effigies`: Colossal ancestral stone monuments possessing immense social authority, spiritual focus (*mana*), and territorial demarcation power.
  * `Mineral: Volcanic-Tuff-Ashlar`: Consolidated volcanic lapilli stone quarried from crater faces, easily worked into fire-resistant building blocks, cistern linings, and monumental masonry.
  * `Agriculture: Lithic-Basalt-Mulch`: Advanced agronomic technology utilizing fractured volcanic stones to buffer soil moisture, protect against wind erosion, and leach vital minerals into exhausted earth.
* **Yields Resource (`yields_resource`):**
  * `Megalithic Moai Effigies`: Monolithic statues capable of anchoring regional spiritual legitimacy, psychological intimidation, or civilizational awe.
  * `Volcanic Tuff Ashlar`: High-volume building stone extracted from the caldera walls for defensive earthworks and megalithic platforms.
  * `Lithic Basalt Mulch`: Specialized soil amendment increasing agricultural yield on windswept, barren, or drought-stricken terrain by 40%.
  * `Flaked Obsidian Mata'a`: Lethal, razor-sharp volcanic glass weapon-heads, ideal for piercing organic armor and inflicting severe bleeding wounds.
* **Standalone Fallback Mode:**
  * When palm timber goes extinct and open-ocean voyaging collapses, the module enters **Lithic Mulch Survival Mode**:
    * Megalithic statue carving ceases permanently; unfinished monoliths remain frozen in Rano Raraku's rock face.
    * The population contracts by 70% due to famine and civil strife; surviving clans disperse into fortified lava tubes and windswept basalt rock gardens.
    * All open-ocean voyaging ceases; society shifts to annual meritocratic cliff-diving contests (*Tangata Manu*) at the Orongo caldera rim, where champions risk life and limb for the eggs of migrating sooty terns to claim temporary island leadership.

### 5. Scene Calculus & Tactical Spatial Encounters

* **Encounter 1: The Moai "Walking" Cable Snap (Arrival Collision)**
  * *Collision ($><$):* Arriving at the foot of Rano Raraku seeking mineral resources or safe passage, the party is forcibly conscripted by the Quarry Master Sculptor’s armed overseers. A 15-ton, 5-meter-tall Moai is midway through an upright "walking" transit along a steep hillside roadway. As forty laborers chant and heave upon the lateral hauling lines, a swarm of Polynesian rats nests within the coil of the primary rear stabilizer cable; the gnawed rope suddenly snaps with a sound like a thunderclap. The massive stone colossus tilts violently backward at a 40-degree angle, poised to topple into a crowded staging terrace packed with resting carvers and the party.
  * *Polarity Shift ($+ \to -$):* The party expects to marvel at a majestic civilizational engineering feat, but is instantly plunged into a frantic physical disaster requiring split-second intervention to prevent hundreds of crushed casualties.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The chief overseer screams prayers to ancestral gods while frantically kicking rat nests out of the snapped cordage, terrified that if the colossus shatters, the rival warlord will execute him for destroying clan *mana*.
  * *Tactical Resolution:* Characters must coordinate a **DC 13 Athletics or Engineering check** to brace secondary timber props, splice emergency lines, or lever the statue's base before it falls, preventing **2d8 bludgeoning damage** to the crew.
* **Encounter 2: The Rat Swarm in the Palm Seed Vault (Ecological Scarcity / Dilemma)**
  * *Collision ($><$):* Guided into a subterranean lava tube beneath the southern hills to negotiate for food or timber, the party and a local elder discover the clan’s ancestral seed vault—the last surviving reserve of four hundred viable, un-gnawed *Paschalococos* palm nuts, held in sealed wicker hampers alongside emergency seed tubers. However, the chamber has been breached from beneath by an invasive rodent colony; thousands of ravenous *Rattus exulans* are chewing furiously through the wicker mesh, devouring the island's botanical future in real time.
  * *The Dilemma:*
    * *Alchemical Fire & Smothering Gas:* The party can seal the vault and ignite sulphur pitch or alchemical fire to exterminate the rodent swarm instantly. This guarantees the death of the rats, but dense smoke and raging heat risk incinerating the brittle, dry palm nuts, permanently extinguishing the island's last tree reserve.
    * *Close-Quarters Blade & Sieve Sifting:* The party can wade into the biting, disease-ridden rodent swarm with bludgeons and sieves (**DC 13 Reflex and Constitution checks**). This preserves the seeds, but exposes the characters to rat bites, infectious swamp-rot, and gives the rodents time to consume 50% of the remaining palm nuts before the swarm is contained.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The local elder weeps as he clubs rats with a stone pestle, confessing that his own ancestors brought the rodents aboard their double-hulled canoes as sacred voyage rations, blind to the biological curse they were unleashing upon the land.
* **Encounter 3: The Ahu Standoff at Sunset (Iconoclastic Severance Crisis / Cliffside Trials)**
  * *Collision ($><$):* At the coastal ceremonial platform of Ahu Tongariki, thirty obsidian-wielding *mata'a* raiders led by the Palm-Timber Deforestation Hegemon surround the seven standing Moai. Equipped with massive palm-trunk levers, they are preparing to topple the central 20-ton colossus into the breaking surf to crush the spiritual morale of the coastal clan. Defending the platform, the elderly Master Sculptor and a cadre of temple acolytes have chained their own children to the base of the statue, declaring that if the raiders topple the ancestors, the blood of their own bloodline will coat the stone.
  * *Tactical Intervention:*
    * *Defend the Platform:* Siding with the Sculptor requires driving off the *mata'a* warband (**DC 16 combat encounter** against obsidian-tipped spears dealing **3d8 piercing damage** with persistent bleeding), preserving the monumental heritage and calming the platform's spiritual *mana*, but leaving the starving raiders desperate enough to burn the surrounding lithic mulch gardens.
    * *Permit the Toppling:* Stepping aside allows the raiders to lever the statue off the ahu; the colossus crashes face-down, shattering its neck on the pavement (**DC 16 Reflex save** to avoid flying stone shrapnel dealing **4d10 bludgeoning damage**). The platform's sacred order is broken, but the raiders spare the children and dismantle the hauling levers for firewood to feed their starving camps.
  * *Bifocal Subtext:* The Hegemon bellows that the stone faces have eaten all the island's fish, screaming into the wind that a god who cannot build a canoe or catch a tuna has no right to stand upright upon the shore.
